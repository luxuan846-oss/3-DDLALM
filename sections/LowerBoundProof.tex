\section{Hard Instances and Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:lower-bound}}
\label{sec:lb-proof}

In Section~\ref{sec:lower-bound}, we stated a lower bound on the iteration
complexity of SPLALM Family within the general 
with constant center stepsizes. In this section, we
prove Theorem~\ref{thm:lower-bound}. Specifically, for every \(\epsilon\)
and \(\theta\) satisfying the conditions of Theorem~\ref{thm:lower-bound}
and every pair \((\alpha,\beta)\in[0,1]^2\), we construct a hard instance
\(I\in\mathcal I(\mathbf C_0,\Delta)\) and show that
\(\mathrm{SPLALM}_{\theta,\alpha,\beta}\), started from the zero
initialization of Definition~\ref{def:splalm}, requires at least the stated
number of iterations to produce an \(\epsilon\)-KKT output. Since such an
instance exists for every admissible choice of parameters, no choice of
parameters improves on the worst-case bound. Throughout this section,
\(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) are fixed across all iterations; time-varying
center stepsizes are treated in Section~\ref{sec:time-varying}. In
quantified form,
\[
\forall\,(\alpha,\beta)\in[0,1]^2,\quad
\exists\,I\in\mathcal I(\mathbf C_0,\Delta):\quad
T^{\mathrm{KKT}}_\epsilon(\theta,\alpha,\beta;I)
\ge\frac{\Delta\,\epsilon^{-4}}{32768\,\gamma\max\{1,4\tau,4\gamma\tau\}}.
\]
Different parameters may use different instances; this is the meaning of
taking the infimum over parameters before the supremum over instances in
Definition~\ref{def:complexity-measure}.

The lower bounds of~\cite[Section~5]{li2026smoothing}
and~\cite[Appendix~C.1]{mahdavinia2022tight} choose the initialization
together with the hard function, along an eigenvector of the iteration
matrix. The two papers also cover different sets of parameters.
\cite{li2026smoothing} establish tightness for the parameters of their
upper bounds, with \(r_y=\Theta(\epsilon/D_{\mathcal Y})\). The proof
in~\cite[Appendix~C.1]{mahdavinia2022tight} fixes
\(\eta_y=\Theta(1/\ell)\) and considers every \(\eta_x\).
Theorem~\ref{thm:lower-bound} fixes the gradient stepsizes \(\eta,\tau\),
covers every pair of constant center stepsizes, and keeps the
initialization fixed in advance.

Given a threshold \(\alpha_\epsilon\), specified in
Section~\ref{subsec:lb-combine}, we assign each pair \((\alpha,\beta)\) to
one of three instances according to the partition
\[
[0,1]^2=\{\alpha\le\alpha_\epsilon\}
\ \cup\ \{\alpha>\alpha_\epsilon,\ \beta>0\}
\ \cup\ \{\alpha>\alpha_\epsilon,\ \beta=0\}.
\]
Each instance handles only the pairs assigned to it.
Section~\ref{subsec:lb-plateau} treats \(\alpha\le\alpha_\epsilon\) with a
plateau instance, on which the primal center moves slowly.
Section~\ref{subsec:lb-rotation} treats \(\alpha>\alpha_\epsilon\) and
\(\beta>0\) with a rotating instance, on which the certificate stays above
\(\epsilon\) for all iterations. Section~\ref{subsec:lb-huber} treats
\(\beta=0\) with an instance whose outer function is a Huber function.
Section~\ref{subsec:lb-combine} specifies \(\alpha_\epsilon\), \(c_1\), and
\(c_2\), and combines the three cases.

\paragraph{Small \(\alpha\).}
Take \(\calA\equiv0\), \(h=|\cdot|\), and let \(f\) be a one-dimensional
Huber function with derivative \(-2\epsilon\) on a linear interval
and initial objective gap \(\Delta\). Since \(\calA\equiv0\), the multipliers stay at zero, and the
center \(z^k\) moves by at most \(2\alpha\gamma\epsilon\) per iteration, so
\(x^k\le2\gamma\epsilon(1+\alpha k)\). The iterates therefore stay on the plateau,
where \(R_k=2\epsilon\), for \(\Omega\bigl(\Delta/(\gamma\alpha\epsilon^2)\bigr)\)
iterations. For \(\alpha\le\alpha_\epsilon\), this is
\(\Omega(\Delta\epsilon^{-4})\).

\paragraph{Large \(\alpha\).}
Take \(h=\|\cdot\|_2\), \(\calA(\bx)=b(\bx-\bx_c)\), and let \(f\) be a
quadratic with negative curvature \(-\kappa\) near \(\bx_c\). While the
trajectory stays in \(\mathcal R_\rho\), Proposition~\ref{prop:embedding}
shows that the update is the linear time-invariant system
\(\bw^{k+1}=\mathbb T\bw^k\). We choose \(b\) and \(\kappa\) so that
\(\mathbb T\) has an eigenvalue \(\zeta\) with \(|\zeta|=1\). Along its
eigenvector \(\bv\), the iterates rotate without decay, and the certificate
keeps a fixed positive value. The zero initialization is not parallel to
\(\bv\). We write it as \(\bv+\br\), where \(\br\) lies in the invariant
subspace of the remaining eigenvalues, all strictly inside the unit circle.
The shifts \(\bx_c\) and \(\blmbd_c\) make \(\bv+\br\) correspond to the
origin. A bound on the transient \(\mathbb T^k\br\) keeps the trajectory in
\(\mathcal R_\rho\) and gives \(R_k\ge\frac76\epsilon\) for all \(k\). The
case \(\beta=0\) uses a separate one-dimensional instance with a Huber outer
function \(h\).
