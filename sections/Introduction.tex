\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
In this paper, we consider the following structured optimization problem
\begin{align}
    \min_{\bx\in\RR^n}\quad & F(\bx) = f(\bx) + h(\calA(\bx))
    \tag{\textbf{P}}
    \label{prob:main}
\end{align}where $f\colon \RR^n \to \RR$ is a smooth (Lipschitz differentiable) function,
and $h$ is a proper closed convex function which is prox-friendly. 
The mapping $\calA\colon \RR^n \to \RR^m$ is continuously
differentiable on $\RR^n$ with Lipschitz continuous Jacobian; the complete regularity conditions are deferred to Section~\ref{sec:assumptions}.

Problems of the form \eqref{prob:main} arise naturally in data science,
machine learning, and statistics: $f$ serves as a smooth data-fidelity
term, while $h$ is a nonsmooth regularizer that promotes desired
structural properties in $\calA(\bx)$
\cite{charisopoulos2021low,markovsky2008structured,parekh2015convex}.



For the weakly convex composite optimization,  since the whole model is weakly convex while admits composite structure, 




Single-loop algorithms do not require that the subproblems be solved to the required accuracy or that the number of inner iterations be tuned to improve the solution accuracy, and thus are often simpler to implement in practice. The study of single-loop algorithms has received much attention recently, and many algorithms of this kind have been proposed to solve \eqref{prob:main} under various structural assumptions. A notable common feature is that in most of these studies, $h$ is either Lipschitz or the indicator function of a closed convex set; see \cite{bohm2021variable,kume2024variable,yao2024note,boct2020variable,deng2025single,bot2015variable,tran2018smooth}, which assumed $h$ to be Lipschitz continuous, and \cite{Alacaoglu24a,deng2025single,tran2018smooth,LuMeiXiao24,LuXiao25}, which studied the case where $h$ is the indicator function of a closed convex set.
However, the case where $h$ is a general non-Lipschitz function is not covered by existing single-loop algorithms.

In this work, we are interested in the non-asymptotic convergence analysis of Problem \eqref{prob:main}. A fundamental question arises:

\begin{center}
\emph{Can we design a single-loop ALM with a fixed penalty parameter
and nearly-optimal $\calO(\varepsilon^{-3})$ oracle complexity?}
\end{center}

This question motivates our study. Our contributions can be summarized as follows.
\begin{enumerate}[label={[\roman*].}]
    \item \textbf{Fixed penalty parameter with dual damping.}
    We derive DDLALM from a dual-damping reformulation whose damped
    multiplier admits a closed-form expression, yielding a single-loop
    algorithm with an explicit first-order primal update. The
    augmented-Lagrangian penalty $\rho$ is held fixed throughout. The
    dual-damping term stabilizes the anchor multiplier $\bmu^k$ through
    a convex-combination update, while the growth of the damping
    schedule $s_k$ induces the increasing effective curvature
    $\rho_{s_k}=\rho+s_k$ that drives the splitting residual to zero.
    This decouples multiplier stabilization from penalty growth, so the
    penalty parameter need not be tuned or increased.

    \item \textbf{An approximate $\epsilon$-KKT point as the target.}
    Under the assumption that $h$ is convex and $L_h$-Lipschitz, the
    exact $\by$-proximal step yields a multiplier
    $\bz^{k+1}\in\partial h(\by^{k+1})$ with $\|\bz^{k+1}\|\le L_h$. Our
    target is an $\epsilon$-KKT point of the split formulation
    of~\eqref{prob:main}, namely a triple $(\bx,\by,\bz)$ satisfying
    \[
        \|\nabla f(\bx)+J_{\calA}(\bx)^\top\bz\|\le\epsilon,
        \qquad
        \bz\in\partial h(\by),
        \qquad
        \|\calA(\bx)-\by\|\le\epsilon.
    \]
    The subdifferential residual is exactly zero by construction, so
    only the stationarity and feasibility residuals must be controlled.
    No constraint qualification on $J_{\calA}$ is needed: the split
    constraint $\calA(\bx)-\by=\zero$ has full-row-rank Jacobian
    $[J_{\calA}(\bx),-I]$, and the multiplier bound follows from the
    Lipschitz continuity of $h$.

    \item \textbf{$O(\epsilon^{-3})$ iteration complexity.}
    With the coupled schedule $s_k\asymp k^{1/3}$,
    $\eta_k\asymp k^{-1/3}$, $\tau_k\asymp k^{-2/3}$, the
    reduced-potential descent and a weighted finite-length estimate
    yield that a deterministic tail-best iterate
    $(\bx^\kappa,\by^\kappa,\bz^\kappa)$ satisfies
    \[
        \left\|
        \nabla f(\bx^\kappa)+J_{\calA}(\bx^\kappa)^\top\bz^\kappa
        \right\|
        =O(K^{-1/3}),
        \qquad
        \|\calA(\bx^\kappa)-\by^\kappa\|=O(K^{-1/3}),
        \qquad
        \bz^\kappa\in\partial h(\by^\kappa).
    \]
    % so that $K=O(\epsilon^{-3})$ iterations suffice to obtain an
    % $\epsilon$-KKT point. The exponent $1/3$ balances
    % the stationarity rate $O(K^{-(1-a)/2})$ with the feasibility rate
    % $O(K^{-a})$.
\end{enumerate}
