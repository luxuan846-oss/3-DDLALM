                                                                                              Abstract
                                                      We study the deterministic first-order oracle complexity of smooth nonconvex-concave
                                                  minimax optimization over a bounded convex dual domain under two notions of stationarity:
                                                  optimization stationarity (OS) and game stationarity (GS). OS concerns stationarity of the
                                                  value function, viewed as a structured nonsmooth objective obtained by maximizing over
                                                  the dual variable. GS concerns the joint first-order optimality conditions for both primal
                                                  minimization and dual maximization, each with the other variable held fixed. Existing upper
                                                  bounds on the oracle complexity of first-order methods for finding ϵ-OS and ϵ-GS points
                                                  have different dependence on ϵ, but whether this difference reflects an intrinsic separation in
                                                  computational hardness remains unresolved. We resolve this question by establishing worst-
                                                  case lower bounds for arbitrary deterministic first-order methods: Ω(ϵ−3 ) oracle queries for
                                                  finding an ϵ-OS point and Ω(ϵ−5/2 ) for finding an ϵ-GS point, with the remaining problem
                                                  parameters fixed. These bounds match existing upper bounds up to logarithmic factors. To
                                                  close the remaining logarithmic gap, we introduce Tracked-FOAM, a new first-order algorithm
                                                  whose oracle complexity matches our lower bounds for both OS and GS. Together, these results
                                                  establish an intrinsic separation between OS and GS in the optimal accuracy dependence of
                                                  deterministic first-order oracle complexity.

    Two notions of stationarity are commonly used for Problem (P) [Jin et al., 2020, Lin et al., 2020b,
Li et al., 2025, 2026]. The first, optimization stationarity (OS), views the problem as minimizing the
value function Φ(x) := maxy ∈Y f (x; y ) + ι X (x), where ι X is the indicator function of X . Although
f is smooth, Φ can be nonsmooth, and OS measures stationarity of this structured nonsmooth
objective through its Moreau envelope [Moreau, 1965, Davis and Drusvyatskiy, 2019]. The second,
game stationarity (GS), concerns the joint first-order optimality conditions for primal minimization
and dual maximization, each with the other variable held fixed. The precise definitions of ϵ-OS and
ϵ-GS are given in Section 2.
    Existing upper bounds on the oracle complexity of first-order methods have different accuracy
dependence for ϵ-OS and ϵ-GS points. With the remaining problem parameters fixed, P ROX -
DIAG [Thekumparampil et al., 2019], primal-dual smoothing [Zhao, 2024], M INIMAX -PPA [Lin
et al., 2020b], and FNE S EARCH [Ostrovskii et al., 2021] attain O(ϵ−3 log2 (1/ϵ)) oracle complexity
for finding an ϵ-OS point. For finding an ϵ-GS point, M INIMAX -PPA [Lin et al., 2020b] and
FNE S EARCH [Ostrovskii et al., 2021] attain O(ϵ−5/2 log2 (1/ϵ)) oracle complexity. More recently,
P ERTURBED S MOOTHED FOAM [Li et al., 2026] improves both bounds by reducing the logarithmic
factor from log2 (1/ϵ) to log(1/ϵ). Table 1 summarizes these bounds and their dependence on the
problem parameters.

FOAM method of Kovalev and Gasnikov [2022]. In the method of Li et al. [2026], each subproblem
is solved to a prescribed ϵ-dependent accuracy, incurring a logarithmic cost at every outer iteration.
In contrast, T RACKED -FOAM retains the full accelerated FOAM state across successive subprob-
lems. We establish a global Lyapunov descent estimate that jointly accounts for progress of the
outer method and the inexact errors in solving the SC-SC subproblems. This estimate shows that
each inner solve need only reduce the FOAM Lyapunov function by a fixed fraction. This removes
the need to prescribe an inner accuracy that depends on ϵ. Consequently, the oracle complexity of
T RACKED -FOAM matches our lower bounds for both OS and GS across all parameter regimes.
    Together, our results establish an intrinsic separation in optimal accuracy dependence between
finding stationary points of the structured nonsmooth value function and satisfying the first-
order equilibrium conditions of the original minimax problem. Our matching lower and upper
bounds give a complete characterization of deterministic first-order oracle complexity under both
stationarity notions across all parameter regimes.

formulation that allows convex nonsmooth regularizers in place of constraint indicators. Assuming

2   Preliminaries
To study Problem (P), we introduce the following class of NC-C functions.
Definition 2.1 (NC-C function class). Given ℓ, DY , ∆ > 0, let F (ℓ, DY , ∆) be the union, over
all m, n ∈ N0 and all closed convex sets X ⊆ Rm and Y ⊆ Rn satisfying (0m , 0n ) ∈ X ×

                                                  4
Y and diam(Y ) ≤ DY , of the class of functions f : X × Y → R satisfying the following properties.
For each such function, define Φ(x) := maxy ∈Y f (x; y ) + ι X (x).

  (i) (ℓ-smoothness) f is jointly ℓ-smooth on X × Y , i.e., for all (x1 , y1 ), (x2 , y2 ) ∈ X × Y ,

                           ∥∇ f (x1 ; y1 ) − ∇ f (x2 ; y2 )∥ ≤ ℓ∥(x1 , y1 ) − (x2 , y2 )∥.

 (ii) (Dual concavity) For every fixed x ∈ X , f (x; ·) is concave on Y .

 (iii) (Initial gap) The initialization satisfies Φ(0) − infx∈X Φ(x) ≤ ∆.

    We consider two standard notions of stationarity for Problem (P): optimization stationarity
(OS) and game stationarity (GS); see, e.g., [Lin et al., 2020b, Definition 18] and [Li et al., 2025,
Definition 2]. See also Lin et al. [2020a, Section 4.3] for a comparison of related stationarity notions.
To formulate the OS notion, note that f (·; y ) + ι X (·) is ℓ-weakly convex for every y ∈ Y , and hence
so is Φ. Consequently, its Moreau envelope with parameter 1/(2ℓ) is continuously differentiable.
We give the precise definitions below.

    For smooth minimization, a first-order oracle returns the function value and gradient at each
query. A deterministic algorithm chooses each new query from the previous oracle replies, and
oracle complexity measures the number of queries needed to reach a prescribed stationarity
tolerance in the worst case.

minimax oracle formulations in Li et al. [2021], Pan and Li [2026]. In our setting, the oracle returns
the function value and both partial gradients, and complexity is measured under OS or GS. We
formalize these definitions below.

6     A Matching Upper-Bound Algorithm
To establish the matching upper bounds, we develop an inexact proximal-point method. By
introducing dual regularization and a primal proximal term, we obtain strongly convex-strongly
concave subproblems, which can be solved approximately using the accelerated FOAM method of
Kovalev and Gasnikov [2022]. Since the proximal center changes across outer iterations, we need to
control the approximation error across successive subproblems. Our approach is to retain the full
FOAM state and track its error together with the progress of the outer method. This allows each
inner solve to use a fixed contraction factor, without requiring a prescribed ϵ-dependent accuracy.
The OS and GS variants share the same tracking recursion but differ in their dual regularization
parameters and output rules. Throughout, f ∈ F (ℓ, DY , ∆).

10      Conclusion
We established matching deterministic first-order lower and upper bounds for smooth NC-C
minimax optimization under both optimization stationarity and game stationarity. For OS, the
optimal dependence on the target accuracy is ϵ−3 , while for GS it is ϵ−5/2 , with the remaining


                                                  48
problem parameters fixed and ϵ ↓ 0. For GS, we further establish matching bounds for every
ℓ, DY , ∆, ϵ > 0, including the additional cost that persists at small initial gaps in the value function.
Thus, the different dependence on ϵ under the two stationarity criteria reflects an intrinsic separation
in their worst-case deterministic first-order oracle complexity.
