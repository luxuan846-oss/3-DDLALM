                                                                                                    Abstract
                                                        This paper studies smooth nonconvex-concave minimax optimization and two acceleration mechanisms
                                                   for single-loop first-order methods: dual perturbation and smoothing. Although both techniques improve
                                                   convergence guarantees, their relative advantages remain unclear due to the distinction between game
                                                   stationarity (GS) and optimization stationarity (OS). We provide a tight characterization of their iteration
                                                   complexities under both notions. We show that smoothing accelerates convergence to both GS and OS,
                                                   whereas dual perturbation improves the rate only for GS and does not accelerate OS. Matching lower
                                                   bounds based on hard instances establish the tightness of these rates. Motivated by this separation, we
                                                   propose Perturbed Smoothed GDA, a single-loop method combining both techniques. It improves the
                                                   complexity for GS over existing single-loop methods while preserving the state-of-the-art rate for OS, and
                                                   further admits asymptotic convergence to 0-GS, which is not available for vanilla Smoothed GDA.

                                           In this paper, we consider the smooth nonconvex–concave (NC-C) minimax problem

a slow convergence rate: TS-GDA requires O(ϵ−6 ) iterations to reach both ϵ-game stationary (ϵ-GS) and
ϵ-optimization stationary (ϵ-OS) points (cf. (Lin et al., 2020b, Definition A.5), (Li et al., 2025, Definition 3.1)).
The slow convergence of TS-GDA stems from the fact that it essentially behaves as an inexact subgradient
method applied to the nonsmooth value function Φ(x) := maxy∈Y f (x, y) + ιX (x), where ιX (·) is an
indicator function that takes value 0 if x ∈ X and +∞ otherwise.
     To improve the convergence rate, two acceleration techniques have recently been proposed to overcome
this nonsmooth barrier. The first is Smoothing via the Moreau envelope: The Moreau envelope of Φ provides
a smooth surrogate with Lipschitz-continuous gradients (Davis and Drusvyatskiy, 2019). Building on this
idea, Zhang et al. (2020) propose the Smoothed GDA method, which replaces the original objective with a
regularized surrogate f (x, y) + 2r ∥x − z∥2 (r > ℓ), where z serves as an approximate proximal point of x
with respect to the value function Φ. Rather than solving the proximal subproblem exactly, Zhang et al. (2020)
perform a single GDA update on (x, y, z) in each iteration, leading to an improved iteration complexity of
O(ϵ−4 ) for finding an ϵ-GS point (Zhang et al., 2020). A recent work (Li et al., 2025) further shows that
Smoothed GDA also attains an ϵ-OS point in O(ϵ−4 ) iterations.
     The second acceleration technique is dual perturbation. This approach adds a small quadratic regularizer
to the dual objective, thereby making it strongly concave. Such a perturbation is closely related to the
regularization technique of (Nesterov, 2013). By the envelope theorem, this modification also yields a smooth
value function maxy∈Y f (·, y) − 2ϵ ∥y∥2 . Moreover, due to the compactness of Y, any ϵ-GS of the perturbed
problem is also a Θ(ϵ)-GS point of the original problem. Applying GDA to this perturbed formulation
yields the Perturbed GDA algorithm, which attains an iteration complexity of O(ϵ−4 ) for finding an ϵ-GS
solution (Xu et al., 2023; Lu et al., 2020; Xu et al., 2026).
     However, in the existing literature, direct comparisons of iteration complexities between dual perturbation
and smoothing can be misleading, as the literature often fails to clearly distinguish between two distinct
notions of stationarity: game stationarity and optimization stationarity. This oversight has fostered the
inaccurate impression that both smoothing and dual perturbation yield the same O(ϵ−4 ) rate for smooth
problems under single-loop first-order methods. In reality, Li et al. (2025) clarifies that Smoothed GDA
achieves both ϵ-GS and ϵ-OS within O(ϵ−4 ) iterations, and the dual-perturbation schemes developed in (Xu
et al., 2023; Lu et al., 2020) establish an O(ϵ−4 ) rate only for ϵ-GS. To the best of our knowledge, the iteration
complexity of Perturbed GDA for attaining an ϵ-OS point has not been characterized in the existing literature.
Our contributions. This distinction between GS and OS is more than a notational nuance; GS and OS
impose intrinsically different complexity requirements, as already evidenced by their different complexity in
the multi-loop setting. Lin et al. (2020b) show that multi-loop first-order methods attain an ϵ-GS solution with
iteration complexity O(ϵ−2.5 log2 ( 1ϵ )), whereas attaining an ϵ-OS solution requires O(ϵ−3 log2 ( 1ϵ )) iterations.
This disparity in complexity shows that OS and GS are distinct for smooth NC-C minimax problems, which
raises the following question for single-loop first-order methods:

(Q1) For single-loop first-order algorithms, is there a complexity gap between the iteration complexity
     of finding an ϵ-GS and ϵ-OS?

In this paper, we provide a precise characterization addressing (Q1). We first show that for Perturbed GDA,
although it achieves an iteration complexity of O(ϵ−4 ) for computing an ϵ-GS, its complexity degrades to
O(ϵ−6 ) for attaining an ϵ-OS. We further prove that these bounds are tight by constructing matching hard
instances on which the algorithm indeed requires this many iterations. This establishes a tight separation
between GS and OS for Perturbed GDA. In contrast, Smoothed GDA achieves the same iteration complexity
under the two stationarity notions, attaining both ϵ-GS and ϵ-OS within O(ϵ−4 ) iterations. We also establish

    These results lead to the following conceptual picture of dual perturbation and smoothing: Dual
perturbation achieves an accelerated rate under the GS notion, but its complexity deteriorates under the OS
notion. In contrast, smoothing attains the same accelerated rate for both GS and OS stationarity. This contrast
implies that the two techniques accelerate the game stationarity through distinct mechanisms, and raises the
possibility of a synergy effect of the two techniques.
    This motivates the second question we tackle in this paper.

(Q2) Can combining dual perturbation and smoothing improve the iteration complexity beyond either
     technique alone?

     To answer (Q2), we introduce Perturbed Smoothed GDA, a single-loop algorithm that uses both smoothing
and dual perturbation techniques. Our analysis shows that this algorithm achieves an ϵ-OS in O(ϵ−4 ) iterations,
and this rate is tight under our assumptions. This result confirms the earlier insight that dual perturbation
does not improve the iteration complexity to ϵ-OS as the iteration complexity remains identical to that of
Smoothed GDA. On the other hand, our Perturbed Smoothed GDA achieves an ϵ-GS in O(ϵ−3 ) iterations, a
strict improvement over both Smoothed GDA and Perturbed GDA, which each requires O(ϵ−4 ) iterations.
This result gives a positive answer to (Q2) and shows that smoothing and perturbation can be synergistic in
accelerating convergence to game stationarity.

     This iteration complexity difference can be explained by the structural relation between ϵ-GS and ϵ-OS.
It is known that ϵ-OS implies Θ(ϵ)-GS, but not conversely, see, e.g., (Li et al., 2025, Theorem 7.1); thus,
OS is a strictly stronger criterion for convergence. Correspondingly, smoothing and dual perturbation act
on different components of the problem. Smoothing modifies the value function and directly targets OS,
which automatically secures GS guarantees. In contrast, dual perturbation regularizes the inner maximization
objective and primarily facilitates the convergence to GS, but the lack of a reverse implication (ϵ-GS ̸⇒ϵ-OS)
prevents this benefit from extending to OS. Therefore, the two techniques operate through distinct yet
complementary mechanisms: Smoothing promotes convergence to OS (and by extension GS), whereas dual
perturbation mainly accelerates convergence to GS by rendering the inner maximization (dual) objective
strongly concave. When combined, these effects yield a strictly better complexity for finding an ϵ-GS point
than either technique alone.

    Beyond the improvement in iteration complexity, the perturbation component in Perturbed Smoothed
GDA also enables us to establish an asymptotic convergence result, i.e., we show that Perturbed Smoothed
GDA converges to 0-GS when its iteration number goes to infinity. Such an asymptotic convergence result is
not available for Smoothed GDA under the current analysis.
    Importantly, the smoothing-perturbation synergy is structural and is not restricted to single-loop methods.
Building on the same principle, we propose a double-loop first-order algorithm, Perturbed Smoothed FOAM,
that combines dual perturbation and smoothing within a multi-loop framework. This combination transforms
the original NC-C problem into a sequence of strongly convex-strongly concave (SC-SC) subproblems,
thereby enabling the use of optimal first-order methods within each iteration. In particular, we employ FOAM
(Kovalev and Gasnikov, 2022) as an efficient inner solver, although the improvement does not rely on a specific
choice of solver. Our algorithm achieves iteration complexities O(ϵ−2.5 log( 1ϵ )) for ϵ-GS and O(ϵ−3 log( 1ϵ ))
for ϵ-OS. In comparison, most existing multi-loop methods either attain weaker guarantees or rely on more
involved triple-loop constructions to obtain their rates (Nouiehed et al., 2019; Thekumparampil et al., 2019;
Lin et al., 2020b; Kong and Monteiro, 2021; Zhao, 2024). A recent double-loop method proposed in (Lu and
Mei, 2026) achieves the rate O(ϵ−2.5 log( 1ϵ )) for ϵ-GS, but does not provide a corresponding guarantee for
ϵ-OS. Thus, Perturbed Smoothed FOAM matches the best-known complexity for ϵ-GS while improving the
state-of-the-art complexity for ϵ-OS among first-order methods (Zhang and Hu, 2025).
    Finally, on the technical side, we develop a unified Lyapunov-function-based framework that subsumes
the analysis of existing single-loop methods, including Smoothed GDA and Perturbed GDA, as well as our
proposed Perturbed Smoothed GDA. The analysis for Perturbed Smoothed GDA avoids the cumbersome
two-stage arguments for Smoothed GDA used in prior works such as (Zhang et al., 2020; Li et al., 2025). As a
result, we are able to prove an asymptotic convergence result for Perturbed Smoothed GDA. Furthermore,
the framework extends naturally to the double-loop setting and applies to our proposed Perturbed Smoothed
FOAM. Overall, this yields a streamlined proof structure that not only simplifies the analysis but explicitly
disentangles the distinct roles of smoothing and perturbation in the convergence dynamics.

In this section, we collect the main assumptions and definitions used in the analysis. We begin with a
smoothness assumption on the objective function f and then describe the properties required of the dual
function. These assumptions are imposed throughout the paper.
Assumption 2.1 (ℓ-smooth). The function f is continuously differentiable and there exists a positive constant
ℓ > 0 such that for all x, x′ ∈ X and y, y ′ ∈ Y, we have

                        ∥∇x f (x, y) − ∇x f (x′ , y ′ )∥ ≤ ℓ(∥x − x′ ∥ + ∥y − y ′ ∥), and
                        ∥∇y f (x, y) − ∇y f (x′ , y ′ )∥ ≤ ℓ(∥x − x′ ∥ + ∥y − y ′ ∥).

Assumption 2.2. For each x ∈ X , the dual function f (x, ·) is concave. The dual domain Y ⊆ Rd is convex
and bounded with diameter DY > 0. Without loss of generality (WLOG), we assume that 0 ∈ Y.

2.1     Dual Perturbation
The idea of dual perturbation is to introduce a small quadratic regularizer of the dual variable to the objective
function. This yields the following perturbed, nonconvex-strongly-concave (NC-SC) minimax problem

                                                                              ryt
                          min max ft (x, y), where ft (x, y) = f (x, y) −         ∥y∥2 .                    (2.1)
                          x∈X y∈Y                                              2

Throughout this paper, we let ryt > 0 be non-increasing in t. This perturbation renders the objective strongly
concave in y at each iteration, thereby enabling the use of standard techniques for NC-SC minimax problems.
    Intuitively, if we choose ryt = O(ϵ) for all t ≥ 0, the perturbation term alters the dual gradient by at
most O(ϵ). As a result, any ϵ-GS point of (1.1) remains an O(ϵ)-GS point of (2.1), so that convergence
guarantees established for the perturbed problem (2.1) translate to the original problem (1.1) up to an O(ϵ)
error. Moreover, if the perturbation parameter ryt gradually diminishes to zero, the iterates are expected to
asymptotically recover stationarity points for the original problem (1.1).
    Motivated by these observations, the algorithms in (Xu et al., 2023; Lu et al., 2020) essentially apply
vanilla GDA to (2.1), which we formalize below as the Perturbed GDA algorithm.

    Recently, Li et al. (2025) further show that Smoothed GDA achieves both ϵ-GS and ϵ-OS within O(ϵ−4 )
iterations. The intuition for such an improvement is as follows: Note that the definition of Moreau envelope
dovetails naturally with the definition of ϵ-OS in Definition 2.1. This structure enables Smoothed GDA to
converge to an ϵ-OS faster. In other words, the acceleration due to smoothing for ϵ-OS is “direct". On the
other hand, because any ϵ-OS can be converted into an Θ(ϵ)-GS in O(ϵ−2 ) iterations (cf. (Lin et al., 2020a,
Proposition 4.13)), the acceleration induced by smoothing on ϵ-OS also carries over to ϵ-GS. In other words,
smoothing improves the convergence rate to an ϵ-GS in an “indirect" manner.

3      Main Results
This section presents our main algorithmic and theoretical results. We first propose a new method, Perturbed
Smoothed GDA, which combines dual perturbation and smoothing in a single-loop first-order algorithm. We
then establish iteration complexity bounds for computing ϵ-GS and ϵ-OS solutions across several representative
single-loop methods, and provide matching lower bounds showing that these rates are tight. Our analysis
makes explicit how the effect of smoothing and perturbation interact and explains why their combination
leads to an improved convergence guarantee to ϵ-GS for NC–C minimax optimization. Finally, we establish
asymptotic convergence to 0-GS for both Perturbed GDA and Perturbed Smoothed GDA, a guarantee that is
not available for vanilla Smoothed GDA under existing analyses.

    These results clearly delineate the roles of the two acceleration techniques: Smoothing alone improves
convergence to both optimization stationarity and game stationarity, whereas dual perturbation alone accelerates
only convergence to game stationarity. By combining the two techniques, Perturbed Smoothed GDA attains

among single-loop first-order methods. In other words, smoothing suffices to achieve the state-of-the-art rate
for optimization stationarity, while dual perturbation contributes only to further accelerating game stationarity
when used alongside smoothing. Moreover, this cooperative speed-up for game stationarity suggests
that smoothing and dual perturbation enhance convergence to ϵ-GS through distinct, yet complementary,
mechanisms.

4     A Unified Convergence Analysis
In this section, we establish iteration complexity guarantees for both Perturbed GDA (see Theorem 3.1) and
Perturbed Smoothed GDA (see Theorem 3.3) under two stationarity notions: optimization stationarity and
game stationarity. To this end, we develop a unified analytical framework that encompasses both algorithms
and serves as the foundation for the subsequent convergence analyses.
    Our framework contains the following two key components:
    (i) The construction of a Lyapunov function that captures both the primal descent and dual ascent dynamics.

    (ii) Derive a primal-dual balancing inequality that characterizes the interaction between the primal and
         dual updates, which ensures a descent property of the Lyapunov function.
Together, these two components establish the iteration complexity guarantees for single-loop algorithms.
Furthermore, this analytical framework can apply to the double-loop algorithm (see Section 6) as well.
Before proceeding to the formal proof, we summarize in Table 2 the main notations used in this section and
throughout the paper.

5      Tight Analysis
In Section 4, we established the iteration complexity of Perturbed GDA and Perturbed Smoothed GDA for
both game stationarity and optimization stationarity. In this section, we show that these bounds are tight.
Specifically, we construct hard instances and derive lower bounds on the number of iterations required by
each algorithm to reach a stationary point. Comparing these lower bounds with the corresponding upper
bounds from Section 4 establishes the tightness claims. For lower bound analysis in this section, we consider
algorithms with constant choice of ryt for simplicity. Thus, we drop iteration superscripts and write ryt = ry ,
ct = c, αt = α, and β t = β, as these parameters are fixed across all iterations.

6      Double-loop Algorithm: Perturbed Smoothed FOAM
Our analysis for the iteration complexity of Perturbed Smoothed GDA on ϵ-GS shows the synergistic effect
between smoothing and perturbation. In this section, we show that this synergy between smoothing and
perturbation is actually structural to the NC-C minimax problem and can extend naturally to a double-loop
algorithmic framework as well. Specifically, we develop a double-loop Perturbed Smoothed FOAM algorithm
that uses both smoothing and perturbationtechniques. We prove      that its iteration complexity of finding an
ϵ-GS and an ϵ-OS point is O ϵ−2.5 log( 1ϵ ) and O ϵ−3 log( 1ϵ ) , respectively. To the best of our knowledge,
these rates are state-of-the-art among all first-order methods for NC-C minimax problems.
     The idea of Perturbed Smoothed FOAM is as follows. We employ the same surrogate function Ft
defined in (3.1) as in Perturbed Smoothed GDA. Importantly, the combination of smoothing and dual
perturbation transforms the original NC-C problem into a sequence of strongly convex-strongly concave
(SC-SC) subproblems. In particular, for any fixed z, the function Ft (·, ·, z) is strongly convex in x and
strongly concave in y. Unlike Perturbed Smoothed GDA, which performs gradient updates on (xt , y t , z t ) in
each iteration, Perturbed Smoothed FOAM adopts a double-loop scheme. Specifically, at each iteration t, we
first fix z t and compute (xt+1 , y t+1 ) as an approximate solution to the SC-SC subproblem
                                           pt (z t ) = min max Ft (x, y, z t ),                           (6.1)
                                                     x∈X y∈Y

and then perform a gradient step on z t . To solve the inner SC-SC problem on x and y, we use the single-loop
algorithm FOAM proposed by (Kovalev and Gasnikov, 2022). Specifically, we let FOAM(h, x0 , y 0 , µx , µy , δ)
denote the output of FOAM algorithm applied to a SC-SC function h. Here, (x0 , y 0 ) is the initial point, and
µx and µy denote the strong convexity modulus of h(x, y) in x and the strong concavity modulus of h(x, y)
in y, respectively. FOAM  returns a δ-accurate solution (cf. (Kovalev and Gasnikov, 2022, Definition 1)) in

7    Closing Remarks
This paper provides a unified framework to analyze smoothing and perturbation in NC-C minimax optimization.
Rather than viewing these techniques as interchangeable acceleration tools, our framework clarifies their
distinct algorithmic roles and explains when and how they interact to yield sharper convergence guarantees.
This separation leads to a more transparent analysis of existing first-order methods and provides deeper
understanding to these acceleration tools, which enables us to unify several seemingly disparate convergence
results in the literature. An interesting direction for future research is to investigate whether the structural
insights developed here—particularly the decoupling of smoothing and perturbation and the associated
primal–dual viewpoint—can be exploited to obtain stronger or more general convergence guarantees. Recent
works such as (Zheng et al., 2024, 2025) and (Lu and Wang, 2025) establish convergence results under
broad global or local Kurdyka–Łojasiewicz–type assumptions. It would be of interest to explore whether
our framework can be used to streamline these analyses or to clarify the algorithmic roles of smoothing and
perturbation under such general conditions.
