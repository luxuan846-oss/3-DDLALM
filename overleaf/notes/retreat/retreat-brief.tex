\sect{Topic I: ALM}
Shrink the Dual Regularization, Keep the Penalty: Tight Complexity of Linearized Augmented Lagrangian Methods

% \subsect{Abstract}
% {\small\sloppy
% Augmented Lagrangian methods can keep the penalty parameter bounded, but
% existing $\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-3})$ guarantees for single-loop linearized
% methods on $\min_{\mathbf{x}} f(\mathbf{x})+h(\mathcal{A}(\mathbf{x}))$, with
% $f,\mathcal{A}$ smooth and $h$ convex and Lipschitz, let the penalty or the dual
% regularization grow with $1/\epsilon$. We study a family of single-loop
% gradient descent--ascent methods on the reduced augmented Lagrangian with fixed
% penalty $\rho$, dual regularization $s$, and moving primal and dual centers;
% every member has the combined penalty $\rho+s$. Hard instances show that if
% $\epsilon(\rho+s)$ is at most a constant, every choice of constant center
% stepsizes needs $\Omega(\epsilon^{-4})$ iterations to reach an
% $\epsilon$-KKT point, and this order is attained for fixed $\rho$. With $\rho$
% fixed and $s$ of order $1/\epsilon$, a member of the family attains
% $\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-3})$. Hence the combined penalty must reach order
% $1/\epsilon$ to improve on $\epsilon^{-4}$, and the dual regularization alone
% can carry this growth.
% \par}
