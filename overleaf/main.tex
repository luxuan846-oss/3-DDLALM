\newcommand{\papertitle}{Accelerated Linearized Augmented Lagrangian
Method with a Fixed Penalty for Nonconvex Composite Optimization}
\newcommand{\paperkeywords}{nonconvex composite optimization, augmented
Lagrangian method, single-loop method, fixed penalty, dual regularization,
lower complexity bound}
\newcommand{\papermsc}{90C26, 90C30, 90C47, 90C60, 65K05, 49M37}
\newcommand{\paperabstract}{%
In this paper, we study single-loop linearized augmented Lagrangian methods
(ALMs) for nonsmooth nonconvex composite optimization. Existing single-loop
penalty and linearized ALM methods typically attain \(\mathcal O(\epsilon^{-3})\)
complexity by scaling the penalty as \(\Theta(\epsilon^{-1})\), which inflates the
primal smoothness modulus and shrinks the primal stepsize. Although a constant
penalty avoids this growth, the achievable complexity of fixed-penalty
single-loop methods remains largely unexplored. To address this gap, we study a
family of linearized regularized ALMs (LRALM) with moving centers, where dual
regularization raises the effective penalty without increasing the primal
smoothness modulus. Matching upper and lower bounds show that, over constant
center stepsizes, the best worst-case \(\epsilon\)-KKT complexity in this family
is \(\Theta(\epsilon^{-4})\) for an effective penalty below order \(\epsilon^{-1}\),
and \(\Theta(\epsilon^{-3})\) under a fixed penalty with \(\Theta(\epsilon^{-1})\)
dual regularization. With a zero dual center in this regime, the multiplier
update performs inexact gradient ascent on a smooth strongly concave dual
objective. Motivated by this structure, we propose Fast LRALM, which updates the
multipliers via inexact Nesterov accelerated gradient ascent. Using one
first-order oracle call per iteration without inner solvers or dual projections,
Fast LRALM achieves \(\mathcal O(\epsilon^{-5/2})\) complexity with a fixed
penalty. To our knowledge, it is the first single-loop linearized ALM to attain
this rate for nonconvex composite optimization without constraint
qualifications, matching the optimal rate for smooth nonconvex-concave minimax
problems.
}
