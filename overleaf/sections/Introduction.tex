\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
In this paper, we study the structured nonsmooth nonconvex composite problem
\begin{align}
    \min_{\bx\in\RR^n}\quad & F(\bx) := f(\bx) + h(\calA(\bx)),
    \tag{\textbf{P}}    
    \label{prob:main}
\end{align}
where \(f\colon\RR^n\to\RR\) is a possibly nonconvex function with a Lipschitz
continuous gradient, \(\calA\colon\RR^n\to\RR^m\) is a smooth and possibly nonlinear
mapping, and \(h\colon\RR^m\to\RR\cup\{+\infty\}\) is a proper closed convex function
whose proximal mapping is easy to evaluate; the complete assumptions are stated in
Section~\ref{sec:assumptions}. In this formulation, \(f\) is
usually a smooth loss or data-fidelity term, whereas \(h\) is a nonsmooth convex function
that imposes robustness, sparsity, or other structure on the transformed variable
\(\calA(\bx)\)~\cite{charisopoulos2021low}. Several models 
of practical interest take this form.  
\subsection{Motivating examples}
\textbf{Minimizing a pointwise maximum}~\cite{zhang2020smoothed}.
For smooth losses \(f_1,\dots,f_m\), such as logistic or robust classification
losses on \(m\) data subgroups, one solves  
\begin{equation*}
  \min_{\bx\in\RR^n}\;\max_{1\le i\le m} f_i(\bx).
\end{equation*}
This is Problem~\eqref{prob:main} with \(h(\bu)=\max_{1\le i\le m}u_i\) and
\(\calA(\bx)=(f_1(\bx),\dots,f_m(\bx))\); a smooth regularizer, if present, 
enters through \(f\). 

\textbf{Nonconvex robust regression with structured regularization}~\cite{bohm2021variable}.
Given samples \(\{(\ba_j,b_j)\}_{j=1}^N\) and a linear analysis operator
\(\bB\in\RR^{m\times n}\), such as a finite-difference matrix for total variation
or fused lasso, one solves
\begin{equation*}
  \min_{\bx\in\RR^n}\;
  \frac{1}{2N}\sum_{j=1}^N\log\bigl(1+(\langle\ba_j,\bx\rangle-b_j)^2\bigr)
  +\|\bB\bx\|_1.
\end{equation*}
This is Problem~\eqref{prob:main} with the Cauchy loss \(f\),
\(\calA(\bx)=\bB\bx\), and \(h=\|\cdot\|_1\).

\textbf{Exact \(\ell_1\)-penalty reformulations}~\cite{burke1985descent,Drusvyatskiy2019}.
For a smooth constrained problem \(\min_{\bx\in\RR^n} f(\bx)\) subject to
\(\bc(\bx)\le\zero\), such as logistic or sigmoid risk constraints, an exact
penalty formulation solves
\begin{equation*}
  \min_{\bx\in\RR^n}\; f(\bx)+\nu\bigl\|[\bc(\bx)]_+\bigr\|_1,
\end{equation*}
with weight \(\nu>0\) and \([\bu]_+:=\max\{\bu,\zero\}\) applied componentwise.
This is Problem~\eqref{prob:main} with \(\calA=\bc\) and
\(h(\bu)=\nu\|[\bu]_+\|_1\).

\subsection{Related work}
A standard way to handle the nonsmooth composition \(h\circ\calA\) in
Problem~\eqref{prob:main} is to introduce an auxiliary variable \(\by=\calA(\bx)\)
and reformulate it as the equality-constrained problem
\begin{equation}\label{prob:split}
  \min_{\bx,\by}\; f(\bx)+h(\by)\quad\text{s.t.}\quad \calA(\bx)=\by.
\end{equation}
Most algorithms for Problems~\eqref{prob:main} and~\eqref{prob:split}, including
prox-linear~\cite{Drusvyatskiy2019,burke1985descent,lewis2016proximal},
quadratic penalty~\cite{kong2019complexity,lin2022complexity}, and inexact or
proximal augmented Lagrangian methods
(ALMs)~\cite{sahin2019inexact,li2021rate,xie2021proximal}, are
multi-loop methods that solve an inner subproblem approximately at each outer
iteration and require precise termination criteria. In large-scale settings,
single-loop designs become attractive. Existing
single-loop methods for Problem~\eqref{prob:main}, however, typically
attain an \(\mathcal O(\epsilon^{-3})\) complexity by letting a parameter
scale with \(\epsilon\). Single-loop penalty and linearized ALM or alternating direction method of
multipliers (ADMM) 
methods~\cite{luSingleLoopGradientDescent2022,sun2024dual_descent_alm_admm,elbourkhissi2025perturbed}
drive the penalty parameter to order \(\epsilon^{-1}\) to
control feasibility. As a result, the primal smoothness bound grows as
\(\Theta(\epsilon^{-1})\) and restricts the primal stepsize to
\(\Theta(\epsilon)\), which slows convergence. Keeping the penalty 
fixed avoids this growth and is a classical appeal of ALMs over penalty
methods~\cite{bertsekas1982,nocedal2006numerical}. Recent fixed-penalty
methods attain \(\mathcal O(\epsilon^{-2})\) complexity under
structural regularity conditions, such as uniform linear independence
constraint qualification (LICQ) for smooth nonlinear
constraints~\cite{elbourkhissi2025linearized,liu2026fixed}, or solve exact
prox-linear subproblems under local regularity~\cite{zhu2026nonconvex}. For
orthogonality constraints, a single-loop linearized ALM keeps the penalty
fixed and transfers the \(\epsilon^{-1}\) scaling to a dual regularizer,
attaining \(\mathcal O(\epsilon^{-3})\) complexity~\cite{zhu2026primal}. Beyond such special structures, a
convergence theory for nonconvex ALMs with a constant penalty is still
lacking~\cite{deng2026augmented}.

Writing \(h\) via its convex conjugate recasts Problem~\eqref{prob:main} as the
nonconvex-linear minimax formulation
\[
  \min_{\bx\in\RR^n}\max_{\bq\in\RR^m}\;
  \calL(\bx,\bq) = \Phi(\bx,\bq)-h^*(\bq), \qquad
  \Phi(\bx,\bq):=f(\bx)+\langle\bq,\calA(\bx)\rangle,
\]
whose game-stationary (GS) points coincide with the KKT points
of Problem~\eqref{prob:main}. Linearized ALM replaces the exact \(\bx\)-minimization of classical
ALM~\cite{hestenes1969multiplier,powell1969method,rockafellar1973multiplier} by
one gradient step on the reduced augmented Lagrangian and becomes gradient
descent-ascent (GDA) on a smooth nonconvex-concave (NC-C) minimax problem. In
the general smooth NC-C setting, two-timescale GDA requires
\(\mathcal O(\epsilon^{-6})\) iterations in Moreau-envelope optimization
stationarity~\cite{lin2020gradient}, as does its alternating proximal
variant~\cite{bot2023alternating}. For game stationarity, primal
smoothing in Smoothed GDA~\cite{zhang2020smoothed} and dual perturbation in
Perturbed GDA~\cite{lu2020hybrid,xu2023unified} each attain
\(\mathcal O(\epsilon^{-4})\), a rate also attained by doubly smoothed
GDA~\cite{zheng2023universal}. Combining both techniques, Perturbed Smoothed GDA
reduces the GS complexity to \(\mathcal O(\epsilon^{-3})\) with a dual
regularization parameter of order \(\epsilon^{-1}\), and matching
lower bounds show that these rates are tight for the two
methods~\cite{li2026smoothing}. Single-loop methods exploiting the
nonconvex-linear structure likewise achieve \(\mathcal O(\epsilon^{-3})\) through
\(\epsilon\)-dependent dual
regularization~\cite{pan2021efficient,he2024approximation,xu2026rada}.

In these \(\mathcal O(\epsilon^{-3})\) single-loop guarantees, either
the penalty parameter or the dual regularization parameter \(s\) grows as
\(\epsilon^{-1}\). For Problem~\eqref{prob:main}, existing lower bounds
either hold over general first-order method classes at
order \(\epsilon^{-2}\)~\cite{carmon2020lower,liu2026lower}, which cannot separate
the \(\epsilon^{-3}\) and \(\epsilon^{-4}\) rates, or apply only to
individual single-loop primal-dual algorithms under prescribed
stepsizes~\cite{mahdavinia2022tight,li2026smoothing}. They leave unclear
whether this growth is necessary and what complexity single-loop
primal-dual methods attain with a fixed penalty. Motivated by this,
we introduce a regularized augmented Lagrangian \(\mathcal P_s\) for
Problem~\eqref{prob:main} in~\eqref{eq:potential}, which adds primal and
dual quadratic regularizers with parameters \(\gamma>0\) and \(s>0\) to
the reduced augmented Lagrangian \(\calD_\rho\) in~\eqref{eq:reduced-al}. It
decouples feasibility control from the penalty \(\rho\) and recasts
Problem~\eqref{prob:main} as a smooth NC-C minimax problem. In this general
setting, similar primal-dual regularization has led to
compelling \(\mathcal O(\epsilon^{-3})\) guarantees with a dual regularization
parameter of order \(\epsilon^{-1}\)~\cite{li2026smoothing}. These results
motivate our first question for Problem~\eqref{prob:main}.
\begin{quote}
  \phantomsection\hypertarget{Q1}{\textbf{(Q1)}}
  \emph{Can a fixed-penalty linearized ALM (\(\rho=\Theta(1)\)) still
  attain \(\mathcal O(\epsilon^{-3})\) complexity for Problem~\eqref{prob:main},
  and if so, can its dual regularization parameter \(s\) also remain
  \(\Theta(1)\)?}
\end{quote}

We resolve \hyperlink{Q1}{\textbf{(Q1)}} by studying a family \(\calC_\theta\)
of single-loop linearized regularized ALMs (LRALM) for
Problem~\eqref{prob:main} with penalty \(\rho\), dual regularization parameter
\(s\), and constant center stepsizes, which take alternating GDA steps on
\(\mathcal P_s\) together with moving primal and dual centers. The family contains linearized ALM
and, viewed as minimax methods on \(\calD_\rho\), Smoothed GDA and Perturbed
Smoothed GDA. Specifically, we establish matching
lower and upper bounds yielding the tight worst-case complexities  
\[
    \underbrace{\Theta(\epsilon^{-4})}_{\rho+s\le c_1/\epsilon} 
    \qquad\text{and}\qquad
    \underbrace{\Theta(\epsilon^{-3})}_{\rho=\Theta(1),\; s=c_s/\epsilon}
\]
in Theorems~\ref{thm:lower-bound} and~\ref{thm:lower-bound-fixed-rho} and their
matching upper bounds. Thus, for a fixed-penalty linearized ALM, the dual
regularization parameter must grow as \(\epsilon^{-1}\) to improve on
\(\epsilon^{-4}\), and \(s=\Theta(\epsilon^{-1})\) suffices for \(\epsilon^{-3}\).
Within the family, \(\epsilon^{-3}\) cannot be improved, and the limitation comes
from the primal center stepsize \(\alpha\). Whereas prior lower bounds cover only
general first-order classes or individual methods, these results establish tight
complexity bounds across a family of single-loop linearized ALMs and show that
leaving \(\calC_\theta\) is necessary for acceleration.

In smooth NC-C minimax optimization, faster rates have recently
been attained. The optimal deterministic first-order complexity
for \(\epsilon\)-GS points is \(\Theta(\epsilon^{-5/2})\), matched by the
multi-loop methods~\cite{lin2020near,ostrovskii2021efficient,pan2026optimal} and by the
single-loop moving-center extragradient method with momentum (MC-EGM) of
Zhang et~al.~\cite{zhang2026matching}. While these methods are developed
for general smooth minimax problems, Problem~\eqref{prob:main} carries
additional structure through \(\calD_\rho\) that a tailored method can exploit.
These results motivate our second question for Problem~\eqref{prob:main}.
\begin{quote}
  \phantomsection\hypertarget{Q2}{\textbf{(Q2)}}
  \emph{Can a single-loop linearized ALM with a fixed penalty parameter improve on
  the \(\Theta(\epsilon^{-3})\) complexity of \(\calC_\theta\) and match the
  \(\mathcal O(\epsilon^{-5/2})\) rate for Problem~\eqref{prob:main}?}
\end{quote}

To answer \hyperlink{Q2}{\textbf{(Q2)}}, we develop Fast LRALM, a single-loop
linearized ALM with a fixed penalty. Going beyond \(\calC_\theta\), the method
exploits the smooth strongly concave dual structure of \(\mathcal P_s\) via a
projection-free inexact Nesterov accelerated ascent
step~\cite{nesterov1983method}. This dual acceleration supports a primal
center stepsize \(\alpha=\Theta(\epsilon^{1/2})\) instead of \(\mathcal O(\epsilon)\)
without inner loops. A refined analysis
then shows that Fast LRALM finds an \(\epsilon\)-KKT point of Problem~\eqref{prob:main} in
\(\mathcal O(\epsilon^{-5/2})\) iterations with a fixed penalty \(\rho\)
by Theorem~\ref{thm:b2-prime-complexity}. This result matches both the
best-known single-loop rate for this problem class and the optimal rate for
smooth NC-C minimax problems~\cite{pan2026optimal}.
Figure~\ref{fig:complexity-axis} summarizes our results, and
Table~\ref{tab:known-results} compares our complexity bounds and parameter
scales with representative methods.

\paragraph{Notation.} We use \(\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\) and \(\|\cdot\|\) for the Euclidean inner
product and norm; for a matrix, \(\|\cdot\|\) denotes the spectral norm. For
\(\calA=(A_1,\ldots,A_m)^\top\), the Jacobian \(J_{\calA}(\bx)\in\RR^{m\times n}\)
has \(i\)th row \(\nabla A_i(\bx)^\top\). When convenient, we identify \(\RR^2\)
with \(\mathbb C\), so that \(\|p\|=|p|\) and
\(\langle p,q\rangle=\operatorname{Re}(\bar pq)\). Standard asymptotic notation
\(\mathcal O(\cdot)\), \(\Omega(\cdot)\), and \(\Theta(\cdot)\) hides universal
positive constants independent of the accuracy \(\epsilon\) and the dimensions
\(n,m\).
