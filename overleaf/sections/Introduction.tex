\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
In this paper, we study the structured nonsmooth nonconvex composite problem
\begin{align}
    \min_{\bx\in\RR^n}\quad & F(\bx) := f(\bx) + h(\calA(\bx)),
    \tag{\textbf{P}} 
    \label{prob:main}
\end{align}
where $f\colon\RR^n\to\RR$ is a possibly nonconvex function with a Lipschitz
continuous gradient, $\calA\colon\RR^n\to\RR^m$ is a smooth and possibly nonlinear
mapping, and $h\colon\RR^m\to\RR\cup\{+\infty\}$ is a proper closed convex function
whose proximal mapping is easy to evaluate; the complete assumptions are stated in
Section~\ref{sec:assumptions}. In this formulation, $f$ is
usually a smooth loss or data-fidelity term, whereas $h$ is a nonsmooth convex function
that imposes robustness, sparsity, or other structure on the transformed variable
$\calA(\bx)$  
\cite{charisopoulos2021low}. Several models 
of practical interest take this form.  
\subsection{Motivating examples}
\textbf{Robust phase  retrieval}~\cite{duchi2019solving}.
Given measurement vectors $\mathbf a_i\in\RR^n$ and observations
$b_i\approx\langle\mathbf a_i,\bx^\star\rangle^2$, a fraction of which may be grossly
corrupted, one solves 
\begin{equation*} 
  \min_{\bx\in\RR^n}\;\frac1m\sum_{i=1}^m
  \bigl|\langle\mathbf a_i,\bx\rangle^2-b_i\bigr|.
\end{equation*}
This is Problem~\eqref{prob:main} with $f\equiv0$, $h=\frac1m\|\cdot\|_1$, and
$\calA(\bx)_i=\langle\mathbf a_i,\bx\rangle^2-b_i$; the $\ell_1$ loss makes the
model robust to outliers.

\textbf{Robust low-rank matrix recovery}~\cite{li2020nonconvex}.
Given a linear measurement map $\mathcal M\colon\RR^{d_1\times d_2}\to\RR^m$ and
observations $\mathbf b$ containing outliers, a rank-$r$ matrix is recovered in
factorized form via
\begin{equation*}
  \min_{\mathbf U\in\RR^{d_1\times r},\,\mathbf V\in\RR^{d_2\times r}}
  \frac1m\bigl\|\mathcal M(\mathbf U\mathbf V^\top)-\mathbf b\bigr\|_1.
\end{equation*}
Here $\bx=(\mathbf U,\mathbf V)$, $h=\frac1m\|\cdot\|_1$, and
$\calA(\bx)=\mathcal M(\mathbf U\mathbf V^\top)-\mathbf b$ is bilinear in
$(\mathbf U,\mathbf V)$, so the model again has the form of Problem~\eqref{prob:main}.

\textbf{Minimizing a pointwise maximum}~\cite{zhang2020smoothed}.
For smooth losses $f_1,\dots,f_m$, such as the losses on $m$ data subgroups, one solves
\begin{equation*}
  \min_{\bx\in\RR^n}\;\max_{1\le i\le m} f_i(\bx).
\end{equation*}
This is Problem~\eqref{prob:main} with $h(\bu)=\max_{1\le i\le m}u_i$ and
$\calA(\bx)=(f_1(\bx),\dots,f_m(\bx))$; a smooth regularizer, if present, enters
through $f$, and the proximal mapping of $h$ reduces to a projection onto the unit simplex. 

\subsection{Related work}
A standard way to handle the nonsmooth composition $h\circ\calA$ in
Problem~\eqref{prob:main} is to introduce an auxiliary variable $\by=\calA(\bx)$
and reformulate it as the equality-constrained problem
\begin{equation}\label{prob:split}
  \min_{\bx,\by}\; f(\bx)+h(\by)\quad\text{s.t.}\quad \calA(\bx)=\by.
\end{equation}
Most algorithms for Problems~\eqref{prob:main} and~\eqref{prob:split} rely on nested
loops. Prox-linear methods~\cite{Drusvyatskiy2019,burke1985descent,lewis2016proximal}, quadratic penalty
methods~\cite{kong2019complexity,lin2022complexity}, and inexact or proximal
augmented Lagrangian methods
(ALMs)~\cite{sahin2019inexact,li2021rate,xie2021proximal,zhu2026nonconvex} solve a subproblem
approximately at every outer iteration, which incurs inner-loop overhead and
requires careful tuning of termination criteria. In large-scale settings,
single-loop first-order methods become attractive because they perform a single
explicit gradient or proximal update per variable at each iteration. Existing
single-loop schemes for Problem~\eqref{prob:main}, however, typically
attain an $\mathcal O(\epsilon^{-3})$ complexity by letting a parameter
scale with $\epsilon$. Variable smoothing~\cite{bohm2021variable,bot2015variable} replaces $h$ with its
Moreau envelope~\cite{moreau1965} using a smoothing parameter of order $\epsilon$, while
single-loop penalty and linearized ALM or ADMM
schemes~\cite{hajinezhad2019perturbed,luSingleLoopGradientDescent2022,hallak2023adaptive,alacaoglu2024complexity,sun2024dual_descent_alm_admm,elbourkhissi2025perturbed,shi2025momentum},
including a linearized ALM for orthogonality constraints~\cite{zhu2026primal},
drive the penalty or dual perturbation parameter to order $\epsilon^{-1}$ to
control feasibility. In either route, the primal smoothness modulus inflates to
$\Theta(\epsilon^{-1})$ and forces the primal stepsize down to
$\Theta(\epsilon)$. Fixed-penalty methods with $\mathcal O(\epsilon^{-2})$
complexity rely on structural regularity conditions, such as polyhedral error
bounds for affine constraints~\cite{zhang2022global} or uniform LICQ for smooth
nonlinear constraints~\cite{elbourkhissi2025linearized,liu2026fixed}.

Writing $h$ via its convex conjugate recasts Problem~\eqref{prob:main} as the
nonconvex-linear minimax formulation
\[
  \min_{\bx\in\RR^n}\max_{\bq\in\RR^m}\;
  \calL(\bx,\bq) = \Phi(\bx,\bq)-h^*(\bq), \qquad
  \Phi(\bx,\bq):=f(\bx)+\langle\bq,\calA(\bx)\rangle,
\]
whose game-stationary (GS) points coincide with the KKT points
of Problem~\eqref{prob:main}. Likewise, eliminating $\by$ from the augmented
Lagrangian for a fixed penalty $\rho>0$ produces a smooth nonconvex-concave
(NC-C) minimax problem, on which single-loop linearized ALMs act as gradient
descent-ascent (GDA) schemes. For smooth NC-C
minimax problems, two-timescale GDA requires $\mathcal O(\epsilon^{-6})$
iterations~\cite{lin2020gradient}, as does its alternating proximal
variant~\cite{bot2023alternating}. Primal smoothing in Smoothed
GDA~\cite{zhang2020smoothed} and dual perturbation in Perturbed
GDA~\cite{lu2020hybrid,xu2023unified} each improve the rate to
$\mathcal O(\epsilon^{-4})$, a rate also attained by doubly smoothed
GDA~\cite{zheng2023universal}. Combining both techniques, Perturbed Smoothed GDA
reduces the GS complexity to $\mathcal O(\epsilon^{-3})$ with a dual
regularization parameter of order $\epsilon^{-1}$, and matching lower bounds of
$\Omega(\epsilon^{-4})$ and $\Omega(\epsilon^{-3})$ certify that these rates
are tight for the two algorithms~\cite{li2026smoothing}. Single-loop methods
exploiting the nonconvex-linear structure likewise achieve
$\mathcal O(\epsilon^{-3})$ through $\epsilon$-dependent dual
regularization~\cite{pan2021efficient,he2024approximation,xu2026rada}.

Across all of these $\mathcal O(\epsilon^{-3})$ single-loop guarantees, either
the penalty parameter or the dual regularization parameter grows as
$\epsilon^{-1}$. Keeping the penalty parameter fixed is a classical appeal of
ALMs over penalty methods~\cite{bertsekas1982,nocedal2006numerical}, since it prevents the primal smoothness modulus from
growing as $\epsilon\to0$, yet a general convergence theory for nonconvex ALMs
with a constant penalty is still lacking~\cite{deng2026augmented}. Moreover,
existing lower bounds either hold over general first-order method classes at
order $\epsilon^{-2}$~\cite{carmon2020lower,liu2026lower}, which cannot separate
the $\epsilon^{-3}$ and $\epsilon^{-4}$ rates, or apply only to
individual single-loop primal-dual algorithms under prescribed
stepsizes~\cite{mahdavinia2022tight,li2026smoothing}, leaving unclear whether
parameter growth is inherent to single-loop linearized ALMs and whether the
penalty itself must carry it. This motivates our first question.
\begin{quote}
  \phantomsection\hypertarget{Q1}{\textbf{(Q1)}}
  \emph{Can a fixed-penalty linearized ALM ($\rho=\Theta(1)$) still
  attain $\mathcal O(\epsilon^{-3})$ complexity for Problem~\eqref{prob:main},
  and if so, can its dual regularization parameter $s$ also remain
  $\Theta(1)$?}
\end{quote}

We resolve \hyperlink{Q1}{\textbf{(Q1)}} by studying a family $\calC_\theta$
of single-loop linearized regularized ALMs (LRALM) with penalty $\rho$, dual
regularization parameter $s$, and constant center stepsizes that subsumes
Smoothed GDA and Perturbed Smoothed GDA. In
Section~\ref{sec:algorithm-framework}, we show that every member shares the
effective penalty $\rho+s$, and establish matching lower and upper bounds
yielding the tight worst-case complexities
\[
    \underbrace{\Theta(\epsilon^{-4})}_{\rho+s\le c_1/\epsilon}
    \qquad\text{and}\qquad
    \underbrace{\Theta(\epsilon^{-3})}_{\rho=\Theta(1),\; s=\Theta(\epsilon^{-1})}
\]
in Theorems~\ref{thm:lower-bound} and~\ref{thm:lower-bound-fixed-rho} and their
matching upper bounds. Thus, the answer to \hyperlink{Q1}{\textbf{(Q1)}} is
negative when both $\rho$ and $s$ remain $\Theta(1)$ (in fact whenever
$\rho+s\le c_1/\epsilon$), and positive when $\rho$ stays fixed while
$s=\Theta(\epsilon^{-1})$, with $\Theta(\epsilon^{-3})$ being tight across
$\calC_\theta$.

While $\Theta(\epsilon^{-3})$ is an unimprovable barrier for $\calC_\theta$,
faster $\epsilon^{-5/2}$ rates are achievable in closely related settings. For
smooth NC-C minimax problems, the optimal deterministic first-order complexity
for $\epsilon$-GS points is $\Theta(\epsilon^{-5/2})$~\cite{pan2026optimal},
consistent with the nonconvex-strongly-concave lower bound at dual condition
number $\Theta(\epsilon^{-1})$~\cite{zhang2021complexity,li2021complexity}.
This rate is attained by multi-loop schemes~\cite{pan2026optimal}, with
earlier ones reaching it up to logarithmic
factors~\cite{lin2020near,ostrovskii2021efficient}, and by a
single-loop projected extragradient method with dual gradient
averaging~\cite{zhang2026matching}. For nonsmooth composite minimax and
affinely constrained problems, existing $\epsilon^{-5/2}$-order algorithms all 
rely on nested loops, including multi-loop proximal-point or anchored minimax
schemes~\cite{kong2021accelerated,ke2026efficient} and inexact ALM or penalty
methods with accelerated inner
solvers~\cite{li2021rate,lin2022complexity,kong2023iteration,li2021augmented}. This contrast
motivates the second question we tackle in this paper.
\begin{quote}
  \phantomsection\hypertarget{Q2}{\textbf{(Q2)}}
  \emph{Can a single-loop linearized ALM with a fixed penalty parameter break
  the $\Theta(\epsilon^{-3})$ barrier of $\calC_\theta$ and match the
  $\mathcal O(\epsilon^{-5/2})$ rate for Problem~\eqref{prob:main}?}
\end{quote}

To answer \hyperlink{Q2}{\textbf{(Q2)}}, we introduce Fast LRALM. A structural insight from
\hyperlink{Q1}{\textbf{(Q1)}} is that, with $\rho$ fixed and $s=\Theta(\epsilon^{-1})$,
anchoring the dual center at zero preserves the optimal rate inside
$\calC_\theta$ and turns the regularized dual objective into a strongly
concave function that does not vary across iterations. Going beyond $\calC_\theta$, Fast LRALM applies Nesterov
extrapolation~\cite{nesterov1983method} directly to the dual multiplier
update, as in accelerated ALMs for linearly constrained convex
problems~\cite{he2010acceleration}, while retaining a single
explicit primal-dual step per iteration. Our analysis shows that Fast LRALM
finds an $\epsilon$-KKT point of Problem~\eqref{prob:main} in
$\mathcal O(\epsilon^{-5/2})$ iterations with a fixed penalty $\rho$
by Theorem~\ref{thm:b2-prime-complexity}, matching, to the best of our
knowledge, the fastest multi-loop rate for this problem class without
inner solvers or dual projections. This rate also matches the
optimal deterministic first-order rate for smooth NC-C minimax
problems under game stationarity~\cite{pan2026optimal}. In the absence of dual
projection, we derive uniform multiplier bounds directly from the reduced
augmented Lagrangian structure and analyze the coupled primal-dual dynamics via
a Lyapunov sequence in Section~\ref{sec:acceleration}.
Figure~\ref{fig:complexity-axis} summarizes our results, and
Table~\ref{tab:known-results} compares our complexity bounds and parameter
scales with representative methods.

\paragraph{Notation.} We use \(\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\) and \(\|\cdot\|\) for the Euclidean inner
product and norm; for a matrix, \(\|\cdot\|\) denotes the spectral norm. For
\(\calA=(A_1,\ldots,A_m)^\top\), the Jacobian \(J_{\calA}(\bx)\in\RR^{m\times n}\)
has \(i\)th row \(\nabla A_i(\bx)^\top\). When convenient, we identify \(\RR^2\)
with \(\mathbb C\), so that \(\|p\|=|p|\) and
\(\langle p,q\rangle=\operatorname{Re}(\bar pq)\). Standard asymptotic notation
\(\mathcal O(\cdot)\), \(\Omega(\cdot)\), and \(\Theta(\cdot)\) hides universal
positive constants independent of the accuracy \(\epsilon\) and the dimensions
\(n,m\).
