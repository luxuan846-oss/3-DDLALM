\begin{abstract}
We study single-loop linearized augmented Lagrangian methods for the
nonconvex composite problem $\min_{\bx} f(\bx)+h(\calA(\bx))$, where $f$ and
$\calA$ are smooth and $h$ is convex and Lipschitz. We consider a family of
methods that perform one gradient descent--ascent step per iteration on the
reduced augmented Lagrangian with a fixed penalty $\rho$, a dual
regularization with parameter $s$, and moving primal and dual centers. Every
member of this family has the effective penalty $\rho+s$. We show that the
iteration complexity of the family for finding an $\epsilon$-KKT point is
determined by $\rho+s$ alone; the center stepsizes do not change its order.
If $\epsilon(\rho+s)$ is at most a constant, then for any arbitrary choice of
constant center stepsizes there is an instance on which the method, started
from zero, requires $\Omega(\epsilon^{-4})$ iterations, and this order is
attained within the family. If $\rho$ is fixed and $s$ is of order
$1/\epsilon$, a member of the family finds an $\epsilon$-KKT point in
$\mathcal O(\epsilon^{-3})$ iterations. Hence the effective penalty must reach
the order $1/\epsilon$ to improve on $\epsilon^{-4}$, and this growth can be
carried by the dual regularization alone, with the penalty kept fixed.
\end{abstract}
