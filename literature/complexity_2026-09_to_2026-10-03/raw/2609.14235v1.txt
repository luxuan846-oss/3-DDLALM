Optimal Deterministic First-Order Oracle Complexity for Nonconvex-Concave Minimax Optimization

We study the deterministic first-order oracle complexity of smooth nonconvex-concave minimax optimization over a bounded convex dual domain. Let $\ell$ denote the joint smoothness constant, $D_{\mathcal{Y}}$ the diameter of the dual domain, and $\Delta$ the initial gap. We prove that every deterministic first-order algorithm requires $\Omega(\ell^{2}D_{\mathcal{Y}}\Delta/\epsilon^{3})$ oracle queries in the worst case to find an $\epsilon$ -optimization-stationary point whenever $\epsilon\lesssim\min\{\ell D_{\mathcal{Y}},\sqrt{\ell\Delta}\}$ . We then develop Tracked-FOAM , a first-order method that attains a matching upper bound, removing the logarithmic factor from previous upper bounds. Together, these results establish the optimal dependence on all problem parameters in the stated regime.

Key words. nonconvex minimax optimization, oracle complexity, lower bounds, first-order methods, convergence analysis

AMS subject classifications. 90C26, 90C47, 90C60

1 Introduction

We study the deterministic first-order oracle complexity of the smooth nonconvex-concave (NC-C) minimax problem

$\min_{\bm{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\max_{\bm{y}\in\mathcal{Y}}f(\bm{x};\bm{y}),$ (P)

where $f$ is jointly $\ell$ -smooth and concave in $\bm{y}$ , $\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}$ is a nonempty closed convex set, and $\mathcal{Y}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}$ is a nonempty compact convex set with diameter $D_{\mathcal{Y}}$ . Let $\Phi(\bm{x}):=\max_{\bm{y}\in\mathcal{Y}}f(\bm{x};\bm{y})+\iota_{\mathcal{X}}(\bm{x})$ , where $\iota_{\mathcal{X}}$ is the indicator function of $\mathcal{X}$ , and assume that $\Phi(\bm{x}^{0})-\inf\Phi\leq\Delta$ . Since $\Phi$ can be nonsmooth, we seek an $\epsilon$ -optimization-stationary point ( $\epsilon$ -OS), namely a point $\bm{x}\in\mathcal{X}$ satisfying $\|\nabla\Phi_{1/(2\ell)}(\bm{x})\|\leq\epsilon$ , where $\Phi_{1/(2\ell)}$ denotes the Moreau envelope of $\Phi$ ( Jin et al., 2020 ; Lin et al., 2020a ; Li et al., 2025 ; Li et al., 2026 ) .

We ask how many first-order oracle queries are necessary and sufficient to find an $\epsilon$ -OS point. Existing first-order approaches include proximal-point methods ( Rafique et al., 2022 ; Thekumparampil et al., 2019 ; Lin et al., 2020a ) , two-timescale gradient descent-ascent (GDA) ( Lin et al., 2020b ) , and smoothing methods ( Zhang et al., 2020 ; Zhao, 2024 ; Li et al., 2025 ; Zheng et al., 2023 ) . With the remaining problem parameters suppressed, the best known upper bounds scale as $\epsilon^{-3}$ up to logarithmic factors. In particular, Minimax-PPA of Lin et al. (2020a) achieves an $O(\epsilon^{-3}\log^{2}(1/\epsilon))$ bound, while Perturbed Smoothed FOAM of Li et al. (2026) improves it to $O(\epsilon^{-3}\log(1/\epsilon))$ . These guarantees leave open whether the $\epsilon^{-3}$ dependence is unavoidable for arbitrary deterministic first-order methods in the worst case and whether the remaining logarithmic factor can be removed.

We answer both questions by establishing the optimal deterministic first-order oracle complexity

$\Theta\!\left(\frac{\ell^{2}D_{\mathcal{Y}}\Delta}{\epsilon^{3}}\right)\qquad\text{whenever}\qquad\epsilon\lesssim\min\!\left\{\ell D_{\mathcal{Y}},\sqrt{\ell\Delta}\right\}.$

The lower bound holds for arbitrary deterministic first-order algorithms. We attain the matching upper bound with Tracked-FOAM , a first-order method that removes the logarithmic factor from previous upper bounds. Thus, the two bounds match in their dependence on $\ell$ , $D_{\mathcal{Y}}$ , $\Delta$ , and $\epsilon$ throughout the stated regime.

The lower bound must account for the restriction imposed by a bounded dual domain. For nonconvex-strongly-concave (NC-SC) minimax problems with strong concavity parameter $\mu$ and condition number $\kappa:=\ell/\mu$ , lower bounds of $\Omega(\ell\Delta\sqrt{\kappa}/\epsilon^{2})$ are known for zero-respecting first-order algorithms ( Li et al., 2021 ) and linear-span first-order methods ( Zhang et al., 2021 ) . Substituting $\kappa\asymp\ell^{2}D_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2}/\epsilon^{2}$ formally gives the desired NC-C rate. However, those constructions use unbounded dual domains. Restricting the dual variable to a prescribed ball can alter the maximized function and invalidate the gradient lower bound. A separate issue arises from the stationarity criterion: a lower bound on the gradient norm of the value function at a query point does not control the gradient norm of its Moreau envelope, which depends on a proximal point that need not have been queried.

We adapt the construction of Li et al. (2021) by modifying its inner dual chain and introducing a new outer construction. The modified inner chain preserves the zero-chain property, has a smoothness constant independent of its length, and admits an explicit bound on its unconstrained dual maximizer. We replace the standard nonconvex outer chain of Carmon et al. (2020) with outer terms that keep the primal inputs to the inner chains bounded at points in our analysis where the gradient of the value function is small. This ensures that the unconstrained dual maximizer lies inside the prescribed dual ball. The same outer terms also let us extend the lower bound on the gradient norm from the value function to its Moreau envelope. Finally, we adapt the finite-horizon resisting-oracle framework of Carmon et al. (2020, Lemma 7) to our setting to extend the lower bound from zero-respecting first-order algorithms to arbitrary deterministic first-order methods.

We next establish a matching upper bound without logarithmic factors. The framework of Li et al. (2026) combines dual perturbation and primal smoothing to generate a sequence of smooth strongly-convex-strongly-concave (SC-SC) subproblems, each solved by the accelerated FOAM method of Kovalev and Gasnikov (2022) . Solving each subproblem to a prescribed accuracy depending on $\epsilon$ incurs a logarithmic cost at every outer iteration, introducing the remaining logarithmic factor in the overall oracle complexity.

Our Tracked-FOAM method retains the full accelerated FOAM state across successive subproblems. We establish a global Lyapunov descent estimate that jointly controls the gradient norm of the Moreau envelope and the error from approximately solving the SC-SC subproblems with FOAM . This estimate shows that a constant-factor contraction of the FOAM Lyapunov function per outer iteration suffices; the inner solver is never run to an $\epsilon$ -dependent accuracy, and the logarithmic factor disappears. Consequently, for every $\epsilon>0$ , Tracked-FOAM finds an $\epsilon$ -OS point within

$\mathcal{O}\left(\left(\frac{\ell\Delta}{\epsilon^{2}}+1\right)\max\left\{1,\frac{\ell D_{\mathcal{Y}}}{\epsilon}\right\}\right)$

first-order oracle queries.

Notation.

Bold lowercase symbols denote vectors, while their scalar coordinates are not bold. The semicolon in $f(\bm{x};\bm{y})$ separates the minimization and maximization variables. We use $\lVert\cdot\rVert$ for the Euclidean norm and its induced operator norm. For $d\in\mathbb{N}$ and $D>0$ , let $\mathbb{B}_{D}^{d}:=\{\bm{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mid\lVert\bm{z}\rVert\leq D/2\}$ denote the centered Euclidean ball of diameter $D$ . We write $[T]:=\{1,\ldots,T\}$ , $[0]:=\varnothing$ , and $\mathbb{N}_{0}:=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}$ . For $d^{\prime}\geq d$ , let $\operatorname{O}(d^{\prime},d):=\{\mathbf{U}\in\mathbb{R}^{d^{\prime}\times d}\mid\mathbf{U}^{\top}\mathbf{U}=\mathbf{I}_{d}\}$ be the set of matrices with orthonormal columns. The zero vector is written as $\mathbf{0}$ , with its dimension omitted when unambiguous. We use $\dist(\bm{x},\mathcal{S}):=\inf_{\bm{z}\in\mathcal{S}}\lVert\bm{x}-\bm{z}\rVert$ to denote the distance from $\bm{x}$ to the set $\mathcal{S}$ . A coordinate ordering is a permutation $\bm{\pi}=(\pi_{1},\ldots,\pi_{d})$ of $[d]$ . For example, the permutation $\bm{\pi}=(2,3,1)$ orders the coordinates of $\bm{z}=(z_{1},z_{2},z_{3})$ as $(z_{2},z_{3},z_{1})$ . For any $\bm{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$ , define its support under $\bm{\pi}$ by $\operatorname{supp}_{\bm{\pi}}(\bm{z}):=\{k\in[d]\mid z_{\pi_{k}}\neq 0\}.$ Under the identity permutation $\bm{\pi}=(1,\ldots,d)$ , we simply write $\operatorname{supp}(\bm{z}):=\{k\in[d]\mid z_{k}\neq 0\}.$ Whenever a coordinate ordering is involved, we identify the pair $(\bm{x},\bm{y})$ with its canonical concatenation, as in $\operatorname{supp}(\bm{x},\bm{y})$ and $\operatorname{supp}_{\bm{\pi}}(\bm{x},\bm{y})$ .

2 Preliminaries

To study Problem ( P ), we introduce the following class of NC-C functions.

Given $\ell,D_{\mathcal{Y}},\Delta>0$ , let $\mathcal{F}(\ell,D_{\mathcal{Y}},\Delta)$ be the union, over all $m,n\in\mathbb{N}$ and all nonempty closed convex sets $\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}$ and $\mathcal{Y}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}$ satisfying $(\mathbf{0}_{m},\mathbf{0}_{n})\in\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}\mbox{ and }\operatorname{diam}(\mathcal{Y})\leq D_{\mathcal{Y}},$ of the class of functions $f:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}\to\mathbb{R}$ satisfying the following properties. For each such function, define $\Phi(\bm{x}):=\max_{\bm{y}\in\mathcal{Y}}f(\bm{x};\bm{y})+\iota_{\mathcal{X}}(\bm{x})$ .

( $\ell$ -smoothness) $f$ is jointly $\ell$ -smooth on $\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}$ , i.e., for all $(\bm{x}_{1},\bm{y}_{1}),(\bm{x}_{2},\bm{y}_{2})\in\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}$ ,

$\left\lVert\nabla f(\bm{x}_{1};\bm{y}_{1})-\nabla f(\bm{x}_{2};\bm{y}_{2})\right\rVert\leq\ell\left\lVert(\bm{x}_{1},\bm{y}_{1})-(\bm{x}_{2},\bm{y}_{2})\right\rVert.$

(Dual concavity) For every fixed $\bm{x}\in\mathcal{X}$ , $f(\bm{x};\cdot)$ is concave on $\mathcal{Y}$ .

(Initial gap) The initialization satisfies $\Phi(\mathbf{0})-\inf_{\bm{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\Phi(\bm{x})\leq\Delta.$

We use the following standard stationarity measure throughout the paper.

Given $\epsilon>0$ , a point $\bm{x}\in\mathcal{X}$ is an $\epsilon$ -optimization-stationary point ( $\epsilon$ -OS) of Problem ( P ) if

$\left\lVert\nabla\Phi_{\frac{1}{2\ell}}(\bm{x})\right\rVert\leq\epsilon,$

where $\Phi_{1/(2\ell)}(\bm{x}):=\min_{\bm{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}\{\Phi(\bm{z})+\ell\lVert\bm{x}-\bm{z}\rVert^{2}\}$ is the Moreau envelope of $\Phi$ with parameter $1/(2\ell)$ .

We next introduce the first-order saddle oracle.

For a differentiable function $f:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}\to\mathbb{R}$ , the first-order saddle oracle at $(\bm{x},\bm{y})\in\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}$ returns

$\mathbb{O}_{f}(\bm{x};\bm{y}):=\bigl(f(\bm{x};\bm{y}),\nabla_{\bm{x}}f(\bm{x};\bm{y}),\nabla_{\bm{y}}f(\bm{x};\bm{y})\bigr).$

For a first-order algorithm $\mathsf{A}$ and a differentiable saddle function $f:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}\to\mathbb{R}$ , let $\mathsf{A}_{\bm{x}}^{(t)}[f]\in\mathcal{X}$ and $\mathsf{A}_{\bm{y}}^{(t)}[f]\in\mathcal{Y}$ denote, respectively, the primal and dual components of its $t$ -th feasible oracle query. We first consider zero-respecting first-order algorithms that access $f$ through the oracle $\mathbb{O}_{f}$ .

A first-order algorithm $\mathsf{A}$ is zero-respecting if, for each such $f$ , it starts from $(\mathsf{A}_{\bm{x}}^{(0)}[f],\mathsf{A}_{\bm{y}}^{(0)}[f])=\mathbf{0}$ and its query sequence satisfies

$\operatorname{supp}\bigl(\mathsf{A}_{\bm{x}}^{(t)}[f],\mathsf{A}_{\bm{y}}^{(t)}[f]\bigr)\subseteq\bigcup_{s<t}\operatorname{supp}\Bigl(\nabla_{\bm{x}}f\bigl(\mathsf{A}_{\bm{x}}^{(s)}[f];\mathsf{A}_{\bm{y}}^{(s)}[f]\bigr),\nabla_{\bm{y}}f\bigl(\mathsf{A}_{\bm{x}}^{(s)}[f];\mathsf{A}_{\bm{y}}^{(s)}[f]\bigr)\Bigr),\qquad\forall t\geq 1.$

We denote the class of such algorithms by $\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{zr}}$ .

We next define $\mathcal{A}_{\det}$ , the class of deterministic first-order algorithms.

A first-order algorithm $\mathsf{A}$ is deterministic if there exists a sequence of maps $\{\Gamma_{t}\}_{t\geq 1}$ such that, for every such $f$ , it starts from $(\mathsf{A}_{\bm{x}}^{(0)}[f],\mathsf{A}_{\bm{y}}^{(0)}[f])=\mathbf{0}$ and its query sequence satisfies

$\bigl(\mathsf{A}_{\bm{x}}^{(t)}[f],\mathsf{A}_{\bm{y}}^{(t)}[f]\bigr)=\Gamma_{t}\left(\Bigl(\mathbb{O}_{f}\bigl(\mathsf{A}_{\bm{x}}^{(s)}[f];\mathsf{A}_{\bm{y}}^{(s)}[f]\bigr)\Bigr)_{s<t}\right),\qquad\forall t\geq 1.$

We denote the class of such algorithms by $\mathcal{A}_{\det}$ .

We measure the number of oracle queries in the worst case as follows.

Fix $\epsilon>0$ . Let $\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{\det}$ be any nonempty subclass of deterministic first-order algorithms, and let $\mathcal{F}$ be a nonempty class of such saddle functions. The oracle complexity of $\mathcal{A}$ over $\mathcal{F}$ is defined by

$\mathcal{T}_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{F}):=\inf_{\mathsf{A}\in\mathcal{A}}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\inf\left\{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\;\middle|\;\mathsf{A}_{\bm{x}}^{(t)}[f]\;{\text{is an }\epsilon\text{-OS for }f}\right\}.$

To construct the hard instance, we next extend the standard zero-chain structure to the joint primal-dual variable.

Let $f:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}$ be differentiable, and let $\bm{\pi}=(\pi_{1},\ldots,\pi_{m+n})$ be a coordinate ordering of $(\bm{x},\bm{y})$ . We say that $f$ is a first-order saddle zero-chain with respect to $\bm{\pi}$ if, for every $i\in[m+n]$ and every $(\bm{x},\bm{y})\in\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}$ ,

$\operatorname{supp}_{\bm{\pi}}(\bm{x},\bm{y})\subseteq[i-1]\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\operatorname{supp}_{\bm{\pi}}\bigl(\nabla_{\bm{x}}f(\bm{x};\bm{y}),\nabla_{\bm{y}}f(\bm{x};\bm{y})\bigr)\subseteq[i].$

The next two lemmas will be used to extend the lower bound to arbitrary deterministic algorithms. Their proofs are given in Appendices A.1 and A.2 , respectively. For $D_{\mathcal{Y}}>0$ , a smooth function $f:\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{B}_{D_{\mathcal{Y}}}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}$ , and matrices $\mathbf{U}\in\operatorname{O}(m^{\prime},m)$ and $\mathbf{V}\in\operatorname{O}(n^{\prime},n)$ , define the rotated function $f_{\mathbf{U},\mathbf{V}}:\mathbb{R}^{m^{\prime}}\times\mathbb{B}_{D_{\mathcal{Y}}}^{n^{\prime}}\to\mathbb{R}$ by

$f_{\mathbf{U},\mathbf{V}}(\bm{x};\bm{y}):=f(\mathbf{U}^{\top}\bm{x};\mathbf{V}^{\top}\bm{y}).$

Let $\Phi_{\mathbf{U},\mathbf{V}}(\bm{x}):=\max_{\bm{y}\in\mathbb{B}_{D_{\mathcal{Y}}}^{n^{\prime}}}f_{\mathbf{U},\mathbf{V}}(\bm{x};\bm{y})$ denote its value function.

Let $f\in\mathcal{F}(\ell,D_{\mathcal{Y}},\Delta)$ be defined on $\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{B}_{D_{\mathcal{Y}}}^{n}$ , and let $\mathbf{U}\in\operatorname{O}(m^{\prime},m)$ and $\mathbf{V}\in\operatorname{O}(n^{\prime},n)$ . Then $f_{\mathbf{U},\mathbf{V}}\in\mathcal{F}(\ell,D_{\mathcal{Y}},\Delta)$ . Moreover, for every $\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{m^{\prime}}$ ,

$\left\lVert\nabla\bigl[\Phi_{\mathbf{U},\mathbf{V}}\bigr]_{\frac{1}{2\ell}}(\bm{x})\right\rVert=\left\lVert\nabla\Phi_{\frac{1}{2\ell}}(\mathbf{U}^{\top}\bm{x})\right\rVert.$ (1)

For any $T_{0}\in\mathbb{N}$ and $\mathsf{A}\in\mathcal{A}_{\rm det}$ , there exists a zero-respecting deterministic algorithm $\mathsf{Z}\in\mathcal{A}_{\rm zr}\cap\mathcal{A}_{\rm det}$ such that, for every $D_{\mathcal{Y}}>0$ and every smooth saddle function $f:\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{B}_{D_{\mathcal{Y}}}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}$ , one can find $\mathbf{U}\in\operatorname{O}(m+T_{0},m)$ and $\mathbf{V}\in\operatorname{O}(n+T_{0},n)$ satisfying

$\bigl(\mathsf{Z}_{\bm{x}}^{(t)}[f],\mathsf{Z}_{\bm{y}}^{(t)}[f]\bigr)=\bigl(\mathbf{U}^{\top}\mathsf{A}_{\bm{x}}^{(t)}[f_{\mathbf{U},\mathbf{V}}],\mathbf{V}^{\top}\mathsf{A}_{\bm{y}}^{(t)}[f_{\mathbf{U},\mathbf{V}}]\bigr),\qquad 0\leq t<T_{0}.$ (2)

3 Main Results

We present lower bounds for deterministic first-order methods and a matching upper bound attained by Tracked-FOAM .

3.1 Lower bound results

We first state the zero-respecting first-order lower bound and then its extension to arbitrary deterministic first-order methods. Both results are proved in Section 6.5 .

There are numerical constants $c_{0},c_{1}>0$ such that, for every $\ell,D_{\mathcal{Y}},\Delta,\epsilon>0$ satisfying $\epsilon\leq c_{0}\min\{\ell D_{\mathcal{Y}},\sqrt{\ell\Delta}\},$ there exists an instance $f\in\mathcal{F}(\ell,D_{\mathcal{Y}},\Delta)$ for which

$\mathcal{T}_{\epsilon}\bigl(\mathcal{A}_{\rm zr}\cap\mathcal{A}_{\rm det},\{f\}\bigr)\geq c_{1}\frac{\ell^{2}D_{\mathcal{Y}}\Delta}{\epsilon^{3}}.$

There are numerical constants $c_{0},c_{1}>0$ such that, for every $\ell,D_{\mathcal{Y}},\Delta,\epsilon>0$ satisfying $\epsilon\leq c_{0}\min\{\ell D_{\mathcal{Y}},\sqrt{\ell\Delta}\},$ one has

$\mathcal{T}_{\epsilon}\bigl(\mathcal{A}_{\rm det},\mathcal{F}(\ell,D_{\mathcal{Y}},\Delta)\bigr)\geq c_{1}\frac{\ell^{2}D_{\mathcal{Y}}\Delta}{\epsilon^{3}}.$

Theorem 2 also covers a deterministic point computed from the final transcript but not previously queried. Indeed, append that primal point, paired with the feasible dual origin, as the algorithm’s next query and apply the lower bound for queried points to the augmented deterministic algorithm.

3.2 Matching upper bound

The following theorem gives our matching upper bound. The algorithm Tracked-FOAM is developed in Section 5 .

Let $\ell,D_{\mathcal{Y}},\Delta>0$ and $f\in\mathcal{F}(\ell,D_{\mathcal{Y}},\Delta)$ . For any $\epsilon>0$ , Algorithm 1 , initialized at $\bm{z}^{0}=\mathbf{0}$ , returns an $\epsilon$ -OS point using

$\mathcal{O}\left(\left(\frac{\ell\Delta}{\epsilon^{2}}+1\right)\max\left\{1,\frac{\ell D_{\mathcal{Y}}}{\epsilon}\right\}\right)$ (3)

first-order saddle-oracle calls.

In the regime of Theorem 2 , the upper bound in ( 3 ) simplifies to $\mathcal{O}(\ell^{2}D_{\mathcal{Y}}\Delta/\epsilon^{3})$ . Thus, Tracked-FOAM is optimal among deterministic first-order methods up to universal constants in this regime.

With suitable parameter choices and initialization, Tracked-FOAM also finds an $\epsilon$ -game-stationary point for NC-C problems, as defined in Li et al. (2026, Definition 2.1 (ii)) , with oracle complexity $\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-5/2})$ . This improves the oracle complexity in Li et al. (2026) by removing the multiplicative $\log(1/\epsilon)$ factor.

For NC-SC problems, where $f$ is $\mu$ -strongly concave in $\bm{y}$ , Tracked-FOAM can be configured to find either an $\epsilon$ -OS point or an $\epsilon$ -GS point with oracle complexity $\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{\kappa}\epsilon^{-2})$ , where $\kappa:=\ell/\mu$ . Zhang et al. (2021) removed the polylogarithmic factors in $1/\epsilon$ from the bound of Lin et al. (2020a) . Our extension further removes the remaining logarithmic factors in the problem parameters, matching the lower bounds of Li et al. (2021) ; Zhang et al. (2021) in its dependence on $\epsilon$ and $\kappa$ .

4 The Construction of Our Hard Instance

In this section, we construct the hard instance used to prove Theorem 1 . We first state four properties sufficient for the lower bound and then give an explicit construction.

4.1 Certificates for the hard instance

We begin by providing four properties of the unscaled hard instance sufficient to prove Theorem 1 . Call an integer pair $(M,N)$ admissible if $M\geq 1$ and $N\geq 10$ . For each admissible pair and each $D>0$ , write the primal variable as $\bm{x}=(\bm{s},\bm{\nu})\in\mathbb{R}^{3M}$ , where the state vector $\bm{s}:=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{M})$ and the connector vector $\bm{\nu}:=(a_{1},b_{1},\ldots,a_{M},b_{M})$ . We additionally set $s_{0}\equiv 1$ . Let dual coordinates be $\bm{y}=(\bm{y}^{(1)},\ldots,\bm{y}^{(M)}),$ where $\bm{y}^{(i)}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}$ for $i\in[M]$ .

Define the bounded dual domain $\mathcal{Y}_{D}:=\mathbb{B}_{D}^{MN}$ , and consider $\bar{f}:\mathbb{R}^{3M}\times\mathcal{Y}_{D}\to\mathbb{R}$ with coordinate ordering $\bm{\pi}$ . Set $L:=M(N+3)$ , and denote the corresponding value function by $\bar{\Phi}(\bm{s},\bm{\nu}):=\max_{\bm{y}\in\mathcal{Y}_{D}}\bar{f}(\bm{s},\bm{\nu};\bm{y}).$ We impose four conditions on $(\bar{f},\bm{\pi})$ .

There exist numerical constants $\ell_{0},g_{0},c_{\Delta},c_{D}>0$ , independent of $M,N,D$ , such that, for every admissible pair $(M,N)$ and every $D>0$ , the corresponding unscaled hard instance $\bar{f}$ and coordinate ordering $\bm{\pi}$ satisfy the following properties:

The function $\bar{f}$ is jointly $\ell_{0}$ -smooth and strongly concave in $\bm{y}$ , and its value function $\bar{\Phi}$ is differentiable.

The function $\bar{f}$ is a first-order saddle zero-chain with respect to $\bm{\pi}$ .

The $\pi_{L}$ -th coordinate of $(\bm{x},\bm{y})$ is $s_{M}$ . Moreover, whenever $N\leq c_{D}D$ ,

$s_{M}\leq\frac{1}{5}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\left\lVert\nabla\bar{\Phi}(\bm{s},\bm{\nu})\right\rVert\geq g_{0}.$

The initial gap of the value function satisfies

$\bar{\Phi}(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{0})-\inf_{(\bm{s},\bm{\nu})\in\mathbb{R}^{3M}}\bar{\Phi}(\bm{s},\bm{\nu})\leq c_{\Delta}M.$

Conditions 1 (C2) and (C3) force sequential coordinate discovery and rule out small gradients of the value function before the terminal coordinate is reached, provided $N\leq c_{D}D$ . Conditions 1 (C1) and (C4) allow the instance to be scaled into the target function class.

The bound on the region $s_{M}\leq 1/5$ in (C3) also allows us to transfer the lower bound on the gradient norm of the value function to that of its Moreau envelope. After scaling, a sufficiently small gradient norm of the Moreau envelope at a query point with $s_{M}=0$ would keep the associated proximal point within the corresponding scaled region. The proximal optimality condition would then contradict the gradient lower bound in (C3).

The remainder of this section constructs a pair $(\bar{f},\bm{\pi})$ satisfying these certificates for every admissible pair $(M,N)$ and every $D>0$ .

4.2 Overview of the construction

Fix an admissible pair $(M,N)$ and $D>0$ . Following Li et al. (2021) , we construct $M$ stages, each containing primal variables $(a_{i},b_{i},s_{i})$ and a strongly concave inner chain with dual variable $\bm{y}^{(i)}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}$ . These variables are coupled so that a zero-respecting algorithm must reveal the coordinates of the inner chain before advancing to the next stage. The outer terms are designed to ensure that the gradient of the value function remains bounded away from zero when the final state variable $s_{M}$ is small.

The full dual vector is constrained to a Euclidean ball of diameter $D$ . We therefore also ensure that the unconstrained dual maximizer is feasible at the points used in the argument for the gradient lower bound. At these points, imposing the dual constraint leaves the inner maximum unchanged.

The resulting coordinate order is

$\bm{\pi}=(a_{1},y_{1}^{(1)},\ldots,y_{N}^{(1)},b_{1},s_{1},\ldots,a_{M},y_{1}^{(M)},\ldots,y_{N}^{(M)},b_{M},s_{M}).$ (4)

The saddle zero-chain property proved in Section 6 forces sequential discovery along this order, giving a chain length of $L=M(N+3)$ ; see Figure 1 .

Figure 1 : Schematic of the hard instance. Each stage follows the path $a_{i}\to y_{1}^{(i)}\to\cdots\to y_{N}^{(i)}\to b_{i}\to s_{i}$ .

We next construct the inner chain and then combine it with the outer terms to define the full instance.

4.3 The strongly concave inner chain

We construct an inner chain with primal variables $a,b\in\mathbb{R}$ and dual variable $\bm{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}$ . Its coefficients are chosen so that the inner maximum is $a^{2}-ab+b^{2}$ , independently of $N$ . The construction adapts the regularized quadratic chain of Li et al. (2021, Section 5.1) , which builds on the classical construction of Nesterov (2004) . Define

$\mathbf{A}_{N}:=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}1&-1&&&\\ -1&2&-1&&\\ &-1&\ddots&\ddots&\\ &&\ddots&2&-1\\ &&&-1&1\end{array}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N},\quad\mathbf{M}_{N}:=\frac{1}{N^{2}}\mathbf{I}_{N}+\mathbf{A}_{N},\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathbf{B}_{N}:=\mathbf{M}_{N}^{-1}.$

Since $\mathbf{A}_{N}$ is positive semidefinite, $\mathbf{M}_{N}\succeq N^{-2}\mathbf{I}_{N}\succ 0$ , so $\mathbf{B}_{N}$ is well defined. Set

$k_{N}:=(\mathbf{B}_{N})_{1N},\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad c_{N}:=\frac{(\mathbf{B}_{N})_{11}}{k_{N}}.$

The estimates of Li et al. (2021, Lemma 7) give $k_{N}>0$ , so $c_{N}$ is also well defined.

We define the inner dual chain by

$H(a,b;\bm{w}):=-\frac{1}{2}\bm{w}^{\top}\mathbf{M}_{N}\bm{w}+k_{N}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left\langle a\bm{e}_{1}-b\bm{e}_{N},\bm{w}\right\rangle+\frac{2-c_{N}}{2}(a^{2}+b^{2}).$

The primal variables couple to the two ends of the dual chain: $a$ interacts with $w_{1}$ , and $b$ with $w_{N}$ . Since $\mathbf{M}_{N}\succeq N^{-2}\mathbf{I}_{N}$ , the function $H(a,b;\cdot)$ is $N^{-2}$ -strongly concave and has the unique maximizer

$\bm{w}^{\star}(a,b)=k_{N}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{B}_{N}(a\bm{e}_{1}-b\bm{e}_{N}).$

The normalization by $k_{N}$ and the quadratic correction $\frac{2-c_{N}}{2}(a^{2}+b^{2})$ ensure that

$Q(a,b):=\max_{\bm{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}H(a,b;\bm{w})=a^{2}-ab+b^{2}.$ (5)

The maximizer satisfies

$\left\lVert\bm{w}^{\star}(a,b)\right\rVert\leq c_{\bm{y}}N\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}},$

where $c_{\bm{y}}>0$ is a numerical constant independent of $N$ . This estimate will be used to control the dual maximizers when the chains are combined over a bounded dual domain. Moreover, $H$ has a joint smoothness constant independent of $N$ and is a zero-chain in the order $(a,w_{1},\ldots,w_{N},b)$ . These properties are proved in Lemma 12 in Appendix A.3 .

4.4 The composite NC-C hard instance

We combine $M$ copies of the inner chain using three coupling functions and a state regularizer. The entrance and exit couplings connect each inner chain to the adjacent states, while the state coupling and regularizer control the state configuration at points with small gradients. We use a smooth activation function to switch the couplings according to the adjacent states, and bounded extensions of the identity to keep the couplings globally smooth. We first define these auxiliary functions.

Let

$\displaystyle p(t)$ $\displaystyle:=\begin{cases}0,&t\leq 0,\\[2.0pt] \displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\exp(-1/v)}{\exp(-1/v)+\exp(-1/(1-v))}\,{\rm d}v,&0<t<1,\\[10.0pt] t-\frac{1}{2},&t\geq 1,\end{cases}\quad\text{and}\quad q(t)$ $\displaystyle:=p^{\prime}\!\left(\frac{5t-1}{4}\right).$ (6)

The function $q$ smoothly transitions from $0$ to $1$ , with $q(t)=0$ for $t\leq 1/5$ and $q(t)=1$ for $t\geq 1$ .

We also define two smooth bounded extensions of the identity mapping:

$\displaystyle e_{\bm{s}}(t)$ $\displaystyle:=p(t+3)-p(t-2)-\frac{5}{2},\qquad\mbox{and}$ $\displaystyle e_{\bm{\nu}}(t)$ $\displaystyle:=p(t+22)-p(t-21)-\frac{43}{2}.$

These satisfy $e_{\bm{s}}(t)=t$ for $|t|\leq 2$ and $e_{\bm{\nu}}(t)=t$ for $|t|\leq 21$ , and are constant for sufficiently large positive or negative inputs. The functions $q$ , $e_{\bm{s}}$ , and $e_{\bm{\nu}}$ are infinitely differentiable, with compactly supported first derivatives. Their required bounds are given in Lemmas 14 and 15 in Appendices A.5 and A.6 .

For consecutive state variables $u,v$ and connector variables $a,b$ , define the entrance, exit, and state coupling functions by

$\displaystyle C_{\rm en}(u,a,v):=-4q(u)\bigl(1-q(v)\bigr)e_{\bm{\nu}}(a),$ (7) $\displaystyle C_{\rm ex}(u,b,v):=-q(u)e_{\bm{s}}(v)\bigl(1-q(v)\bigr)e_{\bm{\nu}}(b),\qquad\mbox{and}$ (8) $\displaystyle C_{\rm st}(u,v):=24q(v)\bigl(1-e_{\bm{s}}(u)\bigr)\bigl(1-q(u)\bigr).$ (9)

Set

$\displaystyle c_{R}:=25+\left\lVert\nabla_{u}C_{\rm st}\right\rVert_{\infty}+\left\lVert\nabla_{v}C_{\rm st}\right\rVert_{\infty}+\left\lVert\nabla_{u}C_{\rm en}\right\rVert_{\infty}+\left\lVert\nabla_{v}C_{\rm en}\right\rVert_{\infty}+\left\lVert\nabla_{u}C_{\rm ex}\right\rVert_{\infty}+\left\lVert\nabla_{v}C_{\rm ex}\right\rVert_{\infty},$

which is a numerical constant, and define the state regularizer

$R(t):=\frac{12}{5}p(-10t)-\frac{c_{R}+1}{10}\bigl[p(10t-1)-p(10t-10)\bigr].$ (10)

The outer terms are designed to keep the norm of the connector vector bounded independently of $M$ whenever $s_{M}\leq 1/5$ and the gradient of the value function is sufficiently small. Together with the inner maximizer estimate, this ensures that the unconstrained dual maximizer is feasible when $N\leq c_{D}D$ . We establish these properties in the proof of Condition 1 (C3).

With $s_{0}=1$ , we define the unscaled hard instance on $\mathbb{R}^{3M}\times\mathcal{Y}_{D}$ by

$\displaystyle\bar{f}(\bm{s},\bm{\nu};\bm{y}):=\sum_{i=1}^{M}\Bigl[$ $\displaystyle C_{\rm en}(s_{i-1},a_{i},s_{i})+H(a_{i},b_{i};\bm{y}^{(i)})+C_{\rm ex}(s_{i-1},b_{i},s_{i})+C_{\rm st}(s_{i-1},s_{i})+R(s_{i})\Bigr].$ (11)

Within each stage, the entrance coupling, inner chain, and exit coupling give the coordinate order

$s_{i-1}\xrightarrow{\ C_{\rm en}\ }a_{i}\xrightarrow{\ H\ }y_{1}^{(i)}\xrightarrow{\ H\ }\cdots\xrightarrow{\ H\ }y_{N}^{(i)}\xrightarrow{\ H\ }b_{i}\xrightarrow{\ C_{\rm ex}\ }s_{i}.$

The state coupling and regularizer preserve this order.

For the analysis in Section 6 , we also consider the value function obtained by ignoring the dual ball constraint:

$\bar{\Phi}_{\infty}(\bm{s},\bm{\nu}):=\max_{\bm{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{MN}}\bar{f}(\bm{s},\bm{\nu};\bm{y}),$

where $\bar{f}$ is extended to all $\bm{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{MN}$ by the same formula ( 11 ). By ( 5 ), maximizing each inner chain replaces $H$ by $Q$ , giving

$\displaystyle\bar{\Phi}_{\infty}(\bm{s},\bm{\nu})=\sum_{i=1}^{M}\Bigl[$ $\displaystyle C_{\rm en}(s_{i-1},a_{i},s_{i})+Q(a_{i},b_{i})+C_{\rm ex}(s_{i-1},b_{i},s_{i})+C_{\rm st}(s_{i-1},s_{i})+R(s_{i})\Bigr].$ (12)

The unique unconstrained maximizer is

$\bm{y}^{\star}(\bm{\nu}):=\bigl(\bm{w}^{\star}(a_{1},b_{1}),\ldots,\bm{w}^{\star}(a_{M},b_{M})\bigr).$

Whenever $\left\lVert\bm{y}^{\star}(\bm{\nu})\right\rVert\leq D/2$ , this maximizer belongs to $\mathcal{Y}_{D}$ , so $\bar{\Phi}(\bm{s},\bm{\nu})=\bar{\Phi}_{\infty}(\bm{s},\bm{\nu})$ .

5 A Matching Upper-Bound Algorithm

We now develop Tracked-FOAM , which attains the upper bound in Theorem 3 . Throughout this section, let $f\in\mathcal{F}(\ell,D_{\mathcal{Y}},\Delta)$ with associated feasible sets $\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}$ and $\mathcal{Y}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}$ .

5.1 Regularized proximal subproblems

Following Li et al. (2026) , we add a negative quadratic term in $\bm{y}$ and a proximal term in $\bm{x}$ . Fix $r_{\bm{x}}:=2\ell$ , let $0<r_{\bm{y}}\leq\ell/8$ , and write $r=(r_{\bm{x}},r_{\bm{y}})$ . Define

$\displaystyle f_{r}(\bm{x};\bm{y}):=f(\bm{x};\bm{y})-\frac{r_{\bm{y}}}{2}\left\lVert\bm{y}\right\rVert^{2},\qquad\mbox{and}$ $\displaystyle F_{r}(\bm{x},\bm{z};\bm{y}):=f_{r}(\bm{x};\bm{y})+\frac{r_{\bm{x}}}{2}\left\lVert\bm{x}-\bm{z}\right\rVert^{2}.$

For each $\bm{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}$ , the function $F_{r}(\cdot,\bm{z};\cdot)$ is $\ell$ -strongly convex in $\bm{x}$ and $r_{\bm{y}}$ -strongly concave in $\bm{y}$ . Denote its unique saddle point on $\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}$ by $(\bm{x}_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}^{\star},\bm{y}_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}^{\star})$ .

To relate this subproblem to stationarity, define the regularized value function and its Moreau envelope by

$\displaystyle\Phi_{r}(\bm{x}):=\max_{\bm{y}\in\mathcal{Y}}f_{r}(\bm{x};\bm{y})+\iota_{\mathcal{X}}(\bm{x}),\qquad\mbox{and}$ $\displaystyle p_{r}(\bm{z}):=\min_{\bm{x}}\left\{\Phi_{r}(\bm{x})+\ell\left\lVert\bm{x}-\bm{z}\right\rVert^{2}\right\}=\min_{\bm{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\max_{\bm{y}\in\mathcal{Y}}F_{r}(\bm{x},\bm{z};\bm{y}).$

The function $p_{r}$ is continuously differentiable, with gradient given by ( 13 ) ( Davis and Drusvyatskiy, 2019 , Lemma 2.2) , i.e.,

$\bm{x}_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}^{\star}=\operatorname{prox}_{\frac{1}{2\ell}\Phi_{r}}(\bm{z}),\quad\text{and}\quad\nabla p_{r}(\bm{z})=2\ell(\bm{z}-\bm{x}_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}^{\star}).$ (13)

Moreover, $p_{r}$ is $2\ell$ -smooth ( Bauschke and Combettes, 2011 , Proposition 12.29) .

We next relate $\lVert\nabla p_{r}(\bm{z})\rVert$ to the original OS criterion. The following lemma bounds the difference between $\nabla p_{r}$ and $\nabla\Phi_{1/(2\ell)}$ caused by the dual regularization.

For every $r=(2\ell,r_{\bm{y}})$ with $r_{\bm{y}}>0$ and every $\bm{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}$ ,

$\left\lVert\nabla\Phi_{\frac{1}{2\ell}}(\bm{z})-\nabla p_{r}(\bm{z})\right\rVert\leq D_{\mathcal{Y}}\sqrt{2\ell r_{\bm{y}}}.$

Since $\mathbf{0}\in\mathcal{Y}$ and $\operatorname{diam}(\mathcal{Y})\leq D_{\mathcal{Y}}$ , we have $\lVert\bm{y}\rVert\leq D_{\mathcal{Y}}$ for every $\bm{y}\in\mathcal{Y}$ . Consequently, for every $\bm{x}\in\mathcal{X}$ ,

$0\leq\Phi(\bm{x})-\Phi_{r}(\bm{x})\leq\frac{r_{\bm{y}}}{2}D_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2}.$ (14)

Let $\bm{x}^{\star}:=\operatorname{prox}_{\Phi/(2\ell)}(\bm{z})$ and $\bm{x}_{r}^{\star}:=\operatorname{prox}_{\Phi_{r}/(2\ell)}(\bm{z})$ . Both $\Phi$ and $\Phi_{r}$ are $\ell$ -weakly convex, so $\bm{x}\mapsto\Phi(\bm{x})+\ell\lVert\bm{x}-\bm{z}\rVert^{2}$ and $\bm{x}\mapsto\Phi_{r}(\bm{x})+\ell\lVert\bm{x}-\bm{z}\rVert^{2}$ are $\ell$ -strongly convex. Applying strong convexity to each function at the minimizer of the other and adding the resulting inequalities gives

$\displaystyle\ell\left\lVert\bm{x}^{\star}-\bm{x}_{r}^{\star}\right\rVert^{2}$ $\displaystyle\leq[\Phi(\bm{x}_{r}^{\star})-\Phi_{r}(\bm{x}_{r}^{\star})]-[\Phi(\bm{x}^{\star})-\Phi_{r}(\bm{x}^{\star})]\leq\frac{r_{\bm{y}}}{2}D_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2}.$

Consequently,

$\left\lVert\nabla\Phi_{\frac{1}{2\ell}}(\bm{z})-\nabla p_{r}(\bm{z})\right\rVert=2\ell\left\lVert\bm{x}^{\star}-\bm{x}_{r}^{\star}\right\rVert\leq D_{\mathcal{Y}}\sqrt{2\ell r_{\bm{y}}}.$

We finish the proof. ∎

For a target accuracy $\epsilon>0$ , set

$r_{\bm{y},\epsilon}:=\min\left\{\frac{\ell}{8},\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{8\ell D_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2}}\right\}.$ (15)

If $0<r_{\bm{y}}\leq r_{\bm{y},\epsilon}$ , Lemma 3 bounds the perturbation error by $\epsilon/2$ . It therefore suffices to find $\bm{z}\in\mathcal{X}$ satisfying $\lVert\nabla p_{r}(\bm{z})\rVert\leq\epsilon/2$ .

5.2 Approximate proximal points from FOAM

To find a point with small $\lVert\nabla p_{r}\rVert$ , we approximately implement gradient descent on $p_{r}$ . By ( 13 ), an exact gradient step with stepsize $\frac{1}{2\ell}$ satisfies $\bm{z}-\frac{1}{2\ell}\nabla p_{r}(\bm{z})=\bm{x}_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}^{\star}.$ Thus each step amounts to solving the SC-SC subproblem

$\min_{\bm{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\max_{\bm{y}\in\mathcal{Y}}F_{r}(\bm{x},\bm{z};\bm{y}).$

We approximately solve this subproblem using the accelerated FOAM method of Kovalev and Gasnikov (2022) , with $\mu_{x}=\ell$ and $\mu_{y}=r_{\bm{y}}$ . Each call provides a primal approximation and an updated solver state, which we retain for the next outer iteration.

For fixed $(\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}})$ , FOAM uses a conjugate formulation involving an auxiliary variable $\bm{\omega}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}$ and the original dual variable $\bm{y}\in\mathcal{Y}$ . To describe its iterates and their errors, we first define the associated conjugate objective

$\displaystyle P_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}(\bm{\omega},\bm{y})$ $\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2\ell}\left\lVert\bm{\omega}\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{r_{\bm{y}}}{2}\left\lVert\bm{y}\right\rVert^{2}+\iota_{\mathcal{Y}}(\bm{y})+\sup_{\bm{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\left\{\left\langle\bm{\omega},\bm{x}\right\rangle-f(\bm{x};\bm{y})-\ell\left\lVert\bm{x}-\bm{z}\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{\ell}{2}\left\lVert\bm{x}\right\rVert^{2}\right\}.$

Let $(\bm{\omega}_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}^{\star},\bm{y}_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}^{\star})$ denote its unique minimizer, and write $P_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}^{\star}:=\min_{\bm{\omega},\bm{y}}P_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}(\bm{\omega},\bm{y})$ . The conjugate and primal solutions satisfy $\bm{\omega}_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}^{\star}=-\ell\bm{x}_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}^{\star}.$

The accelerated recursion maintains two pairs of iterates for this conjugate problem: a slow pair $(\bm{\omega},\bm{y})$ and a fast pair $(\bm{\omega}_{f},\bm{y}_{f})$ . Both pairs target the minimizer $(\bm{\omega}_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}^{\star},\bm{y}_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}^{\star})$ and are retained in the state $S=(\bm{\omega},\bm{y},\bm{\omega}_{f},\bm{y}_{f}).$ The slow dual variable $\bm{y}$ need not belong to $\mathcal{Y}$ . The fast pair has finite value under $P_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}$ , so $\bm{y}_{f}\in\mathcal{Y}$ . All calls to the first-order saddle oracle are made at feasible points in $\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}$ ; see Appendix B.1 .

The fast pair provides the primal approximation

$\bm{z}^{+}:=\proj_{\mathcal{X}}(-\bm{\omega}_{f}/\ell).$

We use a Lyapunov function to measure the error in both pairs and bound the distance from $\bm{z}^{+}$ to $\bm{x}_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}^{\star}$ . Set $\alpha_{r_{\bm{y}}}:=\sqrt{8r_{\bm{y}}/\ell}\leq 1$ . We use $\alpha_{r_{\bm{y}}}$ times the Lyapunov function in Kovalev and Gasnikov (2022, Supplement, Lemma 7) , specialized to $F_{r}(\cdot,\bm{z};\cdot)$ :

$\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}(S)$ $\displaystyle:=\frac{2\alpha_{r_{\bm{y}}}}{\ell}\left\lVert\bm{\omega}-\bm{\omega}_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}^{\star}\right\rVert^{2}+2r_{\bm{y}}\left\lVert\bm{y}-\bm{y}_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}^{\star}\right\rVert^{2}+2\left(P_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}(\bm{\omega}_{f},\bm{y}_{f})-P_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}^{\star}\right).$ (16)

The first two terms measure the error of the slow pair $(\bm{\omega},\bm{y})$ , while the last term measures the suboptimality of the fast pair $(\bm{\omega}_{f},\bm{y}_{f})$ in the conjugate objective.

For fixed $(\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}})$ , a call to FOAM takes an initial state $S$ with $\mathcal{L}_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}(S)<\infty$ and a target error reduction factor $\rho\in(0,1)$ . It runs a prescribed number of iterations and returns

$S^{+}=(\bm{\omega}^{+},\bm{y}^{+},\bm{\omega}_{f}^{+},\bm{y}_{f}^{+}):=\textnormal{{FOAM}}_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}(S;\rho).$

The corresponding primal output is $\proj_{\mathcal{X}}(-\bm{\omega}_{f}^{+}/\ell)$ . The iteration count is chosen to reduce $\mathcal{L}_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}$ by a factor of $\rho$ . The following lemma gives the primal approximation error, the reduction in the Lyapunov function, and the oracle cost.

For every $\bm{z}\in\mathcal{X}$ , $0<r_{\bm{y}}\leq\ell/8$ , and every state $S$ with $\mathcal{L}_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}(S)<\infty$ ,

$\ell\left\lVert\proj_{\mathcal{X}}\left(-\frac{\bm{\omega}_{f}}{\ell}\right)-\bm{x}_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}^{\star}\right\rVert^{2}\leq\mathcal{L}_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}(S).$ (17)

Moreover, for every $\rho\in(0,1)$ ,

$\mathcal{L}_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}\left(\textnormal{{FOAM}}_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}(S;\rho)\right)\leq\rho\,\mathcal{L}_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}(S),$ (18)

and the call requires $\mathcal{O}(1+\sqrt{\ell/r_{\bm{y}}}\log(1/\rho))$ first-order saddle-oracle calls.

The proof of Lemma 4 , the iteration count, and the implementation using feasible oracle queries are given in Appendix B . The call does not require evaluating $\mathcal{L}_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}$ , which depends on the unknown minimizer and optimal value of $P_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}$ . For a fixed numerical $\rho$ , reducing the error by this factor requires $\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{\ell/r_{\bm{y}}})$ oracle calls.

These guarantees apply to a fixed subproblem. In the next subsection, we show how to update the state when $\bm{z}$ changes and control its error for the new subproblem.

5.3 Tracking across successive subproblems

Each outer iteration changes the proximal center and hence the SC-SC subproblem. To reuse the previous FOAM state, we first examine how this change affects the subproblem.

Fix $0<r_{\bm{y}}\leq\ell/8$ in this subsection and consider two proximal centers $\bm{z},\bm{z}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}$ . Write $\bm{d}:=\bm{z}^{\prime}-\bm{z}$ for their difference. Expanding the proximal term gives

$F_{r}(\bm{x},\bm{z}^{\prime};\bm{y})=F_{r}(\bm{x},\bm{z};\bm{y})-2\ell\left\langle\bm{d},\bm{x}\right\rangle+\ell\bigl(\left\lVert\bm{z}^{\prime}\right\rVert^{2}-\left\lVert\bm{z}\right\rVert^{2}\bigr).$ (19)

The last term is independent of $(\bm{x},\bm{y})$ . In the supremum defining $P_{\bm{z}^{\prime},r_{\bm{y}}}$ , the resulting linear term is canceled by replacing $\bm{\omega}$ with $\bm{\omega}-2\ell\bm{d}$ . We therefore translate both conjugate variables in the retained state:

$\widetilde{S}:=\left(\bm{\omega}-2\ell\bm{d},\,\bm{y},\,\bm{\omega}_{f}-2\ell\bm{d},\,\bm{y}_{f}\right).$ (20)

The following proposition bounds the error of this state for the new subproblem.

For every $\bm{z},\bm{z}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}$ with $\bm{z}^{\prime}=\bm{z}+\bm{d}$ and every state $S$ with $\mathcal{L}_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}(S)<\infty$ , the translated state in ( 20 ) satisfies

$\mathcal{L}_{\bm{z}^{\prime},r_{\bm{y}}}(\widetilde{S})\leq 2\mathcal{L}_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}(S)+24\ell\left\lVert\bm{d}\right\rVert^{2}.$ (21)

Consequently, for every $\rho\in(0,1)$ ,

$\mathcal{L}_{\bm{z}^{\prime},r_{\bm{y}}}\left(\textnormal{{FOAM}}_{\bm{z}^{\prime},r_{\bm{y}}}(\widetilde{S};\rho)\right)\leq\rho\left(2\mathcal{L}_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}(S)+24\ell\left\lVert\bm{d}\right\rVert^{2}\right).$ (22)

The proof of Proposition 1 is given in Section 7.1 . This estimate allows us to bound the error for the new subproblem using the previous error bound and the displacement $\bm{d}$ . We use it to maintain a computable upper bound $B^{t}$ satisfying $\mathcal{L}_{\bm{z}^{t},r_{\bm{y}}}(S^{t})\leq B^{t}.$

Suppose that this bound holds at iteration $t$ , and write $S^{t}=(\bm{\omega}^{t},\bm{y}^{t},\bm{\omega}_{f}^{t},\bm{y}_{f}^{t})$ . We first take the approximate proximal step provided by $S^{t}$ :

$\bm{z}^{t+1}:=\proj_{\mathcal{X}}(-\bm{\omega}_{f}^{t}/\ell),\quad\text{and}\quad\bm{d}^{t}:=\bm{z}^{t+1}-\bm{z}^{t}.$

To obtain an initial state for the subproblem at $\bm{z}^{t+1}$ , we translate $S^{t}$ as in ( 20 ):

$\widetilde{S}^{t}:=\left(\bm{\omega}^{t}-2\ell\bm{d}^{t},\,\bm{y}^{t},\,\bm{\omega}_{f}^{t}-2\ell\bm{d}^{t},\,\bm{y}_{f}^{t}\right).$

Starting from $\widetilde{S}^{t}$ , we run the prescribed number of FOAM iterations to reduce the Lyapunov function for this subproblem by a factor of $1/400$ , and denote the returned state by $S^{t+1}:=\textnormal{{FOAM}}_{\bm{z}^{t+1},r_{\bm{y}}}(\widetilde{S}^{t};{1}/{400}).$ We update this computable error bound accordingly:

$B^{t+1}:=\frac{1}{400}\left(2B^{t}+24\ell\left\lVert\bm{d}^{t}\right\rVert^{2}\right).$ (23)

Proposition 1 then gives $\mathcal{L}_{\bm{z}^{t+1},r_{\bm{y}}}(S^{t+1})\leq B^{t+1}.$ Thus the same error bound is maintained at the next iteration, using only $B^{t}$ and the computed displacement $\bm{d}^{t}$ .

For the fixed reduction factor $1/400$ , Lemma 4 gives an oracle cost of $\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{\ell/r_{\bm{y}}})$ per outer iteration. The Lyapunov descent estimate in Section 7 justifies this choice of reduction factor.

5.4 The Tracked-FOAM algorithm

We now explain why the updates in Section 5.3 suffice to find an $\epsilon$ -OS point. The primal error bound in Lemma 4 and the bound maintained in Section 5.3 give

$\ell\left\lVert\bm{z}^{t+1}-\bm{x}_{\bm{z}^{t},r_{\bm{y}}}^{\star}\right\rVert^{2}\leq\mathcal{L}_{\bm{z}^{t},r_{\bm{y}}}(S^{t})\leq B^{t}.$

Using the $2\ell$ -smoothness of $p_{r}$ , we therefore obtain

$\displaystyle p_{r}(\bm{z}^{t+1})-p_{r}(\bm{z}^{t})$ $\displaystyle\leq-\frac{\left\lVert\nabla p_{r}(\bm{z}^{t})\right\rVert^{2}}{4\ell}+\ell\left\lVert\bm{z}^{t+1}-\bm{x}_{\bm{z}^{t},r_{\bm{y}}}^{\star}\right\rVert^{2}$ $\displaystyle\leq-\frac{\left\lVert\nabla p_{r}(\bm{z}^{t})\right\rVert^{2}}{4\ell}+B^{t}.$ (24)

The negative term is the decrease from an exact gradient step, while $B^{t}$ bounds the additional error from approximately solving the subproblem. To control its accumulated contribution, we combine this inequality with the update of $B^{t}$ in ( 23 ) and define

$W^{t}:=p_{r}(\bm{z}^{t})+2B^{t}.$ (25)

With the reduction factor $1/400$ used in the FOAM calls, combining ( 24 ) with ( 23 ) yields

$W^{t+1}-W^{t}\leq-\frac{3}{4}\left(\frac{\left\lVert\nabla p_{r}(\bm{z}^{t})\right\rVert^{2}}{4\ell}+B^{t}\right).$

This estimate controls both the gradient norm of $p_{r}$ and the error from approximately solving the subproblems. Thus reducing the FOAM Lyapunov function by a factor of $1/400$ at each outer iteration suffices to obtain a small gradient norm. By Lemma 4 , each call costs $\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{\ell/r_{\bm{y}}})$ oracle queries. The proof of the descent estimate is given in Lemma 10 .

The iteration bound depends on the initial Lyapunov gap $W^{0}-\inf p_{r}$ , which includes the initial error bound $2B^{0}$ . We therefore initialize FOAM with an error bound $B^{0}$ of order $\Delta+r_{\bm{y}}D_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2}$ . Keeping $\bm{z}^{0}=\mathbf{0}$ fixed, we start with $r_{\bm{y}}=\ell/8$ and successively divide $r_{\bm{y}}$ by four. After each decrease, we run FOAM from the state obtained at the preceding level. We stop at the first level satisfying $r_{\bm{y}}\leq r_{\bm{y},\epsilon}$ , where $r_{\bm{y},\epsilon}$ is defined in ( 15 ). The following proposition bounds the error of the resulting state and the total initialization cost.

Let $\bm{z}^{0}=\mathbf{0}$ . The initialization constructs a dual regularization parameter $r_{\bm{y}}$ and a FOAM state $S^{0}$ satisfying

$\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\bm{z}^{0},r_{\bm{y}}}(S^{0})\leq 15\left(\Delta+r_{\bm{y}}D_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2}\right)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\frac{r_{\bm{y},\epsilon}}{4}<r_{\bm{y}}\leq r_{\bm{y},\epsilon}.$ (26)

The construction requires $\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{\ell/r_{\bm{y}}})$ first-order saddle-oracle calls.

We set $B^{0}:=15\left(\Delta+r_{\bm{y}}D_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2}\right).$ Proposition 2 ensures that $\mathcal{L}_{\bm{z}^{0},r_{\bm{y}}}(S^{0})\leq B^{0}$ , providing the initial error bound required by Section 5.3 . We then keep $r_{\bm{y}}$ fixed and apply the updates described there. The full initialization and its proof are given in Section 7.2 . The cost bounds for the successive initialization levels grow geometrically, so their sum is dominated by the final level. Hence initialization introduces no additional logarithmic factor.

To select the output, we use

$Q^{t}:=\ell\left\lVert\bm{z}^{t+1}-\bm{z}^{t}\right\rVert^{2}+B^{t}.$

The first term measures the computed step, while the second accounts for the error in approximating the exact proximal point. Lemma 11 shows that

$\left\lVert\nabla p_{r}(\bm{z}^{t})\right\rVert^{2}\leq 8\ell Q^{t},\quad\text{and}\quad\min_{0\leq t<T}Q^{t}\leq\frac{4}{T}\left(W^{0}-\inf p_{r}\right).$

Thus a small value of $Q^{t}$ certifies a small gradient norm, and the descent estimate guarantees that such an iterate exists. We return the iterate with the smallest value of $Q^{t}$ , choosing the smallest index in case of a tie. Algorithm 1 gives the complete method.

6 Proof of the Lower Bounds

We verify Conditions 1 (C1)-(C4) for the instance constructed in Section 4 . We then scale the instance and prove Theorems 1 and 2 .

6.1 Smoothness and dual concavity

We begin with the properties required by Condition 1 (C1).

There is a numerical constant $\ell_{0}>0$ , independent of $M,N,D$ , such that $\bar{f}$ is jointly $\ell_{0}$ -smooth and $N^{-2}$ -strongly concave in $\bm{y}$ . For every $D>0$ , the value function $\bar{\Phi}$ is differentiable. Moreover, whenever $\lVert\bm{y}^{\star}(\bm{\nu})\rVert\leq D/2$ ,

$\bar{\Phi}(\bm{s},\bm{\nu})=\bar{\Phi}_{\infty}(\bm{s},\bm{\nu}),\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\nabla\bar{\Phi}(\bm{s},\bm{\nu})=\nabla\bar{\Phi}_{\infty}(\bm{s},\bm{\nu}).$ (27)

Thus Condition 1 (C1) holds.

For each $i\in[M]$ , let $B_{i}$ denote the $i$ -th summand in ( 11 ). Lemma 13 shows that the first two derivatives of $p$ are uniformly bounded. Lemmas 14 and 15 show that $q$ , $e_{\bm{s}}$ , and $e_{\bm{\nu}}$ , together with their first two derivatives, are uniformly bounded by numerical constants. Applying the product and chain rules to ( 7 )-( 9 ) and ( 10 ) therefore gives uniform Hessian bounds for $C_{\rm en}$ , $C_{\rm ex}$ , $C_{\rm st}$ , and $R$ . Lemma 12 (ii) also gives a uniform Hessian bound for $H$ . Thus each $B_{i}$ is jointly $\ell_{B}$ -smooth for a numerical constant $\ell_{B}>0$ independent of $M,N,D$ .

Since $B_{i}$ and $B_{j}$ depend on disjoint coordinates whenever $|i-j|\geq 2$ , the sum over odd indices and the sum over even indices each have a block diagonal Hessian with norm at most $\ell_{B}$ . Their sum $\bar{f}$ is therefore jointly $\ell_{0}$ -smooth with $\ell_{0}:=2\ell_{B}$ .

Only the inner chains depend on $\bm{y}$ . By Lemma 12 (ii), each is $N^{-2}$ -strongly concave in its own dual coordinates. Since these coordinates are disjoint, $\bar{f}$ is $N^{-2}$ -strongly concave in $\bm{y}$ . Compactness of $\mathcal{Y}_{D}$ and strong concavity give a unique maximizer, so Danskin’s theorem implies that $\bar{\Phi}$ is differentiable.

Finally, if $\lVert\bm{y}^{\star}(\bm{\nu})\rVert\leq D/2$ , then the unconstrained maximizer is feasible and hence also maximizes over $\mathcal{Y}_{D}$ . The two value functions therefore have the same value and the same maximizer at this point. Their Danskin gradient formulas give ( 27 ). We finish the proof. ∎

6.2 Saddle zero-chain

We next verify that the construction enforces the coordinate order in ( 4 ).

The objective $\bar{f}$ is a first-order saddle zero-chain with respect to the ordering $\bm{\pi}$ in ( 4 ). Thus Condition 1 (C2) holds.

Equations ( 60 ) and ( 10 ) give $R^{\prime}(0)=0$ . Lemmas 14 and 15 , together with ( 7 )-( 9 ), give, for every $u,a,b\in\mathbb{R}$ ,

$\displaystyle\nabla_{(u,v)}C_{\rm st}(u,0)=(0,0),\qquad\nabla_{(u,a,v)}C_{\rm en}(u,0,0)=(0,-4q(u),0),$ (28) $\displaystyle\nabla_{v}C_{\rm en}(u,a,0)=0,\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\nabla_{(u,b,v)}C_{\rm ex}(u,b,0)=(0,0,-q(u)e_{\bm{\nu}}(b)).$

Fix $k\in\{0,\ldots,L-1\}$ and a point whose support is contained in the first $k$ coordinates of $\bm{\pi}$ . Let $i$ be the stage containing the $(k+1)$ -st coordinate. Since $s_{i}$ lies after the first $k$ coordinates, $s_{i}=0$ . Every later stage therefore has all its variables equal to zero, so its gradient vanishes by $R^{\prime}(0)=0$ , ( 28 ), and $\nabla H(0,0;\mathbf{0})=\mathbf{0}$ . Earlier stages depend only on the first $k$ coordinates. It therefore suffices to consider stage $i$ .

If the $(k+1)$ -st coordinate is $a_{i}$ , then $a_{i},\bm{y}^{(i)},b_{i},s_{i}$ all vanish. By ( 28 ), the only gradient component beyond the first $k$ coordinates that may be nonzero is $\nabla_{a_{i}}\bar{f}=-4q(s_{i-1})$ . This also covers $k=0$ , where $i=1$ and $s_{0}=1$ .

If the $(k+1)$ -st coordinate is one of $y_{1}^{(i)},\ldots,y_{N}^{(i)},b_{i}$ , then $a_{i}$ is among the first $k$ coordinates, while $b_{i}=s_{i}=0$ . Equation ( 28 ) shows that the outer terms have no gradient components beyond the first $k$ coordinates. By Lemma 12 (iii), the inner chain can add only the $(k+1)$ -st coordinate to the gradient support.

Finally, if the $(k+1)$ -st coordinate is $s_{i}$ , then all other coordinates of stage $i$ are among the first $k$ coordinates. By ( 28 ), only $C_{\rm ex}$ can contribute to the derivative with respect to $s_{i}$ , giving $\nabla_{s_{i}}\bar{f}=-q(s_{i-1})e_{\bm{\nu}}(b_{i})$ .

In every case, the gradient support is contained in the first $k+1$ coordinates of $\bm{\pi}$ . This proves the support implication in Definition 7 . ∎

6.3 Gradient lower bound

We now verify Condition 1 (C3). Assuming that $s_{M}\leq 1/5$ and the gradient of the value function is small, we establish the state structure and bound the connector vector. These bounds make the unconstrained maximizer feasible when $N\leq c_{D}D$ , allowing us to use ( 12 ) to derive a contradiction.

Since the outer terms are independent of $\bm{y}$ , the maximization defining $\bar{\Phi}$ only acts on the inner blocks. We therefore define the constrained inner value function

$V_{D}(\bm{\nu}):=\max_{\bm{y}\in\mathcal{Y}_{D}}\sum_{i=1}^{M}H(a_{i},b_{i};\bm{y}^{(i)}).$

With this notation, $\bar{\Phi}$ is the sum of $V_{D}$ and the outer terms, and $V_{D}$ is independent of the state vector $\bm{s}$ .

For every $D>0$ and every primal point, the following statements hold:

For every $i\in[M]$ , if $s_{i}\leq-1/10$ , then $\nabla_{s_{i}}\bar{\Phi}\leq-1$ .

For every $i\in[M]$ , if $1/5<s_{i}<1$ , then $\nabla_{s_{i}}\bar{\Phi}\leq-1$ .

For every $i\in\{2,\ldots,M\}$ , if $-1/10<s_{i-1}\leq 1/5$ and $s_{i}\geq 1$ , then $\nabla_{s_{i-1}}\bar{\Phi}\leq-1$ .

Since $V_{D}$ is independent of $\bm{s}$ , only $R,C_{\rm st},C_{\rm en},C_{\rm ex}$ contribute to the state derivatives. Each $s_{i}$ appears in stage $i$ and, if $i<M$ , stage $i+1$ . Thus the definition of $c_{R}$ gives

$\bigl|\nabla_{s_{i}}\bar{\Phi}-R^{\prime}(s_{i})\bigr|\leq c_{R},\qquad i\in[M].$ (29)

By Lemma 13 , $p^{\prime}$ is nonnegative and nondecreasing. Differentiating ( 10 ) and using the constant branches in ( 60 ) gives $R^{\prime}\leq 0$ . In particular,

$\displaystyle R^{\prime}(t)=-24\quad\left(t\leq-\frac{1}{10}\right),\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad R^{\prime}(t)=-(c_{R}+1)\quad\left(\frac{1}{5}\leq t\leq 1\right).$ (30)

We record the coupling derivatives needed below. If $v\leq 1/5$ , differentiating ( 7 )-( 9 ) and applying Lemmas 14 and 15 gives

$\nabla_{v}C_{\rm st}=\nabla_{v}C_{\rm en}=0,\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\nabla_{v}C_{\rm ex}=-q(u)e_{\bm{s}}^{\prime}(v)e_{\bm{\nu}}(b)\leq\frac{43}{2}<22.$ (31)

Similarly, if $u\leq 1/5$ ,

$\nabla_{u}C_{\rm en}=\nabla_{u}C_{\rm ex}=0,\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\nabla_{u}C_{\rm st}=-24q(v)e_{\bm{s}}^{\prime}(u)\leq 0.$ (32)

For (i), suppose $s_{i}\leq-1/10$ . By ( 31 ), the coupling terms in stage $i$ contribute at most $22$ to the derivative with respect to $s_{i}$ . If $i<M$ , ( 32 ) shows that those in stage $i+1$ contribute $-24q(s_{i+1})e_{\bm{s}}^{\prime}(s_{i})\leq 0$ . Since ( 30 ) gives $R^{\prime}(s_{i})=-24$ , we obtain $\nabla_{s_{i}}\bar{\Phi}\leq-24+22=-2$ .

For (ii), suppose $1/5<s_{i}<1$ . Equation ( 30 ) gives $R^{\prime}(s_{i})=-(c_{R}+1)$ , while ( 29 ) bounds the total coupling contribution by $c_{R}$ . Thus $\nabla_{s_{i}}\bar{\Phi}\leq-(c_{R}+1)+c_{R}=-1$ .

For (iii), suppose $-1/10<s_{i-1}\leq 1/5$ and $s_{i}\geq 1$ . Since $q(s_{i})=e_{\bm{s}}^{\prime}(s_{i-1})=1$ , ( 32 ) gives $\nabla_{u}C_{\rm st}=-24$ and $\nabla_{u}C_{\rm en}=\nabla_{u}C_{\rm ex}=0$ . Thus the coupling terms in stage $i$ contribute exactly $-24$ to the derivative with respect to $s_{i-1}$ . By ( 31 ), those in stage $i-1$ contribute at most $22$ . Together with $R^{\prime}(s_{i-1})\leq 0$ , this gives $\nabla_{s_{i-1}}\bar{\Phi}\leq-24+22=-2$ . We finish the proof. ∎

Set $\tau_{0}:=1/4$ . We call a state coordinate low if it lies in $(-1/10,1/5]$ and high if it lies in $[1,\infty)$ . We next use the derivative bounds to identify the frontier between high and low coordinates and to bound the connector vector.

For every admissible pair $(M,N)$ , every $D>0$ , and every $(\bm{s},\bm{\nu})\in\mathbb{R}^{3M}$ , if

$s_{M}\leq\frac{1}{5},\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\left\lVert\nabla\bar{\Phi}(\bm{s},\bm{\nu})\right\rVert\leq\tau_{0},$

then there is a unique $j\in[M]$ satisfying

$s_{i}\geq 1\quad(0\leq i<j),\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad-\frac{1}{10}<s_{i}\leq\frac{1}{5}\quad(j\leq i\leq M).$ (33)

Equivalently, the state coordinates have the form

$\underbrace{s_{0},\ldots,s_{j-1}}_{\mathrm{high}}\ \big|\ \underbrace{s_{j},\ldots,s_{M}}_{\mathrm{low}}.$

Moreover, $\lVert\bm{\nu}\rVert\leq 20$ .

Lemma 5 and $\tau_{0}<1$ imply that every state coordinate is either low or high, and that no low coordinate is followed by a high one. Since $s_{0}=1$ is high and $s_{M}$ is low, the first low index is the unique $j$ satisfying ( 33 ).

We now bound $\bm{\nu}$ . The connector derivatives of $C_{\rm en}$ and $C_{\rm ex}$ contain the factor $q(s_{i-1})(1-q(s_{i}))$ . By ( 33 ) and Lemma 14 , this factor vanishes unless $i=j$ . Thus only stage $j$ contributes to the gradient of the outer couplings with respect to $\bm{\nu}$ . Lemmas 14 and 15 , together with ( 7 )-( 8 ), give

$|\nabla_{a}C_{\rm en}(u,a,v)|\leq 4,\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad|\nabla_{b}C_{\rm ex}(u,b,v)|\leq\frac{5}{2}.$

This gradient therefore has norm at most $4+5/2<7$ . The bound is independent of $M$ , since only stage $j$ contributes.

Since $R$ and $C_{\rm st}$ are independent of $\bm{\nu}$ , the preceding bound gives

$\left\lVert\nabla_{\bm{\nu}}\bar{\Phi}-\nabla V_{D}(\bm{\nu})\right\rVert\leq 7.$ (34)

Lemma 12 (iv) gives

$\left\langle\bm{\nu},\nabla V_{D}(\bm{\nu})\right\rangle\geq\frac{2}{5}\left\lVert\bm{\nu}\right\rVert^{2}.$ (35)

This estimate holds even when the dual constraint is active. Combining ( 34 ) and ( 35 ) with Cauchy-Schwarz and the assumption that the gradient of the value function is small yields

$\frac{2}{5}\left\lVert\bm{\nu}\right\rVert^{2}-7\left\lVert\bm{\nu}\right\rVert\leq\left\langle\bm{\nu},\nabla_{\bm{\nu}}\bar{\Phi}\right\rangle\leq\tau_{0}\left\lVert\bm{\nu}\right\rVert.$

If $\bm{\nu}=\mathbf{0}$ , the claim is immediate. Otherwise, dividing by $\lVert\bm{\nu}\rVert$ gives $\lVert\bm{\nu}\rVert\leq(5/2)(7+\tau_{0})<20$ . We finish the proof. ∎

We now combine the frontier and connector bounds with the inner maximizer estimate to verify Condition 1 (C3).

There are numerical constants $g_{0},c_{D}>0$ , independent of $M,N,D$ , such that, for every admissible pair $(M,N)$ , every $D>0$ , and every $(\bm{s},\bm{\nu})\in\mathbb{R}^{3M}$ ,

$N\leq c_{D}D,\qquad s_{M}\leq\frac{1}{5}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\left\lVert\nabla\bar{\Phi}(\bm{s},\bm{\nu})\right\rVert\geq g_{0}.$

Since $s_{M}$ is the final coordinate in ( 4 ), this verifies Condition 1 (C3) .

Set $c_{D}:=(40c_{\bm{y}})^{-1}$ . Fix an admissible pair $(M,N)$ , $D>0$ , and a primal point satisfying $N\leq c_{D}D$ and $s_{M}\leq 1/5$ . Suppose, toward a contradiction, that $\lVert\nabla\bar{\Phi}(\bm{s},\bm{\nu})\rVert\leq\tau_{0}$ .

Lemma 6 gives a frontier index $j$ satisfying ( 33 ) and the bound $\lVert\bm{\nu}\rVert\leq 20$ . Summing the squared bounds for the inner chains in Lemma 12 (i) and using $N\leq c_{D}D$ gives

$\left\lVert\bm{y}^{\star}(\bm{\nu})\right\rVert\leq c_{\bm{y}}N\left\lVert\bm{\nu}\right\rVert\leq 20c_{\bm{y}}N\leq\frac{D}{2}.$

Thus the unconstrained maximizer is feasible, and ( 27 ) gives

$\nabla\bar{\Phi}(\bm{s},\bm{\nu})=\nabla\bar{\Phi}_{\infty}(\bm{s},\bm{\nu}).$

At stage $j$ , Lemmas 14 and 15 , together with ( 33 ) and $\lVert\bm{\nu}\rVert\leq 20$ , give

$\displaystyle q(s_{j-1})=1,\qquad q(s_{j})=q^{\prime}(s_{j})=0,$ (36) $\displaystyle e_{\bm{s}}(s_{j})=s_{j},\qquad e_{\bm{s}}^{\prime}(s_{j})=1,$ $\displaystyle e_{\bm{\nu}}(a_{j})=a_{j},\qquad e_{\bm{\nu}}(b_{j})=b_{j},\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad e_{\bm{\nu}}^{\prime}(a_{j})=e_{\bm{\nu}}^{\prime}(b_{j})=1.$

Define $g_{a}:=\nabla_{a_{j}}\bar{\Phi}(\bm{s},\bm{\nu})$ and $g_{b}:=\nabla_{b_{j}}\bar{\Phi}(\bm{s},\bm{\nu})$ . Combining ( 36 ), ( 5 ), and ( 12 ) gives

$g_{a}=2a_{j}-b_{j}-4,\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad g_{b}=-a_{j}+2b_{j}-s_{j}.$

Since $s_{j}>-1/10$ and $|g_{a}|,|g_{b}|\leq\tau_{0}$ , we obtain

$b_{j}=\frac{4+2s_{j}+g_{a}+2g_{b}}{3}>\frac{4-\frac{1}{5}-3\tau_{0}}{3}=\frac{61}{60}>1.$

It remains to examine the derivative with respect to $s_{j}$ . In stage $j$ , ( 31 ) and ( 36 ) give zero contributions from $C_{\rm st},C_{\rm en}$ and a contribution of $-b_{j}$ from $C_{\rm ex}$ . If $j<M$ , then $q(s_{j+1})=0$ by ( 33 ), so ( 32 ) shows that the coupling terms in stage $j+1$ contribute zero. Since $Q$ is independent of $\bm{s}$ , we obtain

$\nabla_{s_{j}}\bar{\Phi}=R^{\prime}(s_{j})-b_{j}\leq-b_{j}<-1,$

where we use $R^{\prime}(s_{j})\leq 0$ and $b_{j}>1$ . Hence $\lVert\nabla\bar{\Phi}(\bm{s},\bm{\nu})\rVert>1>\tau_{0}$ , a contradiction. Taking $g_{0}:=\tau_{0}$ proves the claim. ∎

6.4 Initial gap

We now verify Condition 1 (C4) by bounding the initial gap.

There is a numerical constant $c_{\Delta}>0$ , independent of $M,N,D$ , such that, for every admissible pair $(M,N)$ and every $D>0$ ,

$\bar{\Phi}(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{0})-\inf_{(\bm{s},\bm{\nu})\in\mathbb{R}^{3M}}\bar{\Phi}(\bm{s},\bm{\nu})\leq c_{\Delta}M.$

This verifies Condition 1 (C4) .

At the primal origin, $R$ and all three outer couplings vanish, including at the first stage with $s_{0}=1$ . Lemma 12 (iii) gives $H(0,0;\bm{w})\leq 0$ , with equality at $\bm{w}=\mathbf{0}$ . Since the dual origin is feasible, $\bar{\Phi}(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{0})=0$ .

For any primal point, feasibility of $\bm{y}=\mathbf{0}$ gives $\bar{\Phi}(\bm{s},\bm{\nu})\geq\bar{f}(\bm{s},\bm{\nu};\mathbf{0})$ . At this dual point, Lemma 12 (iii) gives $H(a_{i},b_{i};\mathbf{0})\geq 0$ for every stage. Lemmas 14 and 15 also give $0\leq q\leq 1$ , $|e_{\bm{s}}|\leq 5/2$ , and $|e_{\bm{\nu}}|\leq 43/2$ .

Moreover, $C_{\rm st}(u,v)\geq 0$ : it vanishes when $u\geq 1$ , while monotonicity gives $e_{\bm{s}}(u)\leq e_{\bm{s}}(1)=1$ when $u<1$ . The same bounds applied to $C_{\rm en}$ and $C_{\rm ex}$ yield

$C_{\rm st}\geq 0,\qquad C_{\rm en}\geq-86,\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad C_{\rm ex}\geq-\frac{215}{4}.$

By Lemma 13 , $0\leq p^{\prime}\leq 1$ , so $0\leq p(10t-1)-p(10t-10)\leq 9$ . Since $p\geq 0$ , ( 10 ) gives $R(t)\geq-(9/10)(c_{R}+1)$ .

Set

$c_{\Delta}:=\frac{9}{10}(c_{R}+1)+86+\frac{215}{4}.$

This is a numerical constant independent of $M,N,D$ , and the preceding bounds give

$\bar{\Phi}(\bm{s},\bm{\nu})\geq\bar{f}(\bm{s},\bm{\nu};\mathbf{0})\geq-c_{\Delta}M.$

Together with $\bar{\Phi}(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{0})=0$ , this implies

$\bar{\Phi}(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{0})-\inf_{(\bm{s},\bm{\nu})\in\mathbb{R}^{3M}}\bar{\Phi}(\bm{s},\bm{\nu})\leq c_{\Delta}M,$

as required. ∎

6.5 Proofs of Theorems 1 and 2

We first record the rescaling that transfers the verified unscaled instance to the target parameters.

Assume Condition 1 (C1) . Fix an admissible pair $(M,N)$ and $\lambda>0$ , and set $D:=D_{\mathcal{Y}}/\lambda$ . Let $(\bar{f},\bm{\pi})$ be the corresponding unscaled hard instance. Define

$f(\bm{x};\bm{y}):=\frac{\ell\lambda^{2}}{\ell_{0}}\bar{f}\!\left(\frac{\bm{x}}{\lambda};\frac{\bm{y}}{\lambda}\right)$

on $\mathbb{R}^{3M}\times\mathbb{B}_{D_{\mathcal{Y}}}^{MN}$ . Then $f$ is jointly $\ell$ -smooth and concave in $\bm{y}$ , scaling preserves the coordinate supports of the saddle gradient, and

$\Phi(\bm{x})=\frac{\ell\lambda^{2}}{\ell_{0}}\bar{\Phi}\!\left(\frac{\bm{x}}{\lambda}\right),\qquad\nabla\Phi(\bm{x})=\frac{\ell\lambda}{\ell_{0}}\nabla\bar{\Phi}\!\left(\frac{\bm{x}}{\lambda}\right).$ (37)

The chain rule gives

$\nabla f(\bm{x};\bm{y})=\frac{\ell\lambda}{\ell_{0}}\nabla\bar{f}\!\left(\frac{\bm{x}}{\lambda};\frac{\bm{y}}{\lambda}\right).$

This proves smoothness, concavity, and support preservation. The map $\bm{y}\mapsto\bm{y}/\lambda$ carries $\mathbb{B}_{D_{\mathcal{Y}}}^{MN}$ bijectively onto $\mathcal{Y}_{D}$ , which together with differentiation yields ( 37 ). We finish the proof. ∎

With this rescaling in hand, we prove the zero-respecting lower bound.

Propositions 3 , 4 , 5 , and 6 verify Conditions 1 (C1)-(C4). Because Condition 1 (C3) remains valid after decreasing $g_{0}$ , we may replace $g_{0}$ by $\min\{g_{0},\ell_{0}\}$ and hence assume that $g_{0}\leq\ell_{0}$ . Set

$c_{0}:=\min\left\{\frac{c_{D}g_{0}}{80\ell_{0}},\frac{g_{0}}{\sqrt{128c_{\Delta}\ell_{0}}}\right\},\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad c_{1}:=\frac{c_{D}g_{0}^{3}}{256c_{\Delta}\ell_{0}^{2}}.$

Fix $\ell,D_{\mathcal{Y}},\Delta,\epsilon>0$ satisfying the assumptions of the theorem, and choose

$\lambda:=\frac{4\ell_{0}\epsilon}{g_{0}\ell},\qquad D:=\frac{D_{\mathcal{Y}}}{\lambda},\qquad N:=\lfloor c_{D}D\rfloor,\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad M:=\left\lfloor\frac{\ell_{0}\Delta}{2c_{\Delta}\ell\lambda^{2}}\right\rfloor.$ (38)

The assumed upper bound on $\epsilon$ gives

$c_{D}D=\frac{c_{D}D_{\mathcal{Y}}}{\lambda}\geq 20,\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\frac{\ell_{0}\Delta}{2c_{\Delta}\ell\lambda^{2}}\geq 4.$

Thus $N\geq 20$ and $M\geq 4$ , so $(M,N)$ is admissible. Moreover, ( 38 ) gives $N\leq c_{D}D$ .

Let $(\bar{f},\bm{\pi})$ be the corresponding unscaled instance, and let $f$ be its scaling from Lemma 7 . The lemma gives joint $\ell$ -smoothness and dual concavity, and the scaled dual domain has diameter $D_{\mathcal{Y}}$ . Condition 1 (C4), together with ( 37 ) and ( 38 ), gives

$\Phi(\mathbf{0})-\inf\Phi\leq\frac{\ell\lambda^{2}}{\ell_{0}}c_{\Delta}M\leq\Delta.$

Thus $f\in\mathcal{F}(\ell,D_{\mathcal{Y}},\Delta)$ .

Now let $\mathsf{A}\in\mathcal{A}_{\rm zr}\cap\mathcal{A}_{\rm det}$ run on $f$ , and write $(\bm{x}^{(t)},\bm{y}^{(t)})=(\mathsf{A}_{\bm{x}}^{(t)}[f],\mathsf{A}_{\bm{y}}^{(t)}[f])$ . Support preservation, Definition 4 , and Condition 1 (C2) imply by induction that

$\operatorname{supp}_{\bm{\pi}}(\bm{x}^{(t)},\bm{y}^{(t)})\subseteq[t],\qquad 0\leq t\leq L.$

By Condition 1 (C3), $s_{M}$ is the final coordinate of $\bm{\pi}$ . Hence $(\bm{x}^{(t)})_{s_{M}}=0$ for every $0\leq t<L$ .

Fix $0\leq t<L$ and suppose that $\bm{x}^{(t)}$ is $\epsilon$ -OS. Set $\bm{u}:=\operatorname{prox}_{\Phi/(2\ell)}(\bm{x}^{(t)})$ . The standard identity $\nabla\Phi_{1/(2\ell)}(\bm{x}^{(t)})=2\ell(\bm{x}^{(t)}-\bm{u})$ and Definition 2 give

$\left|\left(\frac{\bm{u}}{\lambda}\right)_{s_{M}}\right|=\frac{|(\bm{u}-\bm{x}^{(t)})_{s_{M}}|}{\lambda}\leq\frac{\lVert\bm{u}-\bm{x}^{(t)}\rVert}{\lambda}\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2\ell\lambda}=\frac{g_{0}}{8\ell_{0}}\leq\frac{1}{8}<\frac{1}{5}.$

By Condition 1 (C1) and the scaling identity for the value function, $\Phi$ is differentiable. The proximal optimality condition, Condition 1 (C3), and ( 37 ) therefore yield

$\epsilon\geq\left\lVert\nabla\Phi_{1/(2\ell)}(\bm{x}^{(t)})\right\rVert=\left\lVert\nabla\Phi(\bm{u})\right\rVert=\frac{\ell\lambda}{\ell_{0}}\left\lVert\nabla\bar{\Phi}\!\left(\frac{\bm{u}}{\lambda}\right)\right\rVert\geq\frac{\ell\lambda g_{0}}{\ell_{0}}=4\epsilon,$

a contradiction. Thus no query with $t<L$ is $\epsilon$ -OS.

Finally, $\lfloor r\rfloor\geq r/2$ for $r\geq 2$ and $\lfloor r\rfloor+3\geq r$ , together with ( 38 ), give

$L=M(N+3)\geq\frac{\ell_{0}\Delta}{4c_{\Delta}\ell\lambda^{2}}\frac{c_{D}D_{\mathcal{Y}}}{\lambda}=c_{1}\frac{\ell^{2}D_{\mathcal{Y}}\Delta}{\epsilon^{3}}.$

Since $\mathsf{A}$ was arbitrary,

$\mathcal{T}_{\epsilon}\bigl(\mathcal{A}_{\rm zr}\cap\mathcal{A}_{\rm det},\{f\}\bigr)\geq c_{1}\frac{\ell^{2}D_{\mathcal{Y}}\Delta}{\epsilon^{3}},$

which proves the theorem. ∎

We now extend the lower bound to arbitrary deterministic algorithms.

Use the constants $c_{0},c_{1}$ from Theorem 1 , and fix parameters satisfying its assumptions. Set $T_{0}:=\lceil c_{1}\ell^{2}D_{\mathcal{Y}}\Delta/\epsilon^{3}\rceil$ . The proof of Theorem 1 constructs an instance $f\in\mathcal{F}(\ell,D_{\mathcal{Y}},\Delta)$ on $\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{B}_{D_{\mathcal{Y}}}^{n}$ . Since $L$ is an integer and $L\geq c_{1}\ell^{2}D_{\mathcal{Y}}\Delta/\epsilon^{3}$ , we have $L\geq T_{0}$ . Thus every $\mathsf{Z}\in\mathcal{A}_{\rm zr}\cap\mathcal{A}_{\rm det}$ satisfies

$\left\lVert\nabla\Phi_{1/(2\ell)}(\mathsf{Z}_{\bm{x}}^{(t)}[f])\right\rVert>\epsilon,\qquad 0\leq t<T_{0}.$

Fix $\mathsf{A}\in\mathcal{A}_{\rm det}$ . Lemma 2 provides $\mathsf{Z}\in\mathcal{A}_{\rm zr}\cap\mathcal{A}_{\rm det}$ and orthogonal embeddings $\mathbf{U},\mathbf{V}$ satisfying ( 2 ). Lemma 1 gives $f_{\mathbf{U},\mathbf{V}}\in\mathcal{F}(\ell,D_{\mathcal{Y}},\Delta)$ and, for every $0\leq t<T_{0}$ ,

$\displaystyle\left\lVert\nabla[\Phi_{\mathbf{U},\mathbf{V}}]_{1/(2\ell)}\bigl(\mathsf{A}_{\bm{x}}^{(t)}[f_{\mathbf{U},\mathbf{V}}]\bigr)\right\rVert$ $\displaystyle=\left\lVert\nabla\Phi_{1/(2\ell)}\bigl(\mathbf{U}^{\top}\mathsf{A}_{\bm{x}}^{(t)}[f_{\mathbf{U},\mathbf{V}}]\bigr)\right\rVert=\left\lVert\nabla\Phi_{1/(2\ell)}\bigl(\mathsf{Z}_{\bm{x}}^{(t)}[f]\bigr)\right\rVert>\epsilon.$

Thus $\mathsf{A}$ needs at least $T_{0}$ queries on an instance in the target class. Since $\mathsf{A}$ was arbitrary,

$\mathcal{T}_{\epsilon}\bigl(\mathcal{A}_{\rm det},\mathcal{F}(\ell,D_{\mathcal{Y}},\Delta)\bigr)\geq T_{0}\geq c_{1}\frac{\ell^{2}D_{\mathcal{Y}}\Delta}{\epsilon^{3}}.$

We finish the proof. ∎

7 Proof of the Upper Bound

We prove Theorem 3 in three steps. First, we establish Proposition 1 , which bounds the error of the translated FOAM state. Next, we prove Proposition 2 by constructing an initial state through successive reductions of the dual regularization parameter. Finally, we combine these bounds with the outer updates to establish descent, stationarity, and the oracle complexity. The implementation of FOAM using feasible queries and the initialization at the first regularization level are given in Appendices B.1 and B.2 , respectively.

7.1 Proof of Proposition 1

Fix $0<r_{\bm{y}}\leq\ell/8$ . We bound the error of the translated state for the new proximal center. The translation lets us express the new conjugate objective as the old one plus a linear term and a constant. We then compare the distance and objective gap that define the Lyapunov function. For a positive definite matrix $\mathbf{H}$ , write $\lVert\bm{v}\rVert_{\mathbf{H}}^{2}:=\bm{v}^{\top}\mathbf{H}\bm{v}$ .

Let $\bm{z},\bm{z}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}$ with $\bm{z}^{\prime}=\bm{z}+\bm{d}$ , and write $S=(\bm{\omega},\bm{y},\bm{\omega}_{f},\bm{y}_{f})$ . The translated state is

$\widetilde{S}=(\bm{\omega}-2\ell\bm{d},\bm{y},\bm{\omega}_{f}-2\ell\bm{d},\bm{y}_{f}).$

Expanding the definition of the conjugate objective gives

$P_{\bm{z}^{\prime},r_{\bm{y}}}(\bm{\omega}-2\ell\bm{d},\bm{y})=P_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}(\bm{\omega},\bm{y})-2\left\langle\bm{d},\bm{\omega}\right\rangle+C,$ (39)

where $C$ is independent of $(\bm{\omega},\bm{y})$ . Set $\bm{u}:=(\bm{\omega},\bm{y})$ and $\bm{u}_{f}:=(\bm{\omega}_{f},\bm{y}_{f})$ , and define

$\widetilde{P}(\bm{u}):=P_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}(\bm{u})-\left\langle\bm{h},\bm{u}\right\rangle,\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\bm{h}:=(2\bm{d},\mathbf{0}).$

Let $\bm{u}^{\star}$ and $\widetilde{\bm{u}}^{\star}$ minimize $P_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}$ and $\widetilde{P}$ , respectively. Equation ( 39 ) gives

$\bm{u}^{\star}=(\bm{\omega}_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}^{\star},\bm{y}_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}^{\star}),\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\widetilde{\bm{u}}^{\star}=(\bm{\omega}_{\bm{z}^{\prime},r_{\bm{y}}}^{\star}+2\ell\bm{d},\bm{y}_{\bm{z}^{\prime},r_{\bm{y}}}^{\star}).$

Thus $\widetilde{\bm{u}}^{\star}$ is the new minimizer expressed in the original coordinates. Define

$\mathbf{H}_{r_{\bm{y}}}:=\operatorname{diag}(\ell^{-1}\mathbf{I}_{m},r_{\bm{y}}\mathbf{I}_{n}),\qquad\mathbf{B}_{r_{\bm{y}}}:=\operatorname{diag}(\alpha_{r_{\bm{y}}}\ell^{-1}\mathbf{I}_{m},r_{\bm{y}}\mathbf{I}_{n}),$

where $\alpha_{r_{\bm{y}}}=\sqrt{8r_{\bm{y}}/\ell}\leq 1$ . The objective $P_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}$ is $1$ -strongly convex with respect to $\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\mathbf{H}_{r_{\bm{y}}}}$ , while $\mathbf{B}_{r_{\bm{y}}}$ gives the weights in the distance term of $\mathcal{L}_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}$ . Strong convexity and the optimality conditions for the two minimizers give

$\left\lVert\widetilde{\bm{u}}^{\star}-\bm{u}^{\star}\right\rVert_{\mathbf{H}_{r_{\bm{y}}}}^{2}\leq\lVert\bm{h}\rVert_{\mathbf{H}_{r_{\bm{y}}}^{-1}}^{2}=4\ell\lVert\bm{d}\rVert^{2}.$ (40)

Since $\mathbf{B}_{r_{\bm{y}}}\preceq\mathbf{H}_{r_{\bm{y}}}$ , ( 40 ) bounds the distance term at the new center as follows:

$\displaystyle 2\left\lVert\bm{u}-\widetilde{\bm{u}}^{\star}\right\rVert_{\mathbf{B}_{r_{\bm{y}}}}^{2}$ $\displaystyle\leq 4\lVert\bm{u}-\bm{u}^{\star}\rVert_{\mathbf{B}_{r_{\bm{y}}}}^{2}+4\left\lVert\widetilde{\bm{u}}^{\star}-\bm{u}^{\star}\right\rVert_{\mathbf{B}_{r_{\bm{y}}}}^{2}$ $\displaystyle\leq 4\lVert\bm{u}-\bm{u}^{\star}\rVert_{\mathbf{B}_{r_{\bm{y}}}}^{2}+16\ell\lVert\bm{d}\rVert^{2}.$

We next bound the objective gap for the fast pair. Applying the same strong convexity inequality at $\bm{u}_{f}$ gives

$\displaystyle P_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}(\bm{u}_{f})-P_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}^{\star}\geq\frac{1}{2}\lVert\bm{u}_{f}-\bm{u}^{\star}\rVert_{\mathbf{H}_{r_{\bm{y}}}}^{2}.$ (41)

Since $\bm{u}^{\star}$ minimizes $P_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}$ , strong convexity gives, for every $\bm{u}$ ,

$\widetilde{P}(\bm{u})\geq P_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}^{\star}-\left\langle\bm{h},\bm{u}^{\star}\right\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\lVert\bm{u}-\bm{u}^{\star}\rVert_{\mathbf{H}_{r_{\bm{y}}}}^{2}-\left\langle\bm{h},\bm{u}-\bm{u}^{\star}\right\rangle.$

Minimizing the last two terms over $\bm{u}$ yields

$\min\widetilde{P}\geq P_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}^{\star}-\left\langle\bm{h},\bm{u}^{\star}\right\rangle-\frac{1}{2}\lVert\bm{h}\rVert_{\mathbf{H}_{r_{\bm{y}}}^{-1}}^{2}.$

Using this bound, together with the bound ( 41 ) and Young’s inequality, we obtain

$\displaystyle\widetilde{P}(\bm{u}_{f})-\min\widetilde{P}$ $\displaystyle\leq P_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}(\bm{u}_{f})-P_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}^{\star}-\left\langle\bm{h},\bm{u}_{f}-\bm{u}^{\star}\right\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\lVert\bm{h}\rVert_{\mathbf{H}_{r_{\bm{y}}}^{-1}}^{2}$ $\displaystyle\leq 2\bigl(P_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}(\bm{u}_{f})-P_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}^{\star}\bigr)+4\ell\lVert\bm{d}\rVert^{2}.$

By ( 39 ), the left-hand side is the objective gap for the translated fast pair at the new center. Combining these estimates with the definition of $\mathcal{L}$ in ( 16 ) gives

$\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\bm{z}^{\prime},r_{\bm{y}}}(\widetilde{S})$ $\displaystyle=2\left\lVert\bm{u}-\widetilde{\bm{u}}^{\star}\right\rVert_{\mathbf{B}_{r_{\bm{y}}}}^{2}+2\bigl(\widetilde{P}(\bm{u}_{f})-\min\widetilde{P}\bigr)$ $\displaystyle\leq 2\mathcal{L}_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}(S)+24\ell\lVert\bm{d}\rVert^{2}.$

This proves ( 21 ). Applying the contraction in Lemma 4 at $(\bm{z}^{\prime},r_{\bm{y}})$ gives, for every $\rho\in(0,1)$ ,

$\mathcal{L}_{\bm{z}^{\prime},r_{\bm{y}}}\bigl(\textnormal{{FOAM}}_{\bm{z}^{\prime},r_{\bm{y}}}(\widetilde{S};\rho)\bigr)\leq\rho\bigl(2\mathcal{L}_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}(S)+24\ell\lVert\bm{d}\rVert^{2}\bigr),$

which proves ( 22 ). ∎

7.2 Proof of Proposition 2

We prove Proposition 2 by constructing a state at the first regularization level $r_{\bm{y}}^{0}=\ell/8$ and then decreasing the dual regularization parameter by a factor of four at each level. The proximal center remains fixed at $\bm{z}^{0}=\mathbf{0}$ throughout. We first state the guarantee for the initial state.

Let $\bm{z}^{0}=\mathbf{0}$ and $r_{\bm{y}}^{0}:=\ell/8$ . A state $\widehat{S}^{0}$ satisfying

$\mathcal{L}_{\bm{z}^{0},r_{\bm{y}}^{0}}(\widehat{S}^{0})\leq 5\left(\Phi(\bm{z}^{0})-\inf\Phi+\frac{3}{2}r_{\bm{y}}^{0}D_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2}\right)$

can be constructed using a universal constant number of first-order saddle-oracle calls.

The construction and proof are given in Appendix B.2 . Choose $\widehat{S}^{0}$ using this construction. Since $\Phi(\bm{z}^{0})-\inf\Phi\leq\Delta$ , we have

$\mathcal{L}_{\bm{z}^{0},r_{\bm{y}}^{0}}(\widehat{S}^{0})\leq 5\left(\Delta+\frac{3}{2}r_{\bm{y}}^{0}D_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2}\right)\leq\widehat{B}^{0},\qquad\widehat{B}^{0}:=8(\Delta+r_{\bm{y}}^{0}D_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2}).$ (42)

We next bound the change in the Lyapunov function when the regularization parameter is divided by four and the state is retained.

For every $\bm{z}\in\mathcal{X}$ , $0<r_{\bm{y}}\leq\ell/8$ , and every state $S$ with $\mathcal{L}_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}(S)<\infty$ ,

$\mathcal{L}_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}/4}(S)\leq\mathcal{L}_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}(S)+\frac{27}{4}r_{\bm{y}}D_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2}.$ (43)

Set $r_{\bm{y}}^{\prime}:=r_{\bm{y}}/4$ , and let $\bm{u}_{r_{\bm{y}}}^{\star}$ and $\bm{u}_{r_{\bm{y}}^{\prime}}^{\star}$ minimize $P_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}$ and $P_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}^{\prime}}$ , respectively. The definition of the conjugate objective gives

$P_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}^{\prime}}(\bm{\omega},\bm{y})=P_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}(\bm{\omega},\bm{y})-\frac{3r_{\bm{y}}}{8}\left\lVert\bm{y}\right\rVert^{2}.$ (44)

Thus $(\mathbf{0},-3r_{\bm{y}}\bm{y}_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}^{\star}/4)\in\partial P_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}^{\prime}}(\bm{u}_{r_{\bm{y}}}^{\star})$ . Define $\mathbf{H}_{r_{\bm{y}}^{\prime}}:=\operatorname{diag}(\ell^{-1}\mathbf{I}_{m},r_{\bm{y}}^{\prime}\mathbf{I}_{n})$ . Strong convexity gives

$\displaystyle\left\lVert\bm{u}_{r_{\bm{y}}}^{\star}-\bm{u}_{r_{\bm{y}}^{\prime}}^{\star}\right\rVert_{\mathbf{H}_{r_{\bm{y}}^{\prime}}}^{2}$ $\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\left(\mathbf{0},-\frac{3r_{\bm{y}}}{4}\bm{y}_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}^{\star}\right)\right\rVert_{\mathbf{H}_{r_{\bm{y}}^{\prime}}^{-1}}^{2}=\frac{9r_{\bm{y}}}{4}\left\lVert\bm{y}_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}^{\star}\right\rVert^{2}\leq\frac{9r_{\bm{y}}}{4}D_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2},$ (45)

where we used $r_{\bm{y}}^{\prime}=r_{\bm{y}}/4$ and the bound $\lVert\bm{y}_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}^{\star}\rVert\leq D_{\mathcal{Y}}$ . Using $\alpha_{r_{\bm{y}}^{\prime}}=\alpha_{r_{\bm{y}}}/2$ and the squared triangle inequality, we obtain

$\displaystyle\frac{2\alpha_{r_{\bm{y}}^{\prime}}}{\ell}\left\lVert\bm{\omega}-\bm{\omega}_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}^{\prime}}^{\star}\right\rVert^{2}+2r_{\bm{y}}^{\prime}\left\lVert\bm{y}-\bm{y}_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}^{\prime}}^{\star}\right\rVert^{2}$ $\displaystyle\leq{}$ $\displaystyle\frac{2\alpha_{r_{\bm{y}}}}{\ell}\left\lVert\bm{\omega}-\bm{\omega}_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}^{\star}\right\rVert^{2}+r_{\bm{y}}\left\lVert\bm{y}-\bm{y}_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}^{\star}\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{2\alpha_{r_{\bm{y}}}}{\ell}\left\lVert\bm{\omega}_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}^{\star}-\bm{\omega}_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}^{\prime}}^{\star}\right\rVert^{2}+r_{\bm{y}}\left\lVert\bm{y}_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}^{\star}-\bm{y}_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}^{\prime}}^{\star}\right\rVert^{2}$ $\displaystyle\leq{}$ $\displaystyle\frac{2\alpha_{r_{\bm{y}}}}{\ell}\left\lVert\bm{\omega}-\bm{\omega}_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}^{\star}\right\rVert^{2}+2r_{\bm{y}}\left\lVert\bm{y}-\bm{y}_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}^{\star}\right\rVert^{2}+6r_{\bm{y}}D_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2}.$

For the last inequality, ( 45 ) and $\alpha_{r_{\bm{y}}}\leq 1$ give $\frac{2\alpha_{r_{\bm{y}}}}{\ell}\left\lVert\bm{\omega}_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}^{\star}-\bm{\omega}_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}^{\prime}}^{\star}\right\rVert^{2}\leq\frac{9}{2}r_{\bm{y}}D_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2}$ . Moreover, since both $\bm{y}_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}^{\star}$ and $\bm{y}_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}^{\prime}}^{\star}$ belong to $\mathcal{Y}$ , $r_{\bm{y}}\left\lVert\bm{y}_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}^{\star}-\bm{y}_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}^{\prime}}^{\star}\right\rVert^{2}\leq r_{\bm{y}}D_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2}$ . Thus the two terms contribute at most $\frac{11}{2}r_{\bm{y}}D_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2}\leq 6r_{\bm{y}}D_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2}$ .

For the fast pair $\bm{u}_{f}:=(\bm{\omega}_{f},\bm{y}_{f})$ , ( 44 ) and the bound $\lVert\bm{y}\rVert\leq D_{\mathcal{Y}}$ on $\mathcal{Y}$ give

$P_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}^{\prime}}(\bm{u}_{f})-P_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}^{\prime}}^{\star}\leq P_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}(\bm{u}_{f})-P_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}^{\star}+\frac{3r_{\bm{y}}}{8}D_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2}.$

Combining the two estimates therefore gives

$\mathcal{L}_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}/4}(S)\leq\mathcal{L}_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}(S)+\frac{27}{4}r_{\bm{y}}D_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2}.$

We finish the proof. ∎

We now use the preceding estimate to construct the initialization. Starting from $\widehat{S}^{0}$ at $r_{\bm{y}}^{0}=\ell/8$ , define

$J:=\min\left\{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\;\middle|\;\frac{r_{\bm{y}}^{0}}{4^{j}}\leq r_{\bm{y},\epsilon}\right\},\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad r_{\bm{y}}^{j}:=\frac{r_{\bm{y}}^{0}}{4^{j}},\quad j=0,\ldots,J.$ (46)

For $j=1,\ldots,J$ , set

$\displaystyle\widehat{S}^{j}$ $\displaystyle:=\textnormal{{FOAM}}_{\bm{z}^{0},r_{\bm{y}}^{j}}\left(\widehat{S}^{j-1};\frac{1}{8}\right),\qquad\mbox{and}$ (47) $\displaystyle\widehat{B}^{j}$ $\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{8}\left(\widehat{B}^{j-1}+\frac{27}{4}r_{\bm{y}}^{j-1}D_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2}\right).$ (48)

The initialization returned to Algorithm 1 is

$r_{\bm{y}}:=r_{\bm{y}}^{J},\qquad S^{0}:=\widehat{S}^{J},\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad B^{0}:=15(\Delta+r_{\bm{y}}D_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2}).$

At each level, $\widehat{B}^{j}$ bounds the error of $\widehat{S}^{j}$ . We verify these bounds below and show that the final state satisfies $\mathcal{L}_{\bm{z}^{0},r_{\bm{y}}}(S^{0})\leq B^{0}$ .

We prove by induction that

$\mathcal{L}_{\bm{z}^{0},r_{\bm{y}}^{j}}(\widehat{S}^{j})\leq\widehat{B}^{j}\leq 15(\Delta+r_{\bm{y}}^{j}D_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2}),\qquad j=0,\ldots,J.$

The case $j=0$ follows from ( 42 ). Suppose both inequalities hold at level $j-1$ . The contraction in Lemma 4 and ( 43 ) give

$\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\bm{z}^{0},r_{\bm{y}}^{j}}(\widehat{S}^{j})$ $\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{8}\mathcal{L}_{\bm{z}^{0},r_{\bm{y}}^{j}}(\widehat{S}^{j-1})\leq\frac{1}{8}\left(\widehat{B}^{j-1}+\frac{27}{4}r_{\bm{y}}^{j-1}D_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2}\right)=\widehat{B}^{j},$

where we used ( 47 ) and ( 48 ). Moreover, the induction hypothesis and $r_{\bm{y}}^{j-1}=4r_{\bm{y}}^{j}$ give

$\displaystyle\widehat{B}^{j}$ $\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{8}\left[15(\Delta+r_{\bm{y}}^{j-1}D_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2})+\frac{27}{4}r_{\bm{y}}^{j-1}D_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2}\right]=\frac{15}{8}\Delta+\frac{87}{8}r_{\bm{y}}^{j}D_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2}\leq 15(\Delta+r_{\bm{y}}^{j}D_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2}).$

This completes the induction. Consequently,

$\mathcal{L}_{\bm{z}^{0},r_{\bm{y}}}(S^{0})\leq\widehat{B}^{J}\leq 15(\Delta+r_{\bm{y}}D_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2})=B^{0},$

which proves the required bound on the returned state.

By ( 46 ), we have $r_{\bm{y}}^{J}\leq r_{\bm{y},\epsilon}$ . If $J\geq 1$ , its minimality gives $r_{\bm{y},\epsilon}<r_{\bm{y}}^{J-1}=4r_{\bm{y}}^{J}$ . If $J=0$ , then $r_{\bm{y}}^{0}=r_{\bm{y},\epsilon}=\ell/8$ . Thus, in either case,

$\frac{r_{\bm{y},\epsilon}}{4}<r_{\bm{y}}^{J}\leq r_{\bm{y},\epsilon},$

which proves ( 26 ).

Finally, Lemma 4 bounds the cost of the FOAM call at level $j$ by $\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{\ell/r_{\bm{y}}^{j}})$ , since the contraction factor is fixed at $1/8$ . Because $\sqrt{\ell/r_{\bm{y}}^{j}}=2^{j}\sqrt{\ell/r_{\bm{y}}^{0}}$ , each successive cost bound is twice the previous one. Their sum is therefore bounded by a constant multiple of the final bound. Including the initialization in Lemma 8 , the total number of calls to the first-order saddle oracle is

$\mathcal{O}\left(1+\sum_{j=1}^{J}\sqrt{\frac{\ell}{r_{\bm{y}}^{j}}}\right)=\mathcal{O}\left(\sqrt{\frac{\ell}{r_{\bm{y}}^{J}}}\right)=\mathcal{O}\left(\sqrt{\frac{\ell}{r_{\bm{y}}}}\right).$

This proves Proposition 2 . ∎

7.3 Proof of Theorem 3

We now combine Propositions 1 and 2 to prove Theorem 3 . Let $r_{\bm{y}},S^{0},B^{0}$ be the initialization used by Algorithm 1 , and set $r=(2\ell,r_{\bm{y}})$ . We keep $r_{\bm{y}}$ fixed throughout the outer iterations. Recall $W^{t}=p_{r}(\bm{z}^{t})+2B^{t}$ from ( 25 ). Since $B^{t}\geq 0$ , we have $W^{t}\geq\inf p_{r}$ . We first prove descent of $W^{t}$ , then establish stationarity of the selected output and bound the oracle complexity.

7.3.1 Coupled descent

Proposition 2 and the definition of $B^{0}$ give $\mathcal{L}_{\bm{z}^{0},r_{\bm{y}}}(S^{0})\leq B^{0}$ . Combining ( 22 ) with the recursion ( 23 ) therefore yields inductively

$\mathcal{L}_{\bm{z}^{t},r_{\bm{y}}}(S^{t})\leq B^{t},\qquad t=0,\ldots,T.$

Since $\bm{z}^{t+1}=\proj_{\mathcal{X}}(-\bm{\omega}_{f}^{t}/\ell)$ , the primal approximation bound in Lemma 4 gives

$\ell\left\lVert\bm{z}^{t+1}-\bm{x}_{\bm{z}^{t},r_{\bm{y}}}^{\star}\right\rVert^{2}\leq B^{t},\qquad t=0,\ldots,T-1.$ (49)

Thus $\bm{z}^{t+1}$ approximates the exact proximal point $\bm{x}_{\bm{z}^{t},r_{\bm{y}}}^{\star}$ at the current center $\bm{z}^{t}$ . Recall that $p_{r}$ is the Moreau envelope of $\Phi_{r}$ with parameter $1/(2\ell)$ , and hence

$\bm{x}_{\bm{z}^{t},r_{\bm{y}}}^{\star}=\bm{z}^{t}-\frac{1}{2\ell}\nabla p_{r}(\bm{z}^{t}).$

The following lemma combines the resulting inexact descent of $p_{r}$ with the recursion for $B^{t}$ .

For every $t=0,\ldots,T-1$ ,

$\displaystyle p_{r}(\bm{z}^{t+1})-p_{r}(\bm{z}^{t})\leq-\ell\left\lVert\bm{z}^{t}-\bm{x}_{\bm{z}^{t},r_{\bm{y}}}^{\star}\right\rVert^{2}+B^{t},\qquad\mbox{and}$ (50) $\displaystyle W^{t+1}-W^{t}\leq-\frac{3}{4}\left(\ell\left\lVert\bm{z}^{t}-\bm{x}_{\bm{z}^{t},r_{\bm{y}}}^{\star}\right\rVert^{2}+B^{t}\right).$ (51)

By the definition of $p_{r}$ ,

$\displaystyle p_{r}(\bm{z}^{t+1})-p_{r}(\bm{z}^{t})$ $\displaystyle\leq\Phi_{r}(\bm{x}_{\bm{z}^{t},r_{\bm{y}}}^{\star})+\ell\left\lVert\bm{z}^{t+1}-\bm{x}_{\bm{z}^{t},r_{\bm{y}}}^{\star}\right\rVert^{2}-p_{r}(\bm{z}^{t})$ $\displaystyle=-\ell\left\lVert\bm{z}^{t}-\bm{x}_{\bm{z}^{t},r_{\bm{y}}}^{\star}\right\rVert^{2}+\ell\left\lVert\bm{z}^{t+1}-\bm{x}_{\bm{z}^{t},r_{\bm{y}}}^{\star}\right\rVert^{2}$ $\displaystyle\leq-\ell\left\lVert\bm{z}^{t}-\bm{x}_{\bm{z}^{t},r_{\bm{y}}}^{\star}\right\rVert^{2}+B^{t},$

where the last inequality follows from ( 49 ). This proves ( 50 ).

Moreover, since $\bm{d}^{t}=\bm{z}^{t+1}-\bm{z}^{t}$ ,

$\displaystyle\ell\left\lVert\bm{d}^{t}\right\rVert^{2}$ $\displaystyle\leq 2\ell\left\lVert\bm{z}^{t}-\bm{x}_{\bm{z}^{t},r_{\bm{y}}}^{\star}\right\rVert^{2}+2\ell\left\lVert\bm{z}^{t+1}-\bm{x}_{\bm{z}^{t},r_{\bm{y}}}^{\star}\right\rVert^{2}\leq 2\ell\left\lVert\bm{z}^{t}-\bm{x}_{\bm{z}^{t},r_{\bm{y}}}^{\star}\right\rVert^{2}+2B^{t}.$ (52)

Substituting ( 52 ) into ( 23 ) gives

$B^{t+1}\leq\frac{48}{400}\ell\left\lVert\bm{z}^{t}-\bm{x}_{\bm{z}^{t},r_{\bm{y}}}^{\star}\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{50}{400}B^{t}.$

Combining this inequality with ( 50 ) and the definition of $W^{t}$ in ( 25 ) gives

$\displaystyle W^{t+1}-W^{t}$ $\displaystyle\leq-\left(1-\frac{96}{400}\right)\ell\left\lVert\bm{z}^{t}-\bm{x}_{\bm{z}^{t},r_{\bm{y}}}^{\star}\right\rVert^{2}-\left(1-\frac{100}{400}\right)B^{t}$ $\displaystyle\leq-\frac{3}{4}\left(\ell\left\lVert\bm{z}^{t}-\bm{x}_{\bm{z}^{t},r_{\bm{y}}}^{\star}\right\rVert^{2}+B^{t}\right),$

which proves ( 51 ). ∎

7.3.2 Stationarity and oracle complexity

The descent inequality ( 51 ) controls the residual

$R^{t}:=\ell\left\lVert\bm{z}^{t}-\bm{x}_{\bm{z}^{t},r_{\bm{y}}}^{\star}\right\rVert^{2}+B^{t}.$

This quantity contains the unknown exact proximal point and cannot be evaluated by the algorithm. Instead, Algorithm 1 uses the computable residual $Q^{t}=\ell\lVert\bm{d}^{t}\rVert^{2}+B^{t}$ . The following lemma compares these residuals and bounds the gradient of $p_{r}$ at the selected output.

For every $t=0,\ldots,T-1$ ,

$Q^{t}\leq 3R^{t},\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\left\lVert\nabla p_{r}(\bm{z}^{t})\right\rVert^{2}\leq 8\ell Q^{t}.$ (53)

Consequently, for the index $t^{\star}$ selected by Algorithm 1 ,

$\left\lVert\nabla p_{r}(\bm{z}^{t^{\star}})\right\rVert^{2}\leq\frac{32\ell}{T}\bigl(W^{0}-\inf p_{r}\bigr).$ (54)

By ( 52 ), we have

$Q^{t}\leq 2\ell\left\lVert\bm{z}^{t}-\bm{x}_{\bm{z}^{t},r_{\bm{y}}}^{\star}\right\rVert^{2}+3B^{t}\leq 3R^{t}.$

Moreover, using $\nabla p_{r}(\bm{z}^{t})=2\ell(\bm{z}^{t}-\bm{x}_{\bm{z}^{t},r_{\bm{y}}}^{\star})$ , $\bm{d}^{t}=\bm{z}^{t+1}-\bm{z}^{t}$ , and ( 49 ), we have

$\displaystyle\left\lVert\nabla p_{r}(\bm{z}^{t})\right\rVert^{2}$ $\displaystyle\leq 8\ell^{2}\left\lVert\bm{d}^{t}\right\rVert^{2}+8\ell^{2}\left\lVert\bm{z}^{t+1}-\bm{x}_{\bm{z}^{t},r_{\bm{y}}}^{\star}\right\rVert^{2}$ $\displaystyle\leq 8\ell\bigl(\ell\left\lVert\bm{d}^{t}\right\rVert^{2}+B^{t}\bigr)=8\ell Q^{t}.$

Combining ( 51 ) with $Q^{t}\leq 3R^{t}$ gives

$W^{t+1}-W^{t}\leq-\frac{1}{4}Q^{t}.$

Summing over $t=0,\ldots,T-1$ and using $W^{T}\geq\inf p_{r}$ , we obtain

$Q^{t^{\star}}\leq\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}Q^{t}\leq\frac{4}{T}\bigl(W^{0}-\inf p_{r}\bigr),$

where the first inequality follows from the choice of $t^{\star}$ . Combining this bound with ( 53 ) proves ( 54 ). ∎

It remains to bound the initial gap $W^{0}-\inf p_{r}$ , verify the original OS criterion, and count the oracle calls.

Since $\bm{z}^{0}=\mathbf{0}$ , $p_{r}(\bm{z}^{0})\leq\Phi_{r}(\bm{z}^{0})$ , and $\inf p_{r}=\inf\Phi_{r}$ , the perturbation bound ( 14 ) gives

$p_{r}(\bm{z}^{0})-\inf p_{r}\leq\Phi_{r}(\bm{z}^{0})-\inf\Phi_{r}\leq\Delta+\frac{r_{\bm{y}}}{2}D_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2}.$

Together with $B^{0}=15(\Delta+r_{\bm{y}}D_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2})$ , this yields

$W^{0}-\inf p_{r}=p_{r}(\bm{z}^{0})-\inf p_{r}+2B^{0}\leq 31\left(\Delta+r_{\bm{y}}D_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2}\right).$ (55)

Combining ( 54 ) with ( 55 ) gives

$\left\lVert\nabla p_{r}(\bm{z}^{t^{\star}})\right\rVert^{2}\leq\frac{992\ell}{T}\left(\Delta+r_{\bm{y}}D_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2}\right).$

Since $r_{\bm{y}}\leq r_{\bm{y},\epsilon}$ , we have $r_{\bm{y}}D_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2}\leq\epsilon^{2}/(8\ell)$ . By the choice of $T$ in Algorithm 1 ,

$\left\lVert\nabla p_{r}(\bm{z}^{t^{\star}})\right\rVert^{2}\leq\frac{992}{T}\left(\ell\Delta+\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{8}\right)\leq\frac{992}{4000}\epsilon^{2}<\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{4}.$

Thus $\lVert\nabla p_{r}(\bm{z}^{t^{\star}})\rVert<\epsilon/2$ . The same bound $r_{\bm{y}}\leq r_{\bm{y},\epsilon}$ gives $D_{\mathcal{Y}}\sqrt{2\ell r_{\bm{y}}}\leq\epsilon/2$ . Lemma 3 therefore implies

$\left\lVert\nabla\Phi_{1/(2\ell)}(\bm{z}^{t^{\star}})\right\rVert\leq\left\lVert\nabla p_{r}(\bm{z}^{t^{\star}})\right\rVert+D_{\mathcal{Y}}\sqrt{2\ell r_{\bm{y}}}\leq\epsilon.$

Since $\bm{z}^{t^{\star}}\in\mathcal{X}$ , the selected output is an $\epsilon$ -OS point.

Finally, we count the calls to the first-order saddle oracle. By Lemma 4 , each FOAM call in the outer iterations requires $\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{\ell/r_{\bm{y}}})$ oracle calls, since its reduction factor is fixed at $1/400$ . Proposition 2 gives the same order of cost for the initialization. Moreover, $r_{\bm{y}}>r_{\bm{y},\epsilon}/4$ implies

$\sqrt{\frac{\ell}{r_{\bm{y}}}}=\mathcal{O}\left(\sqrt{\frac{\ell}{r_{\bm{y},\epsilon}}}\right)=\mathcal{O}\left(\max\left\{1,\frac{\ell D_{\mathcal{Y}}}{\epsilon}\right\}\right).$

Since $T=\mathcal{O}(\ell\Delta/\epsilon^{2}+1)$ , the initialization and the $T$ outer iterations together require

$\mathcal{O}\left((T+1)\sqrt{\frac{\ell}{r_{\bm{y}}}}\right)=\mathcal{O}\left[\left(\frac{\ell\Delta}{\epsilon^{2}}+1\right)\max\left\{1,\frac{\ell D_{\mathcal{Y}}}{\epsilon}\right\}\right]$

calls to the first-order saddle oracle. This proves Theorem 3 . ∎

8 Conclusion

We established the optimal deterministic first-order oracle complexity of finding an $\epsilon$ -OS point in smooth NC-C minimax optimization with a compact convex dual domain. For $\epsilon\lesssim\min\{\ell D_{\mathcal{Y}},\sqrt{\ell\Delta}\}$ , we proved a lower bound of $\Omega(\ell^{2}D_{\mathcal{Y}}\Delta/\epsilon^{3})$ for arbitrary deterministic first-order methods. Our algorithm, Tracked-FOAM , attains the matching upper bound, thereby removing the logarithmic gap in previous complexity guarantees.

A natural next step is to extend our lower bound to randomized first-order algorithms and determine whether randomization can improve the worst-case oracle complexity. Our $C^{\infty}$ hard instance may also serve as a starting point for establishing higher-order oracle lower bounds, extending the study of such bounds for smooth nonconvex minimization ( Carmon et al., 2020 ) to the minimax setting. This direction requires controlling the higher-order derivatives and the information they reveal about the hard instance.

A.1 Proof of Lemma 1

Since $\mathbf{V}^{\top}$ maps $\mathbb{B}_{D_{\mathcal{Y}}}^{n^{\prime}}$ onto $\mathbb{B}_{D_{\mathcal{Y}}}^{n}$ , $\Phi_{\mathbf{U},\mathbf{V}}(\bm{x})=\Phi(\mathbf{U}^{\top}\bm{x})$ . The chain rule and the contractions $\mathbf{U}^{\top},\mathbf{V}^{\top}$ preserve joint smoothness, and dual concavity is unchanged. Surjectivity of $\mathbf{U}^{\top}$ preserves the initial gap, so $f_{\mathbf{U},\mathbf{V}}\in\mathcal{F}(\ell,D_{\mathcal{Y}},\Delta)$ .

For each $\bm{u}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}$ , the minimum of $\lVert\bm{x}-\bm{z}\rVert^{2}$ subject to $\mathbf{U}^{\top}\bm{z}=\bm{u}$ is $\lVert\mathbf{U}^{\top}\bm{x}-\bm{u}\rVert^{2}$ , attained at $\bm{z}=\mathbf{U}\bm{u}+(\mathbf{I}_{m^{\prime}}-\mathbf{U}\mathbf{U}^{\top})\bm{x}$ . Substituting this identity into the definition of the Moreau envelope gives

$\bigl[\Phi_{\mathbf{U},\mathbf{V}}\bigr]_{\frac{1}{2\ell}}(\bm{x})=\Phi_{\frac{1}{2\ell}}(\mathbf{U}^{\top}\bm{x}).$

Differentiating and using $\mathbf{U}^{\top}\mathbf{U}=\mathbf{I}_{m}$ yields

$\nabla\bigl[\Phi_{\mathbf{U},\mathbf{V}}\bigr]_{\frac{1}{2\ell}}(\bm{x})=\mathbf{U}\nabla\Phi_{\frac{1}{2\ell}}(\mathbf{U}^{\top}\bm{x}),$

which proves ( 1 ). ∎

A.2 Proof of Lemma 2

Fix $T_{0}$ and $\mathsf{A}\in\mathcal{A}_{\rm det}$ . We first fix a deterministic rule for choosing orthogonal extensions. Given a subspace of a Euclidean space, project the standard basis vectors onto its orthogonal complement and apply Gram-Schmidt in their fixed order, discarding zero residuals. If several vectors are required, assign the resulting orthonormal vectors in increasing coordinate order. We use this rule independently on the primal and dual sides.

We construct $\mathsf{Z}$ recursively by simulating $\mathsf{A}$ . To describe its execution, fix $m,n\in\mathbb{N}$ , $D_{\mathcal{Y}}>0$ , and a smooth saddle function $f:\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{B}_{D_{\mathcal{Y}}}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}$ . Let $(\bar{\bm{x}}^{(t)},\bar{\bm{y}}^{(t)})$ be the base query generated by the simulator, let $(\bm{x}^{(t)},\bm{y}^{(t)})$ be the virtual ambient query of $\mathsf{A}$ , and let $\widehat{\mathbb{O}}^{(t)}$ be the simulated reply supplied to $\mathsf{A}$ . Define $\mathcal{I}_{\bm{x}}^{(0)}=\mathcal{I}_{\bm{y}}^{(0)}=\varnothing,$ and, for $t\geq 1$ ,

$\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\bm{x}}^{(t)}:=\bigcup_{r<t}\operatorname{supp}\nabla_{\bm{x}}f(\bar{\bm{x}}^{(r)};\bar{\bm{y}}^{(r)}),\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\mathcal{I}_{\bm{y}}^{(t)}:=\bigcup_{r<t}\operatorname{supp}\nabla_{\bm{y}}f(\bar{\bm{x}}^{(r)};\bar{\bm{y}}^{(r)}).$

Along with the transcript, we construct orthonormal families

$\{\bm{u}_{k}\mid k\in\mathcal{I}_{\bm{x}}^{(t)}\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m+T_{0}},\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\{\bm{v}_{j}\mid j\in\mathcal{I}_{\bm{y}}^{(t)}\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n+T_{0}}.$

At the beginning of round $t$ , we maintain the following four properties:

for every $r<t$ ,

$\operatorname{supp}(\bar{\bm{x}}^{(r)})\subseteq\mathcal{I}_{\bm{x}}^{(r)},\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\operatorname{supp}(\bar{\bm{y}}^{(r)})\subseteq\mathcal{I}_{\bm{y}}^{(r)};$

for every $r<t$ ,

$\langle\bm{u}_{k},\bm{x}^{(r)}\rangle=\bar{x}_{k}^{(r)}\quad(k\in\mathcal{I}_{\bm{x}}^{(t)}),\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\langle\bm{v}_{j},\bm{y}^{(r)}\rangle=\bar{y}_{j}^{(r)}\quad(j\in\mathcal{I}_{\bm{y}}^{(t)});$

for every $r<t$ ,

$\widehat{\mathbb{O}}^{(r)}=\left(f(\bar{\bm{x}}^{(r)};\bar{\bm{y}}^{(r)}),\sum_{k\in\mathcal{I}_{\bm{x}}^{(r+1)}}\bigl[\nabla_{\bm{x}}f(\bar{\bm{x}}^{(r)};\bar{\bm{y}}^{(r)})\bigr]_{k}\bm{u}_{k},\sum_{j\in\mathcal{I}_{\bm{y}}^{(r+1)}}\bigl[\nabla_{\bm{y}}f(\bar{\bm{x}}^{(r)};\bar{\bm{y}}^{(r)})\bigr]_{j}\bm{v}_{j}\right);$ (56)

for every $r<t$ , $(\bm{x}^{(r)},\bm{y}^{(r)})$ is the $r$ -th query generated by $\mathsf{A}$ from $\widehat{\mathbb{O}}^{(0)},\ldots,\widehat{\mathbb{O}}^{(r-1)}$ .

These properties hold vacuously at $t=0$ . Suppose they hold at the beginning of a round $t<T_{0}$ . To verify feasibility, temporarily complete the current primal and dual families by choosing the remaining columns orthogonal to the assigned columns and all previous virtual queries. On the primal side, the available space has dimension at least

$(m+T_{0})-t-|\mathcal{I}_{\bm{x}}^{(t)}|\geq m-|\mathcal{I}_{\bm{x}}^{(t)}|,$

which is the number of unassigned columns. The same count applies on the dual side. Properties (i)-(iii) show that the resulting rotated function produces all previous simulated replies. By property (iv) and determinism, the next virtual query $(\bm{x}^{(t)},\bm{y}^{(t)})$ is therefore a feasible query of $\mathsf{A}$ on this function. In particular, $\lVert\bm{y}^{(t)}\rVert\leq D_{\mathcal{Y}}/2$ . These temporary completions are used only to justify feasibility; the simulator retains only the previously assigned columns.

Define the next base query by

$\bar{x}_{k}^{(t)}:=\begin{cases}\langle\bm{u}_{k},\bm{x}^{(t)}\rangle,&k\in\mathcal{I}_{\bm{x}}^{(t)},\\ 0,&k\notin\mathcal{I}_{\bm{x}}^{(t)},\end{cases}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\bar{y}_{j}^{(t)}:=\begin{cases}\langle\bm{v}_{j},\bm{y}^{(t)}\rangle,&j\in\mathcal{I}_{\bm{y}}^{(t)},\\ 0,&j\notin\mathcal{I}_{\bm{y}}^{(t)}.\end{cases}$

The primal query is feasible because its domain is $\mathbb{R}^{m}$ . Bessel’s inequality gives $\lVert\bar{\bm{y}}^{(t)}\rVert\leq\lVert\bm{y}^{(t)}\rVert\leq D_{\mathcal{Y}}/2$ , so the dual query is feasible as well. The two support inclusions show that property (i) holds for the new query. In particular, the initial base query is the origin, and every base query is zero-respecting.

After querying $f$ , let

$\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\bm{x}}^{(t)}$ $\displaystyle:=\operatorname{supp}\nabla_{\bm{x}}f(\bar{\bm{x}}^{(t)};\bar{\bm{y}}^{(t)})\setminus\mathcal{I}_{\bm{x}}^{(t)},\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathcal{N}_{\bm{y}}^{(t)}:=\operatorname{supp}\nabla_{\bm{y}}f(\bar{\bm{x}}^{(t)};\bar{\bm{y}}^{(t)})\setminus\mathcal{I}_{\bm{y}}^{(t)}.$

Thus $\mathcal{I}_{\bm{x}}^{(t+1)}=\mathcal{I}_{\bm{x}}^{(t)}\cup\mathcal{N}_{\bm{x}}^{(t)}$ , and analogously on the dual side. The primal orthogonal complement available for the newly revealed columns has dimension at least

$(m+T_{0})-(t+1)-|\mathcal{I}_{\bm{x}}^{(t)}|\geq m-|\mathcal{I}_{\bm{x}}^{(t)}|\geq|\mathcal{N}_{\bm{x}}^{(t)}|.$

Hence the fixed rule chooses $\{\bm{u}_{k}\mid k\in\mathcal{N}_{\bm{x}}^{(t)}\}$ orthonormally in

$\operatorname{span}\bigl(\{\bm{x}^{(0)},\ldots,\bm{x}^{(t)}\}\cup\{\bm{u}_{k}\mid k\in\mathcal{I}_{\bm{x}}^{(t)}\}\bigr)^{\perp}.$

The identical dimension count with $n$ , $\bm{y}^{(r)}$ , and $\mathcal{I}_{\bm{y}}^{(t)}$ supplies $\{\bm{v}_{j}\mid j\in\mathcal{N}_{\bm{y}}^{(t)}\}$ .

For an old primal index, property (ii) for earlier rounds and the definition of $\bar{\bm{x}}^{(t)}$ give $\langle\bm{u}_{k},\bm{x}^{(r)}\rangle=\bar{x}_{k}^{(r)}$ for every $r\leq t$ . For a newly revealed primal index, the chosen column is orthogonal to $\bm{x}^{(0)},\ldots,\bm{x}^{(t)}$ , while the corresponding base coordinate is zero at every round through $t$ . The same argument applies to the dual columns. Thus property (ii) holds at the beginning of round $t+1$ .

Define $\widehat{\mathbb{O}}^{(t)}$ by ( 56 ) with $r=t$ and supply it to $\mathsf{A}$ . This verifies property (iii), while the construction of the virtual query verifies property (iv). Hence all four properties hold at the beginning of round $t+1$ , completing the induction.

After round $T_{0}-1$ , use the same rule to choose the remaining primal columns orthogonal to the assigned columns and all $T_{0}$ virtual queries. The available space has dimension at least

$(m+T_{0})-T_{0}-|\mathcal{I}_{\bm{x}}^{(T_{0})}|=m-|\mathcal{I}_{\bm{x}}^{(T_{0})}|,$

which is the number of unassigned primal columns. The same count completes the dual family. Set

$\mathbf{U}:=[\bm{u}_{1},\ldots,\bm{u}_{m}]\in\operatorname{O}(m+T_{0},m),\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathbf{V}:=[\bm{v}_{1},\ldots,\bm{v}_{n}]\in\operatorname{O}(n+T_{0},n).$

Property (ii) at $T_{0}$ handles the assigned columns. Every unassigned column is orthogonal to the first $T_{0}$ virtual queries, and the corresponding base coordinates vanish by property (i). Consequently,

$\mathbf{U}^{\top}\bm{x}^{(t)}=\bar{\bm{x}}^{(t)},\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathbf{V}^{\top}\bm{y}^{(t)}=\bar{\bm{y}}^{(t)},\qquad 0\leq t<T_{0}.$ (57)

The two base gradients are supported on $\mathcal{I}_{\bm{x}}^{(t+1)}$ and $\mathcal{I}_{\bm{y}}^{(t+1)}$ , respectively, so ( 57 ), the chain rule, and ( 56 ) give

$\mathbb{O}_{f_{\mathbf{U},\mathbf{V}}}(\bm{x}^{(t)};\bm{y}^{(t)})=\widehat{\mathbb{O}}^{(t)},\qquad 0\leq t<T_{0}.$

Starting from the common initial query at the origin, determinism of $\mathsf{A}$ identifies the virtual queries with its first $T_{0}$ queries on $f_{\mathbf{U},\mathbf{V}}$ . Combining this with ( 57 ) gives

$\bigl(\mathsf{Z}_{\bm{x}}^{(t)}[f],\mathsf{Z}_{\bm{y}}^{(t)}[f]\bigr)=\bigl(\mathbf{U}^{\top}\mathsf{A}_{\bm{x}}^{(t)}[f_{\mathbf{U},\mathbf{V}}],\mathbf{V}^{\top}\mathsf{A}_{\bm{y}}^{(t)}[f_{\mathbf{U},\mathbf{V}}]\bigr),\qquad 0\leq t<T_{0}.$

The fixed extension rule makes $\mathsf{Z}$ deterministic. The construction uses only $\mathsf{A}$ , the known domains and dimensions, and the oracle replies already received, so the same simulation rule applies to every $f$ . After the simulated horizon, let $\mathsf{Z}$ query the origin at every round. On other domains containing the primal-dual origin, define $\mathsf{Z}$ to query the origin throughout. These choices preserve feasibility and the zero-respecting property. Hence $\mathsf{Z}\in\mathcal{A}_{\rm zr}\cap\mathcal{A}_{\rm det}$ , and the proof is complete. ∎

A.3 Properties of the inner chain

The following lemma adapts Li et al. [2021, Lemmas 4 and 7] to our setting and records the properties of the inner chain used in Section 6 .

There are numerical constants $c_{H},c_{\bm{y}}>0$ , independent of $M,N,D$ , such that the following properties hold.

The bound $6/5\leq c_{N}\leq 8/5$ and the identity ( 5 ) hold. Moreover, the maximizer satisfies $\lVert\bm{w}^{\star}(a,b)\rVert\leq c_{\bm{y}}N\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}}$ .

The function $H(a,b;\cdot)$ is $N^{-2}$ -strongly concave and its joint Hessian has norm at most $c_{H}$ ;

$H$ is a zero-chain in the order $(a,w_{1},\ldots,w_{N},b)$ , while $H(a,b;\mathbf{0})\geq 0$ and $H(0,0;\bm{w})\leq 0$ ;

The function

$V_{D}(\bm{\nu}):=\max_{\bm{y}\in\mathcal{Y}_{D}}\sum_{i=1}^{M}H(a_{i},b_{i};\bm{y}^{(i)})$

is differentiable and satisfies

$\left\langle\bm{\nu},\nabla V_{D}(\bm{\nu})\right\rangle\geq\frac{2}{5}\left\lVert\bm{\nu}\right\rVert^{2}.$

We first prove (i). Li et al. [2021, Lemma 7] establish $N/10\leq(\mathbf{B}_{N})_{j1}\leq 20N$ for every $j\in[N]$ . Reversing the coordinate order leaves $\mathbf{M}_{N}$ and its inverse unchanged, so the same bounds hold for the last column. Hence

$\frac{N}{10}\leq k_{N}\leq 20N,\qquad k_{N}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\leq\sqrt{\frac{10}{N}},\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\left\lVert\mathbf{B}_{N}\bm{e}_{1}\right\rVert=\left\lVert\mathbf{B}_{N}\bm{e}_{N}\right\rVert\leq 20N^{\frac{3}{2}}.$ (58)

To bound $c_{N}$ , write $\xi_{j}:=(\mathbf{B}_{N})_{j1}$ . The interior and last rows of $\mathbf{M}_{N}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{N})^{\top}=\bm{e}_{1}$ give

$\xi_{j-1}-(2+N^{-2})\xi_{j}+\xi_{j+1}=0\quad(2\leq j\leq N-1),\quad\mbox{and}\quad\xi_{N-1}=(1+N^{-2})\xi_{N}.$ (59)

Choose $\lambda_{N}>0$ so that $\cosh\lambda_{N}=1+1/(2N^{2})$ . The formula

$\xi_{j}=\xi_{N}\frac{\cosh((N-j+\frac{1}{2})\lambda_{N})}{\cosh(\frac{\lambda_{N}}{2})},\qquad j\in[N],$

satisfies both relations in ( 59 ). These relations determine all entries from $\xi_{N}$ , so the formula holds.

Since $\cosh t\geq 1+t^{2}/2$ for $t\geq 0$ , the defining equation gives $\lambda_{N}\leq 1/N\leq 1$ . We can therefore apply $\cosh t\leq 1+t^{2}$ on $[0,1]$ to obtain $\lambda_{N}\geq 1/(\sqrt{2}N)$ . Since $c_{N}=\xi_{1}/\xi_{N}$ and $\cosh(u+v)\geq\cosh u\cosh v$ for $u,v\geq 0$ , we obtain

$\displaystyle c_{N}$ $\displaystyle\geq\cosh((N-1)\lambda_{N})\geq 1+\frac{(N-1)^{2}}{4N^{2}}\geq 1+\frac{81}{400}>\frac{6}{5},\qquad\mbox{and}$ $\displaystyle c_{N}$ $\displaystyle\leq\cosh(N\lambda_{N})\leq\cosh(1)<\frac{8}{5},$

where we used $N\geq 10$ . Thus $2-c_{N}\in[2/5,4/5]$ .

The dual optimality condition gives $\bm{w}^{\star}(a,b)=k_{N}^{-1/2}\mathbf{B}_{N}(a\bm{e}_{1}-b\bm{e}_{N})$ . The triangle inequality and ( 58 ) yield

$\left\lVert\bm{w}^{\star}(a,b)\right\rVert\leq 20\sqrt{10}\,N(|a|+|b|)\leq 20\sqrt{20}\,N\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}}.$

Thus we may take $c_{\bm{y}}:=20\sqrt{20}$ . Symmetry also gives $(\mathbf{B}_{N})_{11}=(\mathbf{B}_{N})_{NN}=c_{N}k_{N}$ and $(\mathbf{B}_{N})_{1N}=(\mathbf{B}_{N})_{N1}=k_{N}$ . Substituting the maximizer therefore gives

$\displaystyle\max_{\bm{w}}H(a,b;\bm{w})$ $\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2k_{N}}(a\bm{e}_{1}-b\bm{e}_{N})^{\top}\mathbf{B}_{N}(a\bm{e}_{1}-b\bm{e}_{N})+\frac{2-c_{N}}{2}(a^{2}+b^{2})$ $\displaystyle=\frac{c_{N}}{2}(a^{2}+b^{2})-ab+\frac{2-c_{N}}{2}(a^{2}+b^{2})$ $\displaystyle=a^{2}-ab+b^{2},$

which proves ( 5 ) and (i).

For (ii), the eigenvalue bounds $N^{-2}\mathbf{I}_{N}\preceq\mathbf{M}_{N}\preceq(4+N^{-2})\mathbf{I}_{N}$ show that $H(a,b;\cdot)$ is $N^{-2}$ -strongly concave. Let ${\bf E}_{N}:=(\bm{e}_{1},-\bm{e}_{N})$ , so $\lVert{\bf E}_{N}\rVert=1$ . In the variables $(a,b,\bm{w})$ , the Hessian is

$\nabla^{2}H=\begin{pmatrix}(2-c_{N})\mathbf{I}_{2}&k_{N}^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\bf E}_{N}^{\top}\\ k_{N}^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\bf E}_{N}&-\mathbf{M}_{N}\end{pmatrix}.$

Splitting this matrix into its block diagonal and off-diagonal parts gives

$\left\lVert\nabla^{2}H\right\rVert\leq\max\{2-c_{N},\left\lVert\mathbf{M}_{N}\right\rVert\}+k_{N}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left\lVert{\bf E}_{N}\right\rVert<5+1=6,$

where ( 58 ) and $N\geq 10$ give $k_{N}^{-1/2}\leq 1$ . Taking $c_{H}:=6$ proves (ii).

For (iii), $\mathbf{M}_{N}$ is tridiagonal, and the endpoint couplings involve only $aw_{1}$ and $bw_{N}$ . Thus each component of the saddle vector field depends linearly only on its own coordinate and its immediate neighbors in the order $(a,w_{1},\ldots,w_{N},b)$ . If the input is supported on a prefix of this order, every component of the vector field beyond the next coordinate is therefore zero. This proves the zero-chain property. The remaining claims follow from $H(a,b;\mathbf{0})=(2-c_{N})(a^{2}+b^{2})/2\geq 0$ and $H(0,0;\bm{w})=-\bm{w}^{\top}\mathbf{M}_{N}\bm{w}/2\leq 0$ .

For (iv), define $V_{D}^{\circ}(\bm{\nu}):=V_{D}(\bm{\nu})-(2-c_{N})\lVert\bm{\nu}\rVert^{2}/2$ . By the definition of $H$ , this is the maximum of functions affine in $\bm{\nu}$ , so it is convex. The set $\mathcal{Y}_{D}$ is compact and convex, and the objective is strongly concave in $\bm{y}$ , so the maximizer exists and is unique. Danskin’s theorem therefore gives differentiability of $V_{D}^{\circ}$ and $V_{D}$ .

Feasibility of $\bm{y}=\mathbf{0}$ gives $V_{D}^{\circ}(\bm{\nu})\geq 0$ . At $\bm{\nu}=\mathbf{0}$ , the maximized expression is a nonpositive quadratic that vanishes at $\bm{y}=\mathbf{0}$ , so $V_{D}^{\circ}(\mathbf{0})=0$ . Convexity now yields

$\left\langle\bm{\nu},\nabla V_{D}^{\circ}(\bm{\nu})\right\rangle\geq V_{D}^{\circ}(\bm{\nu})-V_{D}^{\circ}(\mathbf{0})\geq 0.$

Adding back the quadratic term gives

$\left\langle\bm{\nu},\nabla V_{D}(\bm{\nu})\right\rangle=\left\langle\bm{\nu},\nabla V_{D}^{\circ}(\bm{\nu})\right\rangle+(2-c_{N})\left\lVert\bm{\nu}\right\rVert^{2}\geq\frac{2}{5}\left\lVert\bm{\nu}\right\rVert^{2},$

which proves (iv). We finish the proof. ∎

A.4 Properties of $p$

$p\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})$ . Moreover, for every $t\in\mathbb{R}$ ,

$0\leq p^{\prime}(t)\leq 1,\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad 0\leq p^{\prime\prime}(t)\leq 2.$

Direct computation gives the explicit form of $p^{\prime}$

$p^{\prime}(t)=\begin{cases}0,&t\leq 0,\\[2.0pt] \displaystyle\frac{\exp(-\frac{1}{t})}{\exp(-\frac{1}{t})+\exp(-\frac{1}{1-t})},&0<t<1,\\[8.0pt] 1,&t\geq 1.\end{cases}$ (60)

The middle branch of ( 60 ) extends smoothly to $0$ and $1$ , with values $0$ and $1$ , respectively, and all derivatives of positive order vanishing at both endpoints. Moreover, the identity $p^{\prime}(t)+p^{\prime}(1-t)=1$ on $(0,1)$ gives $\int_{0}^{1}p^{\prime}(v)\,{\rm d}v=1/2$ . Thus the integral branch in ( 6 ) matches the two affine tails in both value and all derivatives, proving $p\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})$ .

Equation ( 60 ) also gives $0\leq p^{\prime}(t)\leq 1$ and $p^{\prime\prime}(t)=0$ outside $(0,1)$ . For $0<t<1$ , set $v:=2t-1$ and $z:=2v/(1-v^{2})$ . Differentiating ( 60 ) yields

$0\leq p^{\prime\prime}(t)=\frac{2(1+v^{2})}{(1-v^{2})^{2}\cosh^{2}z}\leq\frac{2}{1+v^{2}}\leq 2,$ (61)

where we used $\cosh^{2}z\geq 1+z^{2}=(1+v^{2})^{2}/(1-v^{2})^{2}$ . We finish the proof. ∎

A.5 Properties of $q$

The function $q$ belongs to $C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})$ , with $q(t)=0$ for $t\leq 1/5$ and $q(t)=1$ for $t\geq 1$ . For every $t\in\mathbb{R}$ ,

$0\leq q(t)\leq 1,\qquad 0\leq q^{\prime}(t)\leq\frac{5}{2},\quad\text{and}\quad|q^{\prime\prime}(t)|\leq\frac{75}{2}.$

Figure 3 shows $q$ and its derivative.

Figure 3 : The function $q$ and its derivative.

Lemma 13 gives $q\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})$ and $0\leq q\leq 1$ . The plateau identities follow directly from ( 60 ). Set $r:=(5t-1)/4$ . Differentiating gives

$q^{\prime}(t)=\frac{5}{4}p^{\prime\prime}(r),\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad q^{\prime\prime}(t)=\frac{25}{16}p^{\prime\prime\prime}(r).$

The same lemma gives $0\leq p^{\prime\prime}\leq 2$ , so $0\leq q^{\prime}\leq 5/2$ .

It remains to bound $q^{\prime\prime}$ , so we first show that $|p^{\prime\prime\prime}|\leq 24$ . Fix $0<r<1$ and set $v:=2r-1$ and $z:=2v/(1-v^{2})$ . Let $\sigma:=\sqrt{1+z^{2}}=(1+v^{2})/(1-v^{2})$ and $\psi(z):=\sigma(\sigma+1)$ . Equation ( 61 ) gives $p^{\prime\prime}(r)=\psi(z)/(\cosh^{2}z)$ , and direct differentiation gives ${\rm d}z/({\rm d}r)=2\psi(z)$ . Therefore,

$p^{\prime\prime\prime}(r)=\frac{2\psi(z)}{\cosh^{2}z}\bigl[\psi^{\prime}(z)-2\psi(z)\tanh z\bigr].$

Both $\psi^{\prime}(z)$ and $2\psi(z)\tanh z$ have the sign of $z$ , and

$|\psi^{\prime}(z)|=\frac{|z|(2\sigma+1)}{\sigma}\leq 2\sigma+1\leq 2\psi(z),\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad|2\psi(z)\tanh z|\leq 2\psi(z).$

Their difference therefore has absolute value at most $2\psi(z)$ . Moreover,

$\psi(z)^{2}=\sigma^{2}(\sigma+1)^{2}\leq 2(\sigma^{4}+\sigma^{2}+1)\leq 6\cosh^{2}z.$

The first inequality follows from $2(\sigma^{4}+\sigma^{2}+1)-\sigma^{2}(\sigma+1)^{2}=\sigma^{2}(\sigma-1)^{2}+2\geq 0$ ; the second uses $\cosh^{2}z\geq 1+z^{2}+z^{4}/3=(\sigma^{4}+\sigma^{2}+1)/3$ . Hence $|p^{\prime\prime\prime}(r)|\leq 4\psi(z)^{2}/(\cosh^{2}z)\leq 24$ . Since $p^{\prime\prime\prime}=0$ outside $(0,1)$ , we conclude that $|q^{\prime\prime}|\leq(25/16)\cdot 24=75/2$ on $\mathbb{R}$ . We finish the proof. ∎

The functions $e_{\bm{s}},e_{\bm{\nu}}$ belong to $C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})$ , with $e_{\bm{s}}(t)=t$ for $|t|\leq 2$ and $e_{\bm{\nu}}(t)=t$ for $|t|\leq 21$ . They are constant on each tail outside $[-3,3]$ and $[-22,22]$ , respectively. For every $t\in\mathbb{R}$ ,

B.1 Feasible implementation and contraction of FOAM

Fix a proximal center $\bm{z}\in\mathcal{X}$ and a dual regularization parameter $0<r_{\bm{y}}\leq\ell/8$ , and define

$\widehat{F}_{\bm{z}}(\bm{x};\bm{y}):=F_{r}(\bm{x},\bm{z};\bm{y})-\frac{\ell}{2}\left\lVert\bm{x}\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{r_{\bm{y}}}{2}\left\lVert\bm{y}\right\rVert^{2}.$

The function $\widehat{F}_{\bm{z}}$ is convex in $\bm{x}$ , concave in $\bm{y}$ , and independent of $r_{\bm{y}}$ .

Given $(\bm{\omega}_{g},\bm{y}_{g})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}$ , a relative-prox output is a tuple $(\bm{x}_{f},\bm{y}_{f}^{+},\bm{\omega}_{f}^{+},\bm{w}_{f}^{+})\in\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}$ satisfying the conditions below. Define

$\displaystyle\bm{\Delta}_{x}$ $\displaystyle:=\bm{\omega}_{f}^{+}+\frac{\ell}{2}(\bm{x}_{f}-\ell^{-1}\bm{\omega}_{g}),\qquad\mbox{and}$ $\displaystyle\bm{\Delta}_{y}$ $\displaystyle:=\bm{w}_{f}^{+}+r_{\bm{y}}\bm{y}_{f}^{+}+\frac{\ell}{8}(\bm{y}_{f}^{+}-\bm{y}_{g}).$

We require

$\displaystyle\bm{\omega}_{f}^{+}-\nabla_{\bm{x}}F_{r}(\bm{x}_{f},\bm{z};\bm{y}_{f}^{+})+\ell\bm{x}_{f}\in N_{\mathcal{X}}(\bm{x}_{f}),$ (62) $\displaystyle\bm{w}_{f}^{+}+\nabla_{\bm{y}}F_{r}(\bm{x}_{f},\bm{z};\bm{y}_{f}^{+})+r_{\bm{y}}\bm{y}_{f}^{+}\in N_{\mathcal{Y}}(\bm{y}_{f}^{+}),\qquad\mbox{and}$ (63) $\displaystyle\frac{8}{\ell}\left\lVert\bm{\Delta}_{x}\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{8}{\ell}\left\lVert\bm{\Delta}_{y}\right\rVert^{2}\leq\frac{\ell}{8}\left\lVert\bm{x}_{f}+\ell^{-1}\bm{\omega}_{g}\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{\ell}{8}\left\lVert\bm{y}_{f}^{+}-\bm{y}_{g}\right\rVert^{2}.$ (64)

These are the relative-prox conditions in Kovalev and Gasnikov [2022, Condition (18)] , with $\mu_{x}=\ell$ , $\mu_{y}=r_{\bm{y}}$ , $\theta_{y}=8/\ell$ , and indicator regularizers $\iota_{\mathcal{X}}$ and $\iota_{\mathcal{Y}}$ .

For the FOAM update, set $\alpha_{r_{\bm{y}}}:=\sqrt{8r_{\bm{y}}/\ell}$ , $\eta_{\omega}:=\ell/2$ , and $\eta_{y}:=4/(\alpha_{r_{\bm{y}}}\ell)$ . Algorithm 2 maps the current state $S$ to the next state $S^{+}$ .

Algorithm 3 Projected implementation of the relative-prox step

Algorithm 3 terminates after a universal constant number of iterations and oracle calls, evaluates gradients only in $\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}$ , and returns a tuple satisfying ( 62 )-( 64 ).

The saddle operator of $\widehat{F}_{\bm{z}}$ is monotone. The affine terms in $A_{g}$ contribute strong monotonicity moduli

$\frac{\gamma\ell}{2}=4,\qquad\gamma(r_{\bm{y}}+\gamma^{-1})=1+\frac{8r_{\bm{y}}}{\ell},$

so $A_{g}$ is $1$ -strongly monotone. Since $f$ is jointly $\ell$ -smooth, $\nabla\widehat{F}_{\bm{z}}$ is $2\ell$ -Lipschitz. After scaling, the saddle operator has Lipschitz constant at most $16$ , while the added affine terms have Lipschitz constant at most $4$ . Thus $M_{0}=32$ is a valid Lipschitz bound for $A_{g}$ .

Projection nonexpansiveness and these bounds show that $\bm{u}\mapsto\proj_{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}(\bm{u}-\eta A_{g}(\bm{u}))$ is a contraction with factor $\chi:=\sqrt{1-M_{0}^{-2}}<1$ . Its unique fixed point $\bm{u}^{\star}$ satisfies $0\in A_{g}(\bm{u}^{\star})+N_{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}(\bm{u}^{\star})$ . Let $d_{\rm vi}:=\lVert\bm{u}^{-1}-\bm{u}^{\star}\rVert$ . Since $\bm{u}^{0}=\proj_{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}(\bm{u}^{-1})$ , we have

$\left\lVert\bm{u}^{s}-\bm{u}^{\star}\right\rVert\leq\chi^{s}d_{\rm vi},\qquad s\geq 0.$

Consequently, for $s\geq 1$ ,

$\begin{array}[]{@{}l@{\;}l@{}}\displaystyle\left\lVert A_{g}(\bm{u}^{s})+\bm{b}^{s}\right\rVert&\displaystyle{}\leq(M_{0}+M_{0}^{2})\left\lVert\bm{u}^{s}-\bm{u}^{s-1}\right\rVert\\ &\displaystyle{}\leq(M_{0}+M_{0}^{2})(1+\chi)\chi^{s-1}d_{\rm vi},\qquad\mbox{and}\\[2.0pt] \displaystyle\left\lVert\bm{u}^{s}-\bm{u}^{-1}\right\rVert&\displaystyle{}\geq(1-\chi^{s})d_{\rm vi}.\end{array}$

Since $\chi<1$ is a numerical constant, the stopping condition holds after a universal constant number of iterations. Every iterate $\bm{u}^{s}$ , $s\geq 0$ , lies in $\mathcal{C}^{\prime}$ , so all gradient evaluations occur in $\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}$ . Each iteration and the final output require only a constant number of first-order saddle-oracle calls.

Projection optimality gives $\bm{b}^{s}\in N_{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}(\bm{u}^{s})$ . The output therefore satisfies

$\displaystyle\bm{\omega}_{f}^{+}-\nabla_{\bm{x}}F_{r}(\bm{x}_{f},\bm{z};\bm{y}_{f}^{+})+\ell\bm{x}_{f}=\bm{b}_{x}\in N_{\mathcal{X}}(\bm{x}_{f}),\qquad\mbox{and}$ $\displaystyle\bm{w}_{f}^{+}+\nabla_{\bm{y}}F_{r}(\bm{x}_{f},\bm{z};\bm{y}_{f}^{+})+r_{\bm{y}}\bm{y}_{f}^{+}=\bm{b}_{y}\in N_{\mathcal{Y}}(\bm{y}_{f}^{+}).$

Moreover,

$\displaystyle\left\lVert A_{g}(\bm{u}^{s})+\bm{b}^{s}\right\rVert^{2}=\frac{8}{\ell}\left\lVert\bm{\Delta}_{x}\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{8}{\ell}\left\lVert\bm{\Delta}_{y}\right\rVert^{2},\qquad\mbox{and}$ $\displaystyle\left\lVert\bm{u}^{s}-\bm{u}^{-1}\right\rVert^{2}=\frac{\ell}{8}\left\lVert\bm{x}_{f}+\ell^{-1}\bm{\omega}_{g}\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{\ell}{8}\left\lVert\bm{y}_{f}^{+}-\bm{y}_{g}\right\rVert^{2}.$

Thus the stopping condition gives ( 64 ). ∎

Using $\bm{\omega}_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}^{\star}=-\ell\bm{x}_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}^{\star}$ , projection nonexpansiveness, and strong convexity of $P_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}$ , we obtain

$\displaystyle\ell\left\lVert\proj_{\mathcal{X}}\left(-\frac{\bm{\omega}_{f}}{\ell}\right)-\bm{x}_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}^{\star}\right\rVert^{2}$ $\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\ell}\left\lVert\bm{\omega}_{f}-\bm{\omega}_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}^{\star}\right\rVert^{2}\leq 2\left(P_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}(\bm{\omega}_{f},\bm{y}_{f})-P_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}^{\star}\right)\leq\mathcal{L}_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}(S),$

where the last inequality follows from ( 16 ). This proves ( 17 ).

With $\mu_{x}=\ell$ , $\mu_{y}=r_{\bm{y}}$ , and $\theta_{y}=8/\ell$ , Kovalev and Gasnikov [2022, Appendix C, Lemma 7] give

$\mathcal{L}_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}(S^{+})\leq\left(1-\frac{\alpha_{r_{\bm{y}}}}{2}\right)\mathcal{L}_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}(S).$

The proof of this recursion does not require the slow and fast pairs to coincide at initialization. It applies to any state with finite Lyapunov value when the relative-prox output satisfies ( 62 )-( 64 ). Applying it for $K_{\rho}$ steps gives

$\mathcal{L}_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}\left(\textnormal{{FOAM}}_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}(S;\rho)\right)\leq\left(1-\frac{\alpha_{r_{\bm{y}}}}{2}\right)^{K_{\rho}}\mathcal{L}_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}(S)\leq\rho\,\mathcal{L}_{\bm{z},r_{\bm{y}}}(S).$

By Lemma 16 , each step uses a universal constant number of first-order saddle-oracle calls. Hence the total oracle cost is

$\mathcal{O}(K_{\rho})=\mathcal{O}\left(1+\sqrt{\frac{\ell}{r_{\bm{y}}}}\log\frac{1}{\rho}\right).$

For $\rho\in\{1/8,1/400\}$ , as used in Tracked-FOAM , this reduces to $\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{\ell/r_{\bm{y}}})$ . ∎

B.2 Proof of Lemma 8

Let $\bm{z}^{0}=\mathbf{0}$ and $r_{\bm{y}}^{0}=\ell/8$ . Apply Algorithm 3 at $(\bm{z}^{0},r_{\bm{y}}^{0})$ with coupling pair $(\bm{\omega}_{g},\bm{y}_{g})=(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{0})$ . Denote the returned tuple by $(\bm{x}_{f},\bm{y}_{f},\bm{\omega}_{f},\bm{w}_{f})$ and set

$\widehat{S}^{0}:=(\bm{\omega}_{f},\bm{y}_{f},\bm{\omega}_{f},\bm{y}_{f}).$

By Lemma 16 , this construction uses a universal constant number of first-order saddle-oracle calls.

Write $\bm{u}_{f}=(\bm{\omega}_{f},\bm{y}_{f})$ , $\bm{u}^{0}=(\bm{\omega}_{g},\bm{y}_{g})=(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{0})$ , and $\mathbf{H}^{0}:=\operatorname{diag}(\ell^{-1}\mathbf{I}_{m},r_{\bm{y}}^{0}\mathbf{I}_{n})$ . The normal relations in the relative-prox certificate and Kovalev and Gasnikov [2022, Lemma 1] give

$\bm{s}:=(\bm{x}_{f}+\ell^{-1}\bm{\omega}_{f},\bm{w}_{f}+r_{\bm{y}}^{0}\bm{y}_{f})\in\partial P_{\bm{z}^{0},r_{\bm{y}}^{0}}(\bm{u}_{f}).$

Let $\bm{e}:=\bm{s}+\mathbf{H}^{0}(\bm{u}_{f}-\bm{u}^{0})$ . Its two blocks satisfy $\bm{e}_{\omega}=2\bm{\Delta}_{x}/\ell$ and $\bm{e}_{y}=\bm{\Delta}_{y}$ . Using $r_{\bm{y}}^{0}=\ell/8$ in ( 64 ) and completing the square gives

$\displaystyle\left\lVert\bm{u}_{f}-\bm{u}^{0}\right\rVert_{\mathbf{H}^{0}}^{2}-\left\lVert\bm{e}\right\rVert_{(\mathbf{H}^{0})^{-1}}^{2}$ $\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2\ell}\left\lVert\frac{\ell}{2}(\bm{x}_{f}+\ell^{-1}\bm{\omega}_{g})-2\bm{\Delta}_{x}\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{3}{\ell}\left\lVert\bm{\Delta}_{x}\right\rVert^{2}\geq 0.$

Hence

$\left\lVert\bm{e}\right\rVert_{(\mathbf{H}^{0})^{-1}}\leq\left\lVert\bm{u}_{f}-\bm{u}^{0}\right\rVert_{\mathbf{H}^{0}}.$

Let $\bm{u}^{\star}$ minimize $P_{\bm{z}^{0},r_{\bm{y}}^{0}}$ and define

$d_{0}:=\left\lVert\bm{u}^{0}-\bm{u}^{\star}\right\rVert_{\mathbf{H}^{0}},\qquad d_{f}:=\left\lVert\bm{u}_{f}-\bm{u}^{\star}\right\rVert_{\mathbf{H}^{0}},\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad d_{0f}:=\left\lVert\bm{u}_{f}-\bm{u}^{0}\right\rVert_{\mathbf{H}^{0}}.$

Strong monotonicity, together with the residual inequality above, gives

$d_{0}^{2}\geq 3d_{f}^{2}-2d_{f}d_{0f}+d_{0f}^{2}.$

Minimizing the right-hand side separately over $d_{0f}$ and over $d_{f}$ yields

$d_{f}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}d_{0}^{2},\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad d_{0f}^{2}\leq\frac{3}{2}d_{0}^{2}.$

Moreover, $\lVert\bm{s}\rVert_{(\mathbf{H}^{0})^{-1}}\leq\lVert\bm{e}\rVert_{(\mathbf{H}^{0})^{-1}}+d_{0f}\leq 2d_{0f}$ , so strong convexity gives

$P_{\bm{z}^{0},r_{\bm{y}}^{0}}(\bm{u}_{f})-P_{\bm{z}^{0},r_{\bm{y}}^{0}}^{\star}\leq 2d_{f}d_{0f}\leq\sqrt{3}\,d_{0}^{2}.$

Since $\alpha_{r_{\bm{y}}^{0}}=1$ and the slow and fast pairs of $\widehat{S}^{0}$ both equal $\bm{u}_{f}$ ,

$\mathcal{L}_{\bm{z}^{0},r_{\bm{y}}^{0}}(\widehat{S}^{0})\leq(1+2\sqrt{3})d_{0}^{2}\leq 5d_{0}^{2}.$

Finally, set $r^{0}=(2\ell,r_{\bm{y}}^{0})$ . The proximal optimality inequality gives

$\Phi_{r^{0}}(\bm{x}_{\bm{z}^{0},r_{\bm{y}}^{0}}^{\star})+\ell\left\lVert\bm{x}_{\bm{z}^{0},r_{\bm{y}}^{0}}^{\star}-\bm{z}^{0}\right\rVert^{2}\leq\Phi_{r^{0}}(\bm{z}^{0}).$

Using $\bm{\omega}_{\bm{z}^{0},r_{\bm{y}}^{0}}^{\star}=-\ell\bm{x}_{\bm{z}^{0},r_{\bm{y}}^{0}}^{\star}$ , $\bm{u}^{0}=\mathbf{0}$ , $\bm{z}^{0}=\mathbf{0}$ , and ( 14 ), we obtain

$\displaystyle d_{0}^{2}=\ell\left\lVert\bm{z}^{0}-\bm{x}_{\bm{z}^{0},r_{\bm{y}}^{0}}^{\star}\right\rVert^{2}+r_{\bm{y}}^{0}\left\lVert\bm{y}_{\bm{z}^{0},r_{\bm{y}}^{0}}^{\star}\right\rVert^{2}\leq\Phi(\bm{z}^{0})-\inf\Phi+\frac{3}{2}r_{\bm{y}}^{0}D_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2}.$

Combining the last two bounds proves the lemma. ∎
